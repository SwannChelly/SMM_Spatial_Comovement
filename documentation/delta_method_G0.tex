\section{The question}

The count moment is targeted at $K=0$ only: the variety count $\Nhat_s$ is \emph{calibrated}
as the integer solving $\Gbar_s(0;n)=\Ghat_s$. The panels for $K\ge 1$ are therefore free
checks. What uncertainty band belongs on each panel?

The band currently drawn on the $K=0$ panel is
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{se}\big[\Gbar_s(0)\big]
=\sqrt{a_s^{0\prime}\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{sw}}(\ahat)\,a_s^{0}},
\qquad
a_s^{0}=\frac{\partial \Gbar_s(0)}{\partial\alpha},
\label{eq:current}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ $\sqrt{\operatorname{diag}(G\,\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{sw}}\,G')}$ evaluated on the block-6 row
of the profiled $\alpha$-only Jacobian, with $\Nhat_s$ pinned. It is the $\alpha$ channel
\emph{alone}. The delta method used for $T$ carries three channels,
\begin{equation}
V_T=f_\alpha \mathrm{Var}(\ahat) f_\alpha'
   +f_\gamma \Sigma_{\gamma\gamma} f_\gamma'
   +f_\alpha \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\ghat) f_\gamma' + (\cdot)',
\label{eq:VT}
\end{equation}
so the two figures are built on different principles. That inconsistency is real. But at
$K=0$ the fix is \emph{not} to add channels to \eqref{eq:current}: the calibration makes them
mutually exclusive.

\section{$K=0$: the channels cancel, they do not add}

Write the total differential of the fitted count moment and the implicit-function
differential of the calibration, with
\begin{equation}
D_s^{0}=\frac{\partial \Gbar_s(0)}{\partial n}
       =\frac{1}{|L_s|}\sum_{l\in L_s}(1-\qhat_l)^{\Nhat_s}\ln(1-\qhat_l)\;<\;0 ,
\qquad
b_s^{0}=\frac{\partial \Gbar_s(0)}{\partial T}\,f_\gamma .
\end{equation}
Then
\begin{equation}
d\Gbar_s(0)=a_s^{0}\,d\ahat+b_s^{0}\,d\ghat+D_s^{0}\,dN_s,
\qquad
dN_s=\frac{d\Ghat_s-a_s^{0}\,d\ahat-b_s^{0}\,d\ghat}{D_s^{0}} .
\label{eq:ift}
\end{equation}
Substituting the second into the first, everything cancels:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;d\Gbar_s(0)=d\Ghat_s
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\mathrm{Var}\big[\Gbar_s(0)\big]=\mathrm{Var}(\Ghat_s)=\big[\Sigma_{\mathrm{data}}\big]_{G_s G_s}\; }
\end{equation}
This is not a coincidence. $\Nhat_s$ is \emph{defined} as the root of
$\Gbar_s(n)=\Ghat_s$, so the fitted $\Gbar_s(0)$ \emph{is} the empirical target and any
effect of $\ahat$ on it is undone one-for-one by the response of $\Nhat_s$. Adding
$\mathrm{Var}(\ahat)$ on top of the target variance would double count.

\paragraph{Clamped sectors flip the identity.}
When $\Nhat_s$ sits at $N^{\mathrm{LO}}_s$ or $N^{\mathrm{HI}}_s$ the count cannot respond,
$dN_s=0$, and the $\Ghat$ channel drops out instead:
\begin{equation}
d\Gbar_s(0)=a_s^{0}\,d\ahat+b_s^{0}\,d\ghat .
\end{equation}
So the correct $K=0$ band is a per-sector switch keyed on \texttt{clamped}: the bootstrap SE
of the target for a free sector, the $\alpha+\gamma$ propagation for a clamped one. This is
exactly the ``a clamped sector cannot close its own count residual'' distinction the figure
already stars. The current bar \eqref{eq:current} is approximately right for clamped sectors
(it omits the $\gamma$ term) and wrong for free ones.

\section{$K\ge 1$: the channels do add, residualized}

For $K\ge 1$ the cancellation is only partial. With
$D_s^{K}=\partial\Gbar_s(K)/\partial n$ and $r_s^{K}=D_s^{K}/D_s^{0}$, substituting
\eqref{eq:ift} into $d\Gbar_s(K)=a_s^{K}d\ahat+b_s^{K}d\ghat+D_s^{K}dN_s$ gives
\begin{equation}
d\Gbar_s(K)=r_s^{K}\,d\Ghat_s
+\big(a_s^{K}-r_s^{K}a_s^{0}\big)d\ahat
+\big(b_s^{K}-r_s^{K}b_s^{0}\big)d\ghat .
\label{eq:K}
\end{equation}
The empirical-target variance enters scaled by $r_s^{K}$, and each structural loading enters
\emph{residualized against its $K=0$ direction} --- the part of the $\alpha$ (resp.\ $\gamma$)
response that $\Nhat_s$ cannot absorb. Then
$\mathrm{Var}=L\,\mathcal{J}\,L'$ with $L$ read off \eqref{eq:K} and $\mathcal{J}$ the joint
covariance already assembled for the $\Nhat_s$ delta method,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Sigma_{\Ghat\Ghat} & \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\Ghat)' & \Sigma_{\gamma\Ghat}'\\
\mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\Ghat) & \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{sw}}(\ahat) & \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\ghat)\\
\Sigma_{\gamma\Ghat} & \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\ghat)' & \Sigma_{\gamma\gamma}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\Ghat)=P\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{data}}[:,G],
\end{equation}
PSD by construction, as for $V_T$. Setting $K=0$ gives $r_s^{0}=1$ and recovers
$\mathrm{Var}(\Ghat_s)$, so \eqref{eq:K} nests both cases.

\paragraph{The one new ingredient has a closed form.}
Since $\mathrm{Bin}(n+1,q)=\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)+\mathrm{Bern}(q)$,
\begin{equation}
\Gbar_s(K;n+1)-\Gbar_s(K;n)
=-\frac{1}{|L_s|}\sum_{l\in L_s} q_l\,\Pr\!\big[\mathrm{Bin}(n,q_l)=K\big],
\end{equation}
which at $K=0$ is $-q(1-q)^n$ --- verbatim the unit first difference already computed beside
$D_s^{0}$. So $D_s^{K}$ costs one line and needs only $\qhat$ and $\Nhat_s$: no new
simulation. Only $a_s^{K}$ and $b_s^{K}$ require $\Gbar_s(K)$ to exist as a diagnostic block
inside \texttt{compute\_moments}, so the Jacobian has rows for it.

\section{Whether to keep the $\gamma$ channel}

Keep it. In the $\Nhat_s$ report the $\gamma$ channel dominates (on the synthetic set,
$\mathrm{se}_N=10.5$ of which $9.4$ is $\gamma$, against a naive marginal of $4.1$). Since the
uncertainty of $\Gbar_s(K)$ for $K\ge1$ runs \emph{through} $\Nhat_s$, a $\Ghat+\alpha$
variance would understate it by about the same factor, and would leave the figure
inconsistent with the $T$ convention \eqref{eq:VT} it is meant to match.

\section{Caveats}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Integer relaxation.} $\Nhat_s$ is an integer, so $\Gbar_s(0)=\Ghat_s$ holds only
in the continuum relaxation; the cancellation is exact up to a discretization residual bounded
by one $|D_s^{0}|$ step, visible as the \texttt{residual (fit - target)} column.
\item \textbf{Estimator mismatch in $D_s$.} The fitted $\Gbar_s$ uses the unbiased
hypergeometric estimator (\texttt{gbar\_cell}), while $D_s^{0}$ is coded as the plug-in
derivative $\overline{(1-\qhat)^{n}\ln(1-\qhat)}$. The two agree to $O(\qhat)$, the same order
as the existing analytic-vs-first-difference gap already reported.
\item \textbf{Triangularity.} $N_s$ noise does not feed back into $T$: $\hat T=T^*(\ahat,\ghat)$
is the continuum inversion and $\qhat$ is $N_s$-free, so the system is
$\ahat\to\hat T\to\qhat\to\Nhat_s$. \eqref{eq:K} is therefore complete.
\end{enumerate}
