\title{\textbf{A Granular Eaton--Kortum Model of Spatial Sourcing:\\
Endogenizing Regions with No Suppliers\\[4pt]
\large with Comparative Advantage Defined at the Attraction-Area Level}}
\author{}
\date{\today}

\begin{document}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
The baseline spatial-comovement model is a continuum-of-varieties Eaton--Kortum (EK)
economy. By the law of large numbers, every upstream sector--region with a positive
comparative advantage supplies a strictly positive share of every downstream buyer, so the
\emph{extensive margin of sourcing is degenerate}: every sector$\times$region hosts at least
one supplier. In the data, by contrast, many region--sectors host no supplier at all. This
note develops a \emph{granular} EK model that endogenizes those zeros, and --- the new
content of this version --- it locates the comparative-advantage parameter at the
\emph{level at which the empirical regression actually identifies the trade-cost elasticity}.
Two departures from the baseline are made jointly. \emph{(i) Granularity}: the continuum of
varieties in each upstream sector is replaced by a \emph{finite} number $N_{s}$ of varieties,
keeping a continuum of firms within each region and variety; the sourcing extensive margin
then becomes a genuine $\Bin(N_{s},\gamma)$ random object, and an origin hosts no supplier
with probability $(1-\gamma)^{N_{s}}>0$. \emph{(ii) A two-tier geography}: comparative
advantage $T$ is defined at the level of the \emph{attraction area} (\emph{aire
d'attraction})$\times$sector --- exactly the fixed effect of the empirical extensive-margin
regression --- and is \emph{common} to the employment zones (\emph{zones d'emploi}, ZE) it
contains. This alignment does three things at once. First, attraction areas that host no
supplier at all need \emph{no} comparative-advantage parameter: the regression's fixed effect
already discards them (exactly under a cloglog link, and by an explicit attenuation factor
under a linear probability model), so the model is asked to explain only what the data can
speak to. Second, \emph{within} an active attraction area, comparative advantage is constant,
so which ZE hosts a supplier and which does not is disciplined by \emph{distance alone} ---
first-order, confound-free identification of $\alpha$. Third, and as a consequence, the model
under the granular assumption \emph{is} the empirical specification: the probability that a
ZE hosts a supplier is exactly a complementary log-log function of log distance with an
attraction-area$\times$sector fixed effect and slope $-\theta\alpha$. We discipline $N_{s}$
with the empirical distribution of the number of suppliers per region --- the count analogue
of the number-of-buyers-per-seller moment of \citet{lmm2023} (henceforth LMM). Because
sourcing shares are observed at the ZE level, we then relax the area-level restriction to
$T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}\nu_{sl}$ with a \emph{random} multiplicative deviation whose dispersion is
calibrated on those ZE-level shares. This relaxation must be a distribution and never a free
parameter per ZE: a per-ZE parameter restores exact just-identification (destroying $\alpha$),
is undefined precisely at the empty ZE we set out to explain, and biased in a signable
direction there through selection on $\nu$. Done correctly --- with a mean-one Gamma deviation
--- the supplier count becomes exactly negative binomial, the empirical cloglog slope is
attenuated relative to $-\theta\alpha$ by a computable factor, and the within-area share
dispersion joins the count over-dispersion as a second, independent read on the same variance
parameter. We then answer three modeling questions: (i) whether the continuum of firms per
variety can be retained (yes, and it is the natural choice), (ii) at what geographic level
comparative advantage should live (the attraction area, with a random ZE-level deviation), and
(iii) what else in the model must change (the within-sector price index, the treatment of value
moments, the active set / normalization, and the joint identification of
$(\alpha,N_{s},\sigma^{2}_{s},T)$).
\end{abstract}

\section{Motivation: two problems, one alignment}
\label{sec:motivation}

\subsection{The degenerate extensive margin of the continuum model}

In the baseline model (Appendix~B of \emph{Spatial Comovements}; see also
\texttt{documentation/model.md}) each upstream sector $s$ contains a continuum of varieties
$\rho\in[0,1]$. Variety $\rho$ produced in region $l$ has a Fr\'echet productivity with shape
$\theta$ and scale $T_{sl}$, and a downstream buyer in region $r$ sources each variety from
its cheapest origin. Ricardian selection then delivers the familiar EK sourcing share
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{lrs}
\;=\;
\frac{T_{sl}\,\big(w_{l}\,\tau_{lrs}\big)^{-\theta}}
{\displaystyle\sum_{l'} T_{sl'}\,\big(w_{l'}\,\tau_{l'rs}\big)^{-\theta}}
\;=\;
\frac{T_{sl}\,(w_{l}\tau_{lrs})^{-\theta}}{\Phi_{sr}},
\qquad
\Phi_{sr}\equiv\sum_{l'}T_{sl'}(w_{l'}\tau_{l'rs})^{-\theta},
\label{eq:ek-share}
\end{equation}
with iceberg trade costs $\tau_{lrs}=d_{lr}^{\alpha}$ (in the power-law parametrization) and
upstream wage $w_{l}$.

The share \eqref{eq:ek-share} is the probability that a \emph{single} variety is won by
origin $l$. With a \emph{continuum} of varieties, the law of large numbers turns this
probability into a deterministic realized share: origin $l$ supplies exactly a mass
$\gamma_{lrs}>0$ of sector-$s$ varieties to buyer $r$ whenever $T_{sl}>0$. Consequently
\begin{equation}
\Prob\big(\text{$l$ supplies nothing to $r$ in $s$}\big)
=\lim_{N\to\infty}(1-\gamma_{lrs})^{N}=0.
\label{eq:degenerate}
\end{equation}
Every origin with a positive comparative advantage is active in every market. The extensive
margin is degenerate, and the model \emph{cannot} produce a region--sector that hosts no
supplier. The empirical distance regression (moment block~4) is currently matched through an
approximation to a ``win somewhere'' probability, but the object it approximates is
deterministic in the continuum limit: the zeros observed in the data are not a model outcome.

\subsection{The estimand of the empirical regression is a \emph{within-attraction-area}
object}
\label{sec:motivation-fe}

The second problem is econometric rather than theoretical, and it is what dictates
\emph{where} comparative advantage should live. The empirical extensive-margin regression is
run on the whole upstream geography --- supplier and non-supplier ZE alike --- with a fixed
effect at the \textbf{attraction area $\times$ sector} level.\footnote{In the implementation
the fixed-effect group is \texttt{(s-1)*R\_downstream + CLOSEST\_DOWNSTREAM\_REGION[r]}, i.e.
sector $\times$ nearest downstream region, and the distance regressor is
\texttt{LOG\_CLOSEST\_DIST[r]}, the log distance from upstream ZE $r$ to that nearest
downstream region. The set of upstream ZE sharing a nearest downstream region \emph{is} the
attraction area of that downstream region.} An attraction area in which \emph{no} ZE hosts a
supplier therefore has an outcome that is constant (identically zero) within the fixed-effect
group. Such a group carries no information about the distance slope:

\begin{lemma}[All-zero groups and the distance slope]
\label{lem:fe}
Index observations by upstream ZE $l$ within fixed-effect group $g=(a,s)$, with outcome
$y_{l}\in\{0,1\}$ (``$l$ hosts a supplier''), regressor $x_{l}$ (log distance or distance-bin
dummies), weights $\omega_{l}>0$, and group effect $\phi_{g}$. Let
$\Areas^{+}_{s}=\{a:\exists\,l\in\mathcal{L}_{a},\,y_{l}=1\}$ be the set of attraction areas
hosting at least one supplier.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Complementary log-log.} With $\Prob(y_{l}=1)=1-\exp(-\exp(\phi_{g}+\beta x_{l}))$,
the log-likelihood contribution of an all-zero group is
$\ell_{g}(\phi_{g},\beta)=-e^{\phi_{g}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}e^{\beta x_{l}}$, whose
supremum over $\phi_{g}$ is attained as $\phi_{g}\to-\infty$ with
$\ell_{g}\to0$ and $\partial\ell_{g}/\partial\beta\to0$. All-zero groups drop out of the
concentrated likelihood and contribute \emph{exactly nothing} to $\hat\beta$.
\item \emph{Linear probability model.} With $y_{l}=\phi_{g}+\beta x_{l}+u_{l}$ estimated by
weighted OLS, Frisch--Waugh--Lovell gives
$\hat\beta=\big(\sum_{g,l}\omega_{l}\tilde x_{l}\tilde y_{l}\big)/
\big(\sum_{g,l}\omega_{l}\tilde x_{l}^{2}\big)$, where $\tilde\cdot$ denotes weighted
within-group demeaning. An all-zero group has $\tilde y_{l}\equiv0$, so it contributes
\emph{zero} to the numerator but a strictly positive amount to the denominator. Hence
\begin{equation}
\hat\beta=\lambda\,\hat\beta^{+},
\qquad
\lambda=\frac{\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}_{s}}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\omega_{l}\tilde x_{l}^{2}}
{\sum_{a}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\omega_{l}\tilde x_{l}^{2}}\in(0,1],
\label{eq:attenuation}
\end{equation}
where $\hat\beta^{+}$ is the estimate on the subsample $\Areas^{+}_{s}$: the empty areas
\emph{attenuate} the coefficient toward zero by the known factor $\lambda$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{lemma}

Lemma~\ref{lem:fe} is the empirical fact behind the whole design of this note. Under a
cloglog link --- the link the model actually implies, see
Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} --- the trade-cost elasticity $\alpha$ is identified
\emph{exclusively} from variation \emph{within} attraction areas that host at least one
supplier. Under a linear probability model the empty areas do enter, but only as a mechanical
dilution \eqref{eq:attenuation} of the same within-area signal; they carry no independent
information about $\alpha$.

\subsection{The alignment}

The two problems have a common resolution. If comparative advantage is defined at the
\textbf{attraction area $\times$ sector} level --- the same level as the fixed effect --- then:

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item Attraction areas with no supplier require \emph{no} comparative-advantage parameter.
They are outside the estimation exactly as they are outside the concentrated likelihood
(Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}(i)). The model is not asked to explain why an entire attraction area is
empty --- and the data, having absorbed it in a fixed effect, cannot tell us.
\item \emph{Within} an active attraction area, comparative advantage is \emph{constant}, so
the only thing distinguishing a ZE that hosts a supplier from one that does not is
\textbf{distance}. The within-area extensive margin is a pure trade-cost object: this is
where $\alpha$ comes from, and there is no unobserved comparative-advantage heterogeneity
left to confound it.
\item Granularity (finite $N_{s}$) is still indispensable: it is what makes a ZE-level zero
possible at all inside an area with $T_{s,a}>0$. Without it, (b) has no bite because every ZE
in an active area would host a supplier with probability one.
\end{enumerate}

Sections~\ref{sec:granular} and \ref{sec:twotier} develop (c) and (a)--(b) respectively;
Section~\ref{sec:twotier} closes with the resulting division of labor in identification.

\section{A granular departure: finite varieties, a continuum of firms}
\label{sec:granular}

\subsection{Environment}

We keep the entire downstream block of the baseline model unchanged: downstream regions
$r=1,\dots,\Nd$, each hosting a single representative producer (``one downstream firm per
downstream region''); nested-CES technology with labor/intermediate elasticity $\lambda$,
across-sector elasticity $\nu$, and within-sector elasticity $\nu_{s}$; demand elasticity
$\varepsilon$ and shifters $\delta_{r}$. The only change is on the upstream supply side.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Granular assumption.} Each upstream sector $s$ consists of a \emph{finite} number
$N_{s}$ of horizontally differentiated varieties, indexed $\rho=1,\dots,N_{s}$.
Within each variety $\rho$ and each upstream ZE $l$ there is a \emph{continuum} of firms. The
productivity of firms in $(l,s,\rho)$ is such that the number of firms with productivity above
$z$ is Poisson with mean $T_{sl}\,z^{-\theta}$; equivalently, the most productive firm of
ZE $l$ for variety $\rho$ has a Fr\'echet productivity
$\varphi_{l\rho s}\sim\Frechet(T_{sl},\theta)$, i.e. $\Prob(\varphi_{l\rho s}\le z)=\exp(-T_{sl}z^{-\theta})$.
Draws are independent across varieties $\rho$, ZE $l$, and sectors $s$.
\end{quote}

This is the classical ``fixed number of independent Ricardian draws'' form of granular EK
\citep[cf.][]{eks2012,bejk2003}: the continuum is replaced by $N_{s}$ discrete competitions,
each of which is a standard EK problem. $N_{s}$ is a new structural parameter, one per
upstream sector, to be estimated.

\begin{remark}[Poissonizing the variety count]
\label{rem:poisson}
It is analytically convenient, and economically innocuous, to let the number of varieties
itself be random, $N^{\rho}_{s}\sim\Pois(N_{s})$, rather than exactly $N_{s}$. Nothing in the
value block changes (shares are unaffected by the variety count, see
Section~\ref{sec:q2}b), the expected count of varieties won is identical, and the zero
probability becomes \emph{exactly} exponential,
$\Prob(K=0)=\exp(-N_{s}\gamma)$ instead of $(1-\gamma)^{N_{s}}$. As
Section~\ref{sec:exactcloglog} shows, this is precisely what makes the empirical cloglog
specification the \emph{exact} reduced form of the model rather than an approximation to it.
Both versions coincide to $O(\gamma^{2})$ and are numerically indistinguishable in the
relevant range ($\gamma$ small, $N_{s}\gamma$ moderate). We keep $N_{s}$ fixed in the main
text and flag where Poissonization sharpens a statement.
\end{remark}

\subsection{Per-variety Ricardian selection (the role of the continuum of firms)}
\label{sec:selection}

Fix a variety $\rho$ of sector $s$ and a downstream buyer $r$. Because there is a continuum of
firms in each ZE, ZE $l$'s \emph{lowest delivered cost} for variety $\rho$ is
attained by its most productive firm and equals
\begin{equation}
c_{l\rho s}(r)=\frac{w_{l}\,\tau_{lrs}}{\varphi_{l\rho s}},
\qquad
\varphi_{l\rho s}\sim\Frechet(T_{sl},\theta).
\label{eq:region-cost}
\end{equation}
Since the minimum of independent Fr\'echet costs is again Fr\'echet, the probability that
origin $l$ is the cheapest source of variety $\rho$ for buyer $r$ is \emph{exactly} the EK
share \eqref{eq:ek-share}:
\begin{equation}
\Prob\!\Big(l=\argmin_{l'} c_{l'\rho s}(r)\Big)=\gamma_{lrs}.
\label{eq:per-variety-win}
\end{equation}
The continuum of firms is precisely what delivers \eqref{eq:region-cost}--\eqref{eq:per-variety-win}
in closed form: it collapses each ZE's within-ZE competition into a single
Fr\'echet ``champion'' cost, so that the only randomness left across the $N_{s}$ varieties is
\emph{which ZE wins}. This is the key observation behind the answer to Question~1
(Section~\ref{sec:q1}).

\subsection{The granular extensive margin}
\label{sec:extmargin}

Across the $N_{s}$ varieties, the wins in \eqref{eq:per-variety-win} are i.i.d.
$\text{Bernoulli}(\gamma_{lrs})$. Let $K_{lrs}$ denote the number of sector-$s$ varieties that
buyer $r$ sources from origin $l$. Then
\begin{equation}
K_{lrs}\;\sim\;\Bin(N_{s},\gamma_{lrs}),
\qquad
\Prob(K_{lrs}=0)=(1-\gamma_{lrs})^{N_{s}},
\label{eq:binomial}
\end{equation}
or, under Remark~\ref{rem:poisson}, $K_{lrs}\sim\Pois(N_{s}\gamma_{lrs})$ with
$\Prob(K_{lrs}=0)=\exp(-N_{s}\gamma_{lrs})$.
Equation \eqref{eq:binomial} is the heart of the model. Unlike \eqref{eq:degenerate}, the
zero-supplier probability is now strictly positive and \emph{endogenous}: it falls with the
comparative advantage $\gamma_{lrs}$ (hence rises with distance through
$\tau_{lrs}=d_{lr}^{\alpha}$) and falls with the variety count $N_{s}$. The expected number of
\emph{active} origins in market $(r,s)$ is
\begin{equation}
\E\big[\#\{l:K_{lrs}\ge 1\}\big]=\sum_{l}\Big[1-(1-\gamma_{lrs})^{N_{s}}\Big].
\label{eq:active-origins}
\end{equation}
As $N_{s}\to\infty$, \eqref{eq:active-origins} converges to the number of origins with
$T_{sl}>0$ and we recover the degenerate continuum limit. For finite $N_{s}$, remote/small
origins ($\gamma_{lrs}$ close to $0$) are active with probability $\approx N_{s}\gamma_{lrs}$
and are frequently inactive --- exactly the region-sectors with no suppliers that we want to
reproduce.

\begin{remark}[Dependence across downstream buyers]
\label{rem:dependence}
For a \emph{given} variety $\rho$, the champion costs $\{\varphi_{l\rho s}\}_{l}$ are common
across all downstream buyers; only the trade cost $\tau_{lrs}$ differs across $r$. Hence the
win events $\{l\text{ wins }\rho\text{ for }r\}$ and $\{l\text{ wins }\rho\text{ for }r'\}$ are
\emph{positively dependent}: an origin that drew a good champion for variety $\rho$ tends to
win it in many markets at once. The per-market marginal \eqref{eq:binomial} is nonetheless
exact. This dependence matters for the industry-wide count (Section~\ref{sec:moment}) and is
handled cleanly by simulation.
\end{remark}

\section{Comparative advantage at the attraction-area level}
\label{sec:twotier}

\subsection{The two-tier geography}

\begin{assumption}[Two-tier geography]
\label{as:geo}
The set of upstream employment zones $\mathcal{L}$ is partitioned into attraction areas,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}=\bigsqcup_{a\in\Areas}\mathcal{L}_{a},
\qquad
a(l)\in\Areas \text{ the attraction area of ZE } l,
\qquad
R_{a}\equiv|\mathcal{L}_{a}|,
\end{equation}
where $\mathcal{L}_{a}$ is the set of upstream ZE whose nearest downstream region is the
anchor of area $a$. Comparative advantage is \textbf{area-specific}:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}\quad\text{for every } l\in\mathcal{L}_{a},\;}
\label{eq:T-area}
\end{equation}
with one parameter $T_{s,a}$ per (sector, attraction area) pair. Productivity draws remain
\emph{independent across ZE}: two ZE in the same area share the \emph{scale} $T_{s,a}$ of
their Fr\'echet distribution, not the realization of their champions.
\end{assumption}

The last sentence is essential and easy to misread. Restriction \eqref{eq:T-area} does
\emph{not} make ZE within an area identical or interchangeable: each ZE runs its own
independent Ricardian competition for each variety, and two ZE in the same area with
different distances to the downstream anchor have different win probabilities. What
\eqref{eq:T-area} removes is only \emph{unobserved technological heterogeneity} across ZE
within an area.

Under \eqref{eq:T-area} the sourcing share \eqref{eq:ek-share} becomes
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{lrs}=\frac{T_{s,a(l)}\,(w_{l}\tau_{lrs})^{-\theta}}{\Phi_{sr}},
\qquad
\Phi_{sr}=\sum_{a'\in\Areas}T_{s,a'}\sum_{l'\in\mathcal{L}_{a'}}(w_{l'}\tau_{l'rs})^{-\theta},
\label{eq:share-area}
\end{equation}
so the geography enters twice: through the area's technology $T_{s,a}$ and through each ZE's
own trade cost $\tau_{lrs}=d_{lr}^{\alpha}$.

\subsection{Which areas need a parameter --- and which do not}
\label{sec:activeset-area}

Let $\Areas^{+}_{s}$ be the set of attraction areas hosting at least one observed supplier in
sector $s$. Assumption~\ref{as:geo} together with Lemma~\ref{lem:fe} yields the central
practical simplification of this note:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{Only $\{T_{s,a}\}_{a\in\Areas^{+}_{s}}$ needs to be estimated.} For
$a\notin\Areas^{+}_{s}$, set $T_{s,a}=0$ (equivalently, leave $(s,a)$ out of the active set):
the entire area is a \emph{structural} zero. This is not an approximation forced on us by
computation --- it is the only choice consistent with the information the data contain, since
the attraction-area $\times$ sector fixed effect has already absorbed every all-zero area
(Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}(i)).
\end{quote}

Three comments.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=3pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{What we give up, explicitly.} The model does not explain why an entire attraction
area hosts no supplier in sector $s$. That is a statement about the sector's absence from a
whole labor-market catchment area --- plausibly driven by non-tradable local fundamentals,
historical specialization, or the sector simply not existing there --- and it is a different
economic question from the one the paper asks. Under \eqref{eq:T-area} the boundary is drawn
in exactly the same place the econometrics draws it.
\item \emph{What we gain in dimension.} The comparative-advantage block shrinks from one
parameter per active $(s,\ZE)$ pair to one per active $(s,\text{area})$ pair, i.e. by a factor
of roughly $\overline{R_{a}}$ (the average number of ZE per attraction area). This is a large
reduction in the dimension of the outer search and, as Section~\ref{sec:profiling} explains,
it is also what restores $\alpha$'s identification when $T$ is profiled out.
\item \emph{Reference normalization.} The per-sector gauge is now one reference \emph{area},
$T_{s,\mathrm{ref}(s)}=1$, instead of one reference ZE. Sourcing shares are ratios of $T$
within a sector, hence invariant to the common scale, so the gauge is free exactly as before.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Within an area, only distance matters}
\label{sec:within}

Take two ZE $l,l'$ in the \emph{same} attraction area $a$ and the same downstream buyer $r$.
By \eqref{eq:share-area} the technology term and the price index $\Phi_{sr}$ cancel:
\begin{equation}
\ln\frac{\gamma_{lrs}}{\gamma_{l'rs}}
=-\theta\,\alpha\,\ln\frac{d_{lr}}{d_{l'r}}
\;-\;\theta\,\ln\frac{w_{l}}{w_{l'}},
\label{eq:within-contrast}
\end{equation}
and under the model's wage normalization ($w\equiv1$, as in the estimated version) the second
term vanishes:
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\ln\frac{\gamma_{lrs}}{\gamma_{l'rs}}
=-\theta\,\alpha\,\big(\ln d_{lr}-\ln d_{l'r}\big),
\qquad l,l'\in\mathcal{L}_{a}.
\;}
\label{eq:within-contrast-norm}
\end{equation}
Combined with the granular zero probability \eqref{eq:binomial}, \eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}
says that the \emph{within-area pattern of which ZE hosts a supplier} is a function of
$(\theta,\alpha)$ and observed distances \emph{only}. This is the identification statement the
whole construction is built for: within an active attraction area the extensive margin is a
pure trade-cost object, with no unobserved comparative advantage left to soak up the distance
gradient.

\subsection{The empirical cloglog \emph{is} the model's reduced form}
\label{sec:exactcloglog}

The alignment is tighter than an identification argument: the specification estimated in the
data is, up to a stated approximation, the model itself.

\begin{proposition}[Exact reduced form, \emph{cell} level]
\label{prop:cloglog-exact}
Consider a single downstream buyer per area (the area's anchor $r=a$, so that
$d_{l}\equiv d_{l,a(l)}$ is the distance regressor used in the data), granular varieties with
$N_{s}$ Poissonized (Remark~\ref{rem:poisson}), and comparative advantage area-specific
\eqref{eq:T-area}. Then the probability that upstream ZE $l$ hosts at least one sector-$s$
supplier is exactly
\begin{equation}
\Prob\big(K_{l a s}\ge1\big)
=1-\exp\!\big(-\exp(\eta_{ls})\big),
\qquad
\eta_{ls}
=\underbrace{\ln N_{s}+\ln T_{s,a(l)}-\ln\Phi_{s,a(l)}}_{\textstyle \phi_{a(l),s}}
\;-\;\theta\alpha\,\ln d_{l}
\;-\;\theta\,\ln w_{l},
\label{eq:cloglog}
\end{equation}
i.e. a \textbf{complementary log-log model of the supplier indicator on log distance with an
attraction area $\times$ sector fixed effect}, with slope
\begin{equation}
\beta_{\mathrm{dist}}=-\theta\,\alpha
\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
\alpha=-\beta_{\mathrm{dist}}/\theta .
\label{eq:alpha-map}
\end{equation}
With the distance-bin parametrization the same statement holds bin by bin, with
$\beta_{b}=-\theta\ln\tau_{b}$.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Under Poissonization, $K_{las}\sim\Pois(N_{s}\gamma_{las})$, so
$\Prob(K_{las}=0)=\exp(-N_{s}\gamma_{las})$ and
$\Prob(K_{las}\ge1)=1-\exp(-\exp(\ln N_{s}+\ln\gamma_{las}))$. Substituting
$\ln\gamma_{las}=\ln T_{s,a(l)}-\theta\ln w_{l}-\theta\alpha\ln d_{l}-\ln\Phi_{s,a(l)}$ from
\eqref{eq:share-area} gives \eqref{eq:cloglog}. Every term that is constant within
$(a,s)$ --- $\ln N_{s}$, $\ln T_{s,a}$, and $\ln\Phi_{s,a}$, the last because the anchor
buyer is common to the area --- is absorbed by $\phi_{a,s}$.
\end{proof}

Four consequences.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=3pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{Why cloglog and not a linear probability model.} \eqref{eq:cloglog} is a cloglog,
exactly. The LPM is a linear approximation to it, and Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}(ii) shows the LPM's
estimand is additionally scaled by the attenuation factor $\lambda$ of
\eqref{eq:attenuation}. The switch to a cloglog link is therefore not a robustness choice but
a specification requirement.
\item \emph{$N_{s}$ is \emph{not} identified by the regression.} In \eqref{eq:cloglog},
$\ln N_{s}$ enters only through the fixed effect $\phi_{a,s}$, perfectly collinear with
$\ln T_{s,a}$. The distance coefficient identifies $\alpha$ alone. $N_{s}$ must come from a
\emph{level} moment --- the count moment of Section~\ref{sec:moment} --- given the normalized
$T$; see Table~\ref{tab:division}.
\item \emph{Sign and outcome conventions.} \eqref{eq:cloglog} is exact for the indicator
$y_{l}=\one\{\text{$l$ hosts a supplier}\}$, with a \emph{negative} distance coefficient
$-\theta\alpha$. Running the cloglog on the reversed outcome
$y_{l}=\one\{\text{no supplier}\}$ instead gives
$\eta=-\ln\!\big(1-e^{-N_{s}\gamma_{las}}\big)\approx-\phi_{a,s}+\theta\alpha\ln d_{l}$, i.e.
a \emph{positive} slope $+\theta\alpha$ --- but only to first order in $N_{s}\gamma$ (the
sparse regime), since the cloglog link is not symmetric under outcome reversal. The exact
version is the supplier indicator; if the reversed convention is retained for continuity, note
that the model moment must be computed by running the \emph{identical} reversed regression on
simulated data (which the SMM does), so consistency is preserved and only the direct
interpretation \eqref{eq:alpha-map} of the coefficient is approximate.
\item \emph{Multiple downstream buyers.} With $\Nd>1$ the relevant object is the
industry-wide zero, driven by the probability $q_{ls}$ that ZE $l$ wins a variety
\emph{somewhere} (Section~\ref{sec:moment}). To first order in small shares,
$q_{ls}\approx T_{s,a(l)}w_{l}^{-\theta}\sum_{r}d_{lr}^{-\theta\alpha}\hat E_{sr}/\Phi_{sr}$,
so \eqref{eq:cloglog} holds with $\ln d_{l}$ replaced by a \emph{market-access} index
$-\tfrac{1}{\theta}\ln\sum_{r}d_{lr}^{-\theta\alpha}\hat E_{sr}/\Phi_{sr}$. The empirical
practice --- distance to the nearest downstream region plus an area fixed effect --- is the
leading term of that index, exact when one buyer dominates. \textbf{The estimator does not
rely on the exactness of \eqref{eq:cloglog}}: block~4 is matched by running the same
regression on model-simulated data, so any wedge between the index and $\ln d_{l}$ is
reproduced on both sides. Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} is what licenses the
\emph{interpretation} \eqref{eq:alpha-map}, and what tells us the specification is
well chosen.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why profiling $T$ stays safe under the area-level restriction}
\label{sec:profiling}

The estimator can concentrate $T$ out of the outer search by inverting the sourcing-share
moments (the Sinkhorn/GE inversion $T^{*}(\alpha,\Omega,A)$). With ZE-level comparative advantage
this is structurally fragile: the number of free $T_{sl}$ equals the number of $\gamma$ targets,
so the inversion can hit \emph{every} share exactly for \emph{any} $\alpha$, and it does so by
raising $T$ on remote origins precisely as $\alpha$ rises. The distance channel is then at risk
of being absorbed by $T^{*}(\alpha)$, flattening the regression coefficient in $\alpha$ and
leaving the trade-cost elasticity with nothing to fit.

\begin{remark}[Status of this concern]
The flattening was diagnosed on the \emph{linear} extensive-margin specification. With the
cloglog link --- the model's own reduced form, Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} --- it no
longer materialises: the profiled search returns an interior $\alpha$. The argument below is
therefore not a description of current behaviour but a statement about \emph{why the
area-level restriction is structurally safe}: it removes the degrees of freedom that made the
cancellation possible in the first place, so the property does not depend on the link
happening to be well chosen.
\end{remark}

Assumption~\ref{as:geo} removes the cancellation by counting equations:

\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Free parameters:} $|\Areas^{+}_{s}|$ per sector (one per active area).
\item \textbf{Share moments available:} one per active $(s,\ZE)$ pair, i.e.
$\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}_{s}}\#\{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}: \text{active}\}\;\gg\;|\Areas^{+}_{s}|$.
\end{itemize}

The natural profiling step is therefore to invert the \emph{area-aggregated} shares,
\begin{equation}
\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\gamma_{ls}\big(T,\alpha\big)
=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\hat\gamma_{ls},
\qquad a\in\Areas^{+}_{s},
\label{eq:area-sinkhorn}
\end{equation}
which is a \emph{square} system --- $|\Areas^{+}_{s}|$ equations in $|\Areas^{+}_{s}|$ unknowns,
with the same multiplicative/Sinkhorn structure as before, hence the same contraction
properties. The \emph{within}-area split of each area aggregate across its ZE is then left as
a \emph{model prediction}, a function of distance and $\alpha$ alone by
\eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}. Profiling $T$ out no longer profiles $\alpha$ out: the
within-area shares and the within-area extensive margin both remain live functions of
$\alpha$ that the loss must fit --- by construction, not by luck. Appendix~\ref{app:df} counts the resulting degrees of
freedom.

\subsection{The main risk, and the release valve}
\label{sec:risk}

Restriction \eqref{eq:T-area} is strong, and it is worth being blunt about how it can fail.
With $w\equiv1$ and $T$ constant within an area, \emph{all} within-area variation in sourcing
shares and in the extensive margin is attributed to distance. But ZE within an attraction area
also differ in \textbf{size}: a large ZE plausibly hosts suppliers more often than a small one
at the same distance, for reasons that have nothing to do with trade costs. If size is omitted,
the model must load it on the distance coefficient, biasing $\alpha$ by the usual
omitted-variable amount (with the sign of the size--distance correlation within areas).

Three responses, in order of preference. The third --- letting comparative advantage deviate
from its area level by a \emph{random} ZE-level term calibrated on the ZE-level shares --- is
developed in its own section (Section~\ref{sec:eps}), because it is the general case that
nests the other two.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Let observed ZE covariates scale $T$, with a common loading.} Replace
\eqref{eq:T-area} by
\begin{equation}
T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}\cdot\prod_{k}\big(x_{kl}\big)^{\eta_{k}},
\label{eq:T-area-cov}
\end{equation}
with $x_{kl}$ observed (ZE employment or establishment count, an urbanization indicator,
\dots) and $\eta_{k}$ a \emph{small number of scalars shared across areas and sectors}
(e.g. $\eta_{\text{size}}=1$ under the microfoundation ``$T$ is proportional to the mass of
potential entrepreneurs''). The parameter count stays at the area level --- the dimension gain
of Section~\ref{sec:activeset-area} is preserved --- while within-area heterogeneity is no
longer forced onto distance. Crucially, \eqref{eq:T-area-cov} must include the \emph{same}
controls as the empirical regression: whatever the cloglog conditions on within the fixed
effect, the model must condition on too, or the two $\alpha$'s are not the same object.
\item \textbf{Test the restriction before imposing it.} \eqref{eq:T-area} is directly
checkable in the data, without any estimation: regress $\ln\hat\gamma_{ls}$ (observed shares,
active ZE only) on an area$\times$sector fixed effect, log distance, and candidate size
controls, and inspect the residual \emph{within-area} dispersion. If the within-area residual
variance is small relative to the across-area variance, \eqref{eq:T-area} is a good
approximation; if it is large, the surviving dispersion tells you which covariate is missing
in \eqref{eq:T-area-cov}. Running this diagnostic first is cheap and settles the question the
restriction raises.
\end{enumerate}

Note also that \eqref{eq:T-area} converts what were free parameters into $R_{a}-1$
\emph{over-identifying restrictions} per active area (Appendix~\ref{app:df}). That is a
feature --- the model becomes testable where it was previously just-identified --- but it means
a Hansen $J$ on the $\gamma$ block now has power against the geography restriction itself, and
should be read as such.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item the \emph{intensive} margin is almost entirely \textbf{between} areas --- which is what

\item the \emph{extensive} margin is almost entirely \textbf{within} areas --- which is what

\end{itemize}
One caveat on the $0.944$: with $99$ observations and one dummy per active area$\times$sector,
$R^{2}$ is mechanically inflated by a near-saturated design. Report the number of groups and the
adjusted $R^{2}$ before leaning on that figure.

\paragraph{Section~\ref{sec:risk}'s concern is confirmed: size is the dominant within-area force,
and it does confound distance.} In logs, $\ln\text{SIREN}$ carries a coefficient of $0.492$
$(0.126)$ and lifts the within $R^{2}$ from $0.084$ to $0.330$ --- roughly four times the
explanatory power of distance. Adding it moves the distance coefficient toward zero in both
specifications, $-0.284\to-0.130$ in logs and $-0.017\to-0.011$ in levels. The sign of that
movement is informative: within an attraction area, closer ZE are \emph{bigger}, so omitting size
made distance look more powerful than it is. \textbf{The covariate extension
\eqref{eq:T-area-cov} is therefore not optional, and the firm count must be in it.} Any $\alpha$
estimated from a within-area specification without it inherits the same upward bias in magnitude.

\paragraph{But neither log coefficient can be read as $-\theta\alpha$: granular censoring.}
The insignificance of columns (5)--(6) is not evidence that distance does not matter within areas.
Conditioning on activity censors the share, and severely so in the granular regime.

\begin{lemma}[Granular censoring of the observed positive share]
\label{lem:censoring}
With $K_{ls}\sim\Pois(u_{ls})$, $u_{ls}=N_{s}\gamma_{ls}$, and equal-value varieties,
$\E[\hat\gamma_{ls}\mid K_{ls}\ge1]=\gamma_{ls}/(1-e^{-u_{ls}})$, so
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \ln \E\big[\hat\gamma_{ls}\mid K_{ls}\ge1\big]}{\partial \ln\gamma_{ls}}
=B(u_{ls}),
\qquad
B(u)=1-\frac{u}{e^{u}-1}\in(0,1),
\label{eq:censoring}
\end{equation}
with $B$ increasing, $B(0^{+})=0$ and $B(\infty)=1$. The regression actually run --- on
$\ln\hat\gamma$ rather than $\ln\E[\hat\gamma]$ --- carries the closely related factor
$B_{\log}(u)=\partial\,\E[\ln K\mid K\ge1]/\partial\ln u$, which is slightly smaller than $B$ for
$u\lesssim3$ and equals it in the limits.
\end{lemma}

The mechanism is quantization: a ZE that supplies at all supplies at least one variety, and when
$u\ll1$ it supplies \emph{exactly} one, so its observed share is $\approx1/N_{s}$ almost
irrespective of its true $\gamma$. All of the distance signal is pushed out of the intensive
margin and into whether $K\ge1$. Exact values of the two factors:

\paragraph{Why columns (4)--(6) are doubly uninformative, and the next diagnostic to run.}
Beyond censoring, there is a mechanical problem: the within-area log regression is identified only
off area$\times$sectors with \emph{at least two} active ZE. A group with exactly one active cell
has its single residual demeaned to zero and contributes nothing --- Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}(ii)'s
logic, now applied to the intensive margin. With $99$ active cells spread over the active
area$\times$sectors, the effective sample may be far below $99$. The cheap next step is to
tabulate the number of active ZE per active area$\times$sector and report how many groups have
$\ge2$; that number, not $99$, is what columns (5)--(6) are estimated on, and it explains the
standard errors of $0.197$ and $0.136$ without any appeal to a small effect.

\paragraph{Verdict.} Four conclusions carry forward.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Keep the two-tier geography.} $91.5\%$ zeros inside active areas, with the intensive
margin $94\%$ between-area and the extensive margin $94\%$ within-area: the data split along
exactly the seam Assumption~\ref{as:geo} cuts.
\item \textbf{Include the firm count in \eqref{eq:T-area-cov}, and estimate its loading.} Size
dominates within-area variation and biases $\alpha$ upward in magnitude when omitted. Do not fix
$\eta_{\text{size}}=1$ by assumption --- \eqref{eq:alpha-over-eta} shows the whole reading of
$\alpha$ hinges on it.
\item \textbf{Identify $\alpha$ from the extensive margin, not from the within-area intensive
margin.} The informative variation is the $1062$ zeros, not the $99$ positive shares --- which
are censored (Lemma~\ref{lem:censoring}) and thin within groups. Table~\ref{tab:division}'s
$\alpha$ row should be read accordingly: the share channel is the \emph{level} specification over
all cells, and its role is to pin $\alpha/\eta_{\text{size}}$, while the cloglog on the supplier
indicator carries the rest.
\item \textbf{Do not read $\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon}$ off column (6)'s residual.} A within $R^{2}$
of $0.330$ leaves two thirds of the within-area log-share variation unexplained, but that residual
mixes $\varepsilon$, granular sampling noise, censoring, and truncation on the selected high-$\gamma$
tail. Table~\ref{tab:eps-mc} already ruled out the plug-in route;
Lemma~\ref{lem:censoring} adds a second reason. Estimate $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ inside the SMM.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Employment-zone deviations: a distribution, not a parameter vector}
\label{sec:eps}

Assumption~\ref{as:geo} imposes $T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}$ exactly. Since sourcing shares are observed
\emph{at the ZE level}, it is natural to relax it --- write comparative advantage as an area
level plus a ZE-specific deviation, and calibrate the deviation on those ZE-level shares. This
section does exactly that. Two things must be settled first: the functional form
(Section~\ref{sec:eps-form}), and --- decisively --- whether the deviation is a
\emph{parameter vector} or a \emph{distribution} (Section~\ref{sec:eps-trap}). The answer to
the second question determines whether the identification of $\alpha$ survives the relaxation.

\subsection{Multiplicative, not additive}
\label{sec:eps-form}

$T_{sl}$ is a Fr\'echet \emph{scale}: it must be strictly positive, and it enters every model
object multiplicatively --- shares are ratios of $T$'s \eqref{eq:share-area}, and
$(w_{l}\tau_{lrs})^{-\theta}$ multiplies it. An additive deviation $T_{s,a}+\epsilon_{sl}$
respects neither property: it can go negative, and its magnitude has no scale-free reading
(the same $\epsilon$ means something different in a large and a small sector). The right form
is multiplicative,
\begin{equation}
T_{sl}
=T_{s,a(l)}\cdot\nu_{sl}\cdot\prod_{k}x_{kl}^{\eta_{k}},
\qquad \nu_{sl}>0,\qquad \E[\nu_{sl}]=1,
\label{eq:eps-spec}
\end{equation}
or in logs, with $\varepsilon_{sl}\equiv\ln\nu_{sl}$,
\begin{equation}
\ln T_{sl}=\ln T_{s,a(l)}+\textstyle\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\ln x_{kl}+\varepsilon_{sl}.
\label{eq:eps-log}
\end{equation}
Three payoffs. Positivity is automatic. The normalization $\E[\nu]=1$ makes $T_{s,a}$
\emph{exactly} the area level, so the area-aggregate inversion \eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn} is
unchanged in expectation. And --- the point that makes the proposal work ---
$\varepsilon_{sl}$ is \emph{literally the residual of the within-area log-share regression},
because \eqref{eq:within-contrast} is additive in $\ln T$:
\begin{equation}
\ln\gamma_{lrs}
=\underbrace{\ln T_{s,a(l)}-\ln\Phi_{sr}}_{\text{area}\times\text{sector}}
\;-\;\theta\alpha\ln d_{lr}
\;-\;\theta\ln w_{l}
\;+\;\textstyle\sum_{k}\eta_{k}\ln x_{kl}
\;+\;\varepsilon_{sl}.
\label{eq:eps-logshare}
\end{equation}
The observable block $\prod_{k}x_{kl}^{\eta_{k}}$ should absorb as much as it can --- size above
all. Every piece of within-area heterogeneity moved from $\varepsilon$ into observables is
heterogeneity that does \emph{not} have to be integrated out, and doing so is precisely the fix
to the size concern of Section~\ref{sec:risk}. What remains in $\varepsilon$ is genuinely
unexplained ZE-level comparative advantage.

\subsection{The trap: a free $\varepsilon$ per ZE undoes the whole design}
\label{sec:eps-trap}

``Calibrate $\varepsilon$ on the ZE-level shares'' admits two readings, and they are not
variations on a theme --- one of them destroys the estimator.

\begin{description}[topsep=3pt,itemsep=2pt,leftmargin=1.6em]
\item[(A) Fixed effects.] One free parameter $\varepsilon_{sl}$ per ZE, chosen so that the
model reproduces each observed $\hat\gamma_{ls}$ exactly.
\item[(B) Random effect.] A \emph{distribution} for $\varepsilon$ with one or two parameters
per sector, chosen so that the model reproduces the \emph{dispersion} of the ZE-level shares
(and of the supplier counts).
\end{description}

Reading (A) must be rejected, for three compounding reasons.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=3pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{It restores exact just-identification and kills $\alpha$.} The number of free
$T$-elements returns to the number of $\gamma$ targets, so $T^{*}(\alpha)$ can again reproduce
every share for \emph{any} $\alpha$: the pathology of Section~\ref{sec:profiling} comes back
verbatim, and the $R_{a}-1$ over-identifying restrictions per area of Appendix~\ref{app:df} ---
the entire testable content of the two-tier geography --- vanish.
\item \emph{$\varepsilon$ is undefined exactly where we need it.} The ZE the model was built to
explain are those with no supplier, i.e.\ $\hat\gamma_{ls}=0$. There is no share to calibrate
against, so $\varepsilon_{sl}$ for precisely those ZE is unidentified by construction. One
would end up identifying $\alpha$ off the ZE for which $\varepsilon$ had to be assumed.
\item \emph{And assuming $\varepsilon=0$ for them is biased in a signable direction.} Empty ZE
are empty partly \emph{because} they drew a low $\nu$. Under the specification of
Section~\ref{sec:eps-gamma} this is exact:
\begin{equation}
\E\big[\nu_{sl}\;\big|\;K_{ls}=0\big]=\frac{1}{1+\sigma^{2}_{s}\mu_{ls}}\;<\;1,
\qquad \mu_{ls}\equiv N_{s}\bar q_{ls},
\label{eq:eps-selection}
\end{equation}
so plugging $\nu=1$ over-states an empty ZE's comparative advantage by the factor
$(1+\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls})$, over-predicts its probability of hosting a supplier, and forces
the estimator to raise $\alpha$ (or lower $N_{s}$) to reconcile the observed zero. This is
selection on unobserved comparative advantage, and it is first-order: at
$\sigma^{2}=1,\mu=5$ the factor is $6$. On top of it sits the ordinary incidental-parameter
problem --- one parameter per ZE with $O(1)$ observations each.
\end{enumerate}

Reading (B) avoids all three: it never needs $\varepsilon$ at a particular ZE, and
\eqref{eq:eps-selection} is not a bias to be corrected but a property the mixture embeds
automatically. It is what the rest of this section develops, under one design principle worth
stating in isolation:

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The number of free parameters must not grow with the number of employment zones.}
$\varepsilon$ may be a distribution; it may not be a parameter vector.
\end{quote}

\subsection{The Gamma specification and its closed form}
\label{sec:eps-gamma}

\begin{proposition}[Negative-binomial supplier counts]
\label{prop:negbin}
Relax Assumption~\ref{as:geo} to \eqref{eq:eps-spec} with $\nu_{sl}$ drawn i.i.d.\ across ZE
from a Gamma distribution with mean $1$ and variance $\sigma_{s}^{2}$ (shape $1/\sigma_{s}^{2}$,
scale $\sigma_{s}^{2}$), and Poissonize the variety count (Remark~\ref{rem:poisson}). Then the
supplier count of ZE $l$ is \textbf{negative binomial}:
\begin{equation}
\Prob(K_{ls}=n)
=\frac{\Gamma\big(n+1/\sigma_{s}^{2}\big)}{n!\,\Gamma\big(1/\sigma_{s}^{2}\big)}
\left(\frac{\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}}{1+\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}}\right)^{\!n}
\big(1+\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}\big)^{-1/\sigma_{s}^{2}},
\qquad \mu_{ls}=N_{s}\,\bar q_{ls},
\label{eq:negbin}
\end{equation}
where $\bar q_{ls}$ is the win-somewhere probability evaluated at $\nu=1$. In particular
\begin{equation}
\Prob(K_{ls}=0)=\big(1+\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}\big)^{-1/\sigma_{s}^{2}},
\qquad
\E[K_{ls}]=\mu_{ls},
\qquad
\mathrm{Var}(K_{ls})=\mu_{ls}\big(1+\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}\big),
\label{eq:negbin-moments}
\end{equation}
and the posterior of the deviation given the observed count is Gamma with mean
\begin{equation}
\E\big[\nu_{sl}\mid K_{ls}\big]=\frac{1+\sigma_{s}^{2}K_{ls}}{1+\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}} .
\label{eq:eps-posterior}
\end{equation}
As $\sigma_{s}^{2}\to0$, \eqref{eq:negbin} collapses to the Poisson case and
\eqref{eq:negbin-moments} to the cloglog of Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact}.
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
$K\mid\nu\sim\Pois(\mu\nu)$ and $\nu\sim\text{Gamma}(k,\vartheta)$ with $k\vartheta=1$ and
$k\vartheta^{2}=\sigma^{2}$, i.e.\ $k=1/\sigma^{2}$, $\vartheta=\sigma^{2}$. Mixing a Poisson
mean over a Gamma gives the negative binomial \eqref{eq:negbin}; the zero probability is the
Gamma moment generating function at $-\mu$, $\E[e^{-\mu\nu}]=(1+\vartheta\mu)^{-k}$; the
variance is $\E[\mathrm{Var}(K\mid\nu)]+\mathrm{Var}(\E[K\mid\nu])=\mu+\mu^{2}\sigma^{2}$;
and Gamma--Poisson conjugacy gives a Gamma$\big(k+K,\vartheta/(1+\vartheta\mu)\big)$ posterior
with mean $(k+K)\vartheta/(1+\vartheta\mu)$, which is \eqref{eq:eps-posterior}. Setting $K=0$
in \eqref{eq:eps-posterior} returns \eqref{eq:eps-selection}.
\end{proof}

All three closed forms in \eqref{eq:negbin-moments}--\eqref{eq:eps-posterior} were checked
against Monte-Carlo simulation of the mixture over $\sigma^{2}\in\{0.25,1,3\}$ and
$\mu\in\{0.3,1,5\}$, agreeing to simulation error ($\sim10^{-4}$).

Proposition~\ref{prop:negbin} buys three things.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{The analytical path survives.} The whole count-moment family
$\bar G_{s}(n)$ of Section~\ref{sec:moment} stays closed form --- a negative-binomial CDF
instead of a Poisson one --- so the GMM/analytical route is not sacrificed to the relaxation.
\item \emph{$\sigma_{s}^{2}$ has a second, independent signature.} Over-dispersion of the
supplier counts, $\mathrm{Var}(K)/\E[K]-1=\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls}$, is a direct read on the
within-area dispersion of comparative advantage, observable from the count distribution alone
and completely separate from the share dispersion.
\item \emph{ZE-level deviations for counterfactuals, at no parameter cost.}
\eqref{eq:eps-posterior} is an empirical-Bayes shrinkage of each ZE toward its area level:
active ZE get a $\nu$ informed by their own count, empty ZE get
$1/(1+\sigma^{2}\mu)<1$. This is how to recover ZE-level heterogeneity for a counterfactual
\emph{without} ever making $\varepsilon$ a free parameter.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Gamma is a convenience, not a commitment]
Log-normal $\varepsilon$ gives the familiar frailty cloglog with no closed form; it is just as
easy in the simulation, which is where the estimator lives anyway. The two parameterizations
agree to $O(\sigma^{4})$: for a mean-one Gamma,
$\mathrm{Var}(\ln\nu)=\psi'(1/\sigma^{2})=\sigma^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}\sigma^{4}+O(\sigma^{6})$. Note
also that $\E[\nu]=1$ implies $\E[\ln\nu]=\psi(1/\sigma^{2})+\ln\sigma^{2}<0$; the Jensen gap is
constant within a sector and is absorbed by the area$\times$sector fixed effect in
\eqref{eq:eps-logshare}, so no correction is needed.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[The price index is unaffected to leading order]
The deviations also enter
$\Phi_{sr}=\sum_{a}T_{s,a}\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\nu_{sl}(w_{l}\tau_{lrs})^{-\theta}$.
Because it sums over many ZE with $\E[\nu]=1$, $\Phi_{sr}$ concentrates by the law of large
numbers and is $\nu$-free up to $O(R^{-1/2})$. Hence $\E[\gamma_{lrs}]\approx\bar\gamma_{lrs}$
evaluated at $\nu=1$, and the \emph{expected} within-area log-share profile is still exactly
the distance profile of \eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}. The simulation evaluates the exact
object regardless; the approximation matters only for the analytical path.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The frailty attenuates the measured distance coefficient}
\label{sec:eps-attenuation}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} said the reduced form is exactly a cloglog with slope
$-\theta\alpha$. With $\sigma_{s}^{2}>0$ it becomes a cloglog \emph{with frailty}, and the
mapping from coefficient to parameter acquires a known attenuation.

\begin{lemma}[Frailty attenuation]
\label{lem:attenuation}
Under Proposition~\ref{prop:negbin},
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \ln\big(-\ln\Prob(K_{ls}=0)\big)}{\partial \ln d_{l}}
=-\theta\alpha\cdot A(u_{ls}),
\qquad
A(u)=\frac{u}{(1+u)\ln(1+u)}=\frac{1-e^{-t}}{t}\bigg|_{t=\ln(1+u)},
\qquad u_{ls}=\sigma_{s}^{2}\mu_{ls},
\label{eq:attenuation-frailty}
\end{equation}
where $A$ is strictly decreasing on $(0,\infty)$ with $A(0^{+})=1$ and $A(\infty)=0$. Hence the
cloglog distance coefficient understates $\theta\alpha$ in absolute value whenever
$\sigma_{s}^{2}>0$.
\end{lemma}

Representative values (verified numerically against \eqref{eq:negbin-moments}):
$A=0.94$ at $u=0.125$, $A=0.82$ at $u=0.5$, $A=0.61$ at $u=2$, $A=0.44$ at $u=6$. Three
consequences.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{A third wedge on the interpretation of $\hat\beta_{\mathrm{dist}}$.} The naive map
$\alpha=-\hat\beta_{\mathrm{dist}}/\theta$ of \eqref{eq:alpha-map} is now a \emph{lower bound}
on $|\alpha|$; the corrected map divides by $\theta\,\overline{A}$. This sits on top of the LPM
attenuation $\lambda$ (Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}(ii)) and the market-access wedge
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact}(d)). All three are harmless for the SMM --- which runs the
identical regression on simulated data, so every wedge is reproduced on both sides --- and all
three matter as soon as one wants to read the reduced-form coefficient structurally.
\item \emph{The frailty bends the distance profile, and that is testable with what is already
there.} $u_{ls}$ varies with $\bar q_{ls}$, hence with distance: near ZE (large $u$) are
attenuated more than remote ones, so the cloglog index becomes \emph{concave} in log distance.
The existing over-identified configuration is exactly the right test --- the binned
specification ($N_{\mathrm{REG}}=4$) against the power law ($N_{\mathrm{TAU}}=1$) leaves three
degrees of freedom on the reg\_coef block, and this concavity is what the residual pattern
across bins should look like if $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ is materially positive. That pattern is
information about $\sigma_{s}^{2}$, not misfit.
\item \emph{The cost of the relaxation is precision, not consistency.} Section~\ref{sec:within}
weakens from a deterministic statement to one in expectation: the expected within-area log-share
profile is still distance-only, so $\alpha$ remains identified from the within-area slope, but
the standard error of $\hat\alpha$ grows with $\sigma_{s}^{2}$ --- more of the within-area
variation is noise the model cannot attribute to distance. That is the honest price of not
imposing Assumption~\ref{as:geo} exactly, and it is a price paid in variance only \emph{as long
as $\varepsilon$ stays a distribution} (Section~\ref{sec:eps-trap}).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{What the ZE-level shares actually identify --- and a warning}
\label{sec:eps-calibration}

\emph{Unconditionally}, the within-area residual variance of log shares decomposes cleanly into
a structural and a sampling term, the latter being the granular noise of
Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion} carried through the log by the delta method:
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Var}_{\text{within}}\big(\ln\hat\gamma_{ls}\;\big|\;\text{FE},\ln d,x\big)
=\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon,s}
+\underbrace{\frac{\big(1-\gamma_{ls}\big)\big(1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}\big)}{N_{s}\,\gamma_{ls}}}_{\text{granular sampling}} .
\label{eq:eps-decomp}
\end{equation}
This suggests an attractive two-way separation: regress the squared within-area residual on
$1/\hat\gamma_{ls}$ and read $\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon}$ off the intercept and $N_{s}$ off the
slope, since only the sampling term scales with $1/\gamma$.

\textbf{Do not do this in the granular regime.} The feasible regression can only run on ZE with
$\hat\gamma_{ls}>0$, and that truncation removes precisely the low draws where most of the
dispersion of $\ln\hat\gamma$ lives. The truncation and the sampling inflation push in
\emph{opposite} directions and do not cancel in any controlled way. A Monte-Carlo of the exact
object --- one area with $R_{a}=40$ ZE, true $\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon}=0.30$, multinomial
granular shares, within-area regression of $\ln\hat\gamma$ on $\ln d$, active ZE only ---
gives:

\label{tab:eps-mc}

The conclusion is methodological, and it is the operative answer to ``how do we calibrate
$\varepsilon$?'':

\begin{quote}
$\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon,s}$ and $N_{s}$ must be estimated \textbf{jointly, inside the SMM},
where the simulation reproduces the granular sampling, the truncation at $\hat\gamma>0$, and the
selection \eqref{eq:eps-selection} automatically --- not read off a closed-form variance
decomposition.
\end{quote}

Their identifying moments are three, each computed \emph{identically} on the data and on
simulated data:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=\arabic*.]
\item the within-area dispersion of log shares among active ZE, net of the
area$\times$sector fixed effect, log distance, and the covariates of \eqref{eq:eps-spec};
\item the empty-ZE share $\bar G_{s}(0)$ --- a \emph{level};
\item the higher count thresholds $\bar G_{s}(n)$, $n\ge1$, equivalently the count
over-dispersion $\mathrm{Var}(K)/\E[K]-1=\sigma^{2}_{s}\mu$.
\end{enumerate}
$N_{s}$ loads mainly on (2), $\sigma_{s}^{2}$ on (1) and (3). That \emph{both} (1) and (3)
respond to $\sigma^{2}_{s}$, through completely different data --- share dispersion among active
ZE versus the shape of the count distribution --- is an over-identifying check on the granular
story as a whole, and it is the sharpest test the two-tier model offers.

This also retroactively qualifies Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}: the $1/N_{s}$ read on share
dispersion, equation \eqref{eq:app-var}, is correct \emph{unconditionally}, but on the observed
sample (active ZE, granular regime) it is contaminated both by $\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon}$ and by
truncation. Use it through the simulation, never as a plug-in formula.

Lemma~\ref{lem:censoring} adds a third contaminant to the same residual, and it is the largest of
the three in a sparse sector: conditional on activity the observed share is quantized toward
$1/N_{s}$, so the within-area dispersion of $\ln\hat\gamma$ among active ZE is compressed by the
censoring factor $B(u_{ls})$ --- which is itself correlated with size and distance. Moment (1)
above must therefore be matched through the simulation like the other two, and never inverted
analytically. The empirical evidence in Section~\ref{sec:risk-evidence} is what makes this
concrete: a within $R^{2}$ of $0.330$ on the observed log shares leaves two thirds of the
within-area variation unexplained, and none of that residual can be attributed to
$\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon}$ without the simulation.

\subsection{What is preserved, and the recommended sequence}
\label{sec:eps-preserved}

The relaxation costs two scalars per sector and preserves everything that mattered.

\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Parameter count.} $|\Areas^{+}_{s}|-1$ area levels, plus $\dim(\eta)$ common
covariate loadings, plus $(N_{s},\sigma^{2}_{s})$ per sector. Still $O(\#\text{areas})$, not
$O(\#\ZE)$: the design principle of Section~\ref{sec:eps-trap} holds.
\item \textbf{Profiling.} The area-aggregate inversion \eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn} is unchanged in
expectation, because $\E[\nu]=1$; the within-area split remains a function of $\alpha$ and
distance in expectation. The repair of Section~\ref{sec:profiling} survives.
\item \textbf{Over-identification.} Appendix~\ref{app:df} loses one restriction per sector (for
$\sigma^{2}_{s}$), not one per ZE. The $R_{a}-1$ within-area restrictions become restrictions on
the \emph{distribution} of the within-area profile rather than on each ZE.
\item \textbf{Nesting.} $\sigma^{2}_{s}=0$ with no covariates returns Assumption~\ref{as:geo}
exactly (and Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} exactly); $\sigma_{s}^{2}\to\infty$
approaches free ZE-level $T$, which is unidentified. The estimated $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ is thus a
\emph{measure of how wrong the pure area-level restriction is} --- and testing
$\sigma^{2}_{s}=0$ is the formal version of the diagnostic proposed in
Section~\ref{sec:risk}(2).
\end{itemize}

Recommended sequence, in increasing order of commitment:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=\arabic*.]
\item Run the descriptive within-area regression \eqref{eq:eps-logshare} on observed shares.
Read off how much within-area dispersion the covariates absorb, and how much is left. This costs
nothing and tells you whether $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ is worth estimating at all.
\item Estimate with $\sigma^{2}_{s}=0$ (Assumption~\ref{as:geo}, covariates included). This is
the cleanest configuration: $\alpha$ identified off a within-area gradient with no unobserved
heterogeneity, and Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} exact.
\item Add $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ with the three moments above. Compare $\hat\alpha$ across (2) and
(3): the gap is the frailty attenuation of Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation}, and if it is large the
reduced-form coefficient should not be read structurally.
\end{enumerate}

\section{From firms in the data to varieties in the model}
\label{sec:mapping}

The data record which upstream firms supply the downstream industry, but not which upstream
firm supplies which downstream buyer. Let $N$ be the number of distinct upstream firms
observed to supply the downstream industry in sector $s$. We now show that the model implies
the bounds
\begin{equation}
\frac{N}{\Nd}\;\le\;N_{s}\;\le\;N,
\label{eq:bounds}
\end{equation}
and that these bounds are exactly the granular content of the mapping between observed firms
and model varieties.

Consider a single variety $\rho$. Within origin ZE $l$, the firm that serves \emph{any} buyer
sourcing $\rho$ from $l$ is always the same firm --- the ZE-$l$ champion
$\varphi_{l\rho s}$ --- because the iceberg cost $\tau_{lrs}$ scales the cost of \emph{all}
ZE-$l$ firms identically and therefore never changes which firm is cheapest within the
ZE. Consequently:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item Each variety $\rho$ generates \emph{one distinct supplying firm per winning origin
ZE}. If all $\Nd$ downstream buyers happen to source $\rho$ from the same origin, variety
$\rho$ shows up as a single firm; if the $\Nd$ buyers source $\rho$ from $\Nd$ different
origins, it shows up as $\Nd$ distinct firms.
\item Therefore the number of distinct firms attributable to variety $\rho$ lies between $1$
and $\Nd$, and the total number of observed firms is
$N=\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}} \#\{\text{origins that win }\rho\text{ somewhere}\}\in[N_{s},\,N_{s}\Nd]$.
\end{itemize}
Inverting the range $N\in[N_{s},N_{s}\Nd]$ yields exactly \eqref{eq:bounds}. The bounds are
tight: $N_{s}=N$ when every variety is globally sourced from a single origin (no cross-market
reallocation), and $N_{s}=N/\Nd$ when every variety is sourced from a different origin in each
of the $\Nd$ downstream markets. The observed firm count therefore bounds, but does not pin
down, $N_{s}$; the distribution of the number of suppliers across origins does.

\begin{remark}[The champion is ZE-specific, not area-specific]
\label{rem:champion-ze}
Assumption~\ref{as:geo} equalizes the Fr\'echet \emph{scale} across ZE within an area, not the
draws. Each ZE therefore has its own champion for each variety, two ZE in the same area can
both win different varieties, and the firm$\to$variety accounting above is unchanged: the unit
that generates an observed supplier is still (variety $\times$ winning ZE), and the counts in
\eqref{eq:bounds} are counts over ZE. Nothing in this section depends on the level at which
$T$ is defined.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What is and is not observable]
Because firm-to-firm links are unobserved, the per-market Binomial \eqref{eq:binomial} is not
directly measurable. What \emph{is} measurable is the \emph{pooled} count of supplying firms
per origin ZE (the object underlying \eqref{eq:bounds}). The identifying moment below is
built from that observable pooled count, which the simulation reproduces without requiring the
firm-to-firm assignment.
\end{remark}

\section{The identifying moment: the distribution of suppliers per region}
\label{sec:moment}

\subsection{The LMM template}

LMM face the mirror-image problem. They observe, for each French exporter, the number of
foreign buyers it serves, and they use the \emph{distribution of the number of buyers per
seller} to identify the strength of search frictions. Their theoretical object is the
probability $h_{i}(M)$ that an exporter serves exactly $M$ buyers in market $i$
(\citealp{lmm2023}, eq.~7), and their headline moment is a variance built from ratios
$h(M)/h(1)$ that is (i) monotone in the structural parameter of interest and (ii) uncorrelated
with the intercept, which is governed by the raw number of potential sellers and buyers
(\citealp{lmm2023}, eq.~8; Online Appendix~C). The empirical counterpart they display is the
cumulative curve ``share of sellers with $x$ buyers or less'' (Online Appendix Figure~D.1).

\subsection{The count moment in our model}

We transpose LMM to the \emph{supplier} count. For sector $s$, let $K_{ls}$ be the total number
of firm-links (equivalently, the number of distinct varieties, netted for the champion identity
of Section~\ref{sec:mapping}) that origin ZE $l$ supplies to the downstream industry. The
empirical moment is the cumulative distribution of $K_{ls}$ across origin ZE \emph{inside
active attraction areas},
\begin{equation}
G_{s}(n)\;=\;\#\big\{\,l\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s} : K_{ls}\le n\,\big\},
\qquad
\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}\equiv\bigsqcup_{a\in\Areas^{+}_{s}}\mathcal{L}_{a},
\qquad n=0,1,2,\dots,
\label{eq:moment}
\end{equation}
i.e. \emph{the number of ZE hosting at most $n$ suppliers}. The threshold $n=0$ is the
number of ZE with \emph{no} supplier --- the quantity we set out to endogenize --- and the
higher thresholds trace out the whole granular extensive-margin curve, exactly as LMM's
``$x$ buyers or less'' curve does on the buyer side.

The restriction of the support to $\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}$ is the count-moment counterpart of
Section~\ref{sec:activeset-area} and of Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}: ZE in empty attraction areas are
structural zeros carrying no information about $(\alpha,N_{s})$, and including them would let
an arbitrary number of mechanically-explained zeros drown the informative ones. Equivalently,
one may compute \eqref{eq:moment} \emph{area by area} and aggregate, which makes the parallel
with the fixed effect explicit.

The model counterpart is the expectation of \eqref{eq:moment}. Writing $q_{ls}$ for the
probability that origin $l$ wins a given variety \emph{somewhere} in the downstream industry
(the union over buyers of the per-market win event; see Remark~\ref{rem:dependence}), the
industry-wide supplier count of origin $l$ is a Binomial-type count
$K_{ls}\sim\text{PoissonBinomial}$ over the $N_{s}$ varieties, and
\begin{equation}
\E\big[G_{s}(n)\big]
=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}}\Prob\big(K_{ls}\le n\big),
\qquad
\Prob(K_{ls}=0)=(1-q_{ls})^{N_{s}}.
\label{eq:model-moment}
\end{equation}
Under Poissonization (Remark~\ref{rem:poisson}), Poisson thinning makes this exact and clean:
each variety is won somewhere by $l$ independently with probability $q_{ls}$, so
$K_{ls}\sim\Pois(N_{s}q_{ls})$ and $\Prob(K_{ls}=0)=\exp(-N_{s}q_{ls})$ with no
Poisson-Binomial machinery required. If comparative advantage is additionally allowed to
deviate within areas (Section~\ref{sec:eps}), the Gamma mixture replaces this Poisson by a
\emph{negative binomial} \eqref{eq:negbin} --- still closed form, and with the over-dispersion
parameter $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ appearing as a second identified object. The single per-market case ($\Nd=1$) gives
$q_{ls}=\gamma_{ls}$ and reduces \eqref{eq:model-moment} to the clean Binomial
\eqref{eq:binomial}; with $\Nd>1$ the union probability $q_{ls}$ is evaluated by simulation
(or by a low-dimensional integral over the champion draws), which also respects the
cross-market dependence of Remark~\ref{rem:dependence}.

\subsection{Why this moment identifies $N_{s}$}

Given the sourcing shares $\{\gamma_{lrs}\}$ --- which are pinned down by the area-aggregated
value shares and by $\alpha$, independently of $N_{s}$, because \eqref{eq:share-area} does not
involve $N_{s}$ --- the only free object shaping \eqref{eq:model-moment} is $N_{s}$. Increasing
$N_{s}$ shifts the whole curve $G_{s}(\cdot)$ to the right (more suppliers per ZE, fewer
empty ZE): $\partial\,\E[G_{s}(0)]/\partial N_{s}=\sum_{l}(1-q_{ls})^{N_{s}}\ln(1-q_{ls})<0$,
so the number of empty ZE is strictly decreasing in $N_{s}$. A small set of thresholds
$n\in\{0,1,2,\dots\}$ --- in the spirit of LMM's three ratio-moments --- over-identifies the
single parameter $N_{s}$ and is robust to the exact upper support. Because the mapping is
smooth in $N_{s}$ through the expected count \eqref{eq:model-moment}, $N_{s}$ can be treated as
a continuous parameter by the optimizer and rounded only for reporting.

It bears repeating (Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact}(b)) that the count moment is
\emph{necessary}: $N_{s}$ is invisible to the distance regression, where it is collinear with
the fixed effect. The regression gives $\alpha$; the count level gives $N_{s}$.

\section{Computing the granular model: what is drawn, what is integrated in closed form,
and how $N_{s}$ is obtained}
\label{sec:compute}

This section answers three implementation questions that the granular assumption raises, and
that determine whether the estimator is usable at all. What exactly is simulated
(Section~\ref{sec:compute-draws})? Does a finite $N_{s}$ mean we must draw exactly $N_{s}$
varieties (Section~\ref{sec:compute-rb})? And in what sense is $N_{s}$ \emph{estimated} rather
than calibrated (Section~\ref{sec:compute-profile})?

\subsection{What is drawn: the Fr\'echet draw \emph{is} the maximum over a continuum of firms}
\label{sec:compute-draws}

A natural reading of the granular assumption is that one should now, for every
(sector $\times$ region $\times$ variety), draw a large number of individual firms, keep the most
productive, and only then run the Ricardian selection across regions. \textbf{That reading is
exactly right as a description of the model --- and the Fr\'echet draw already performs it, in
closed form.}

Recall the assumption of Section~\ref{sec:granular}: within $(l,s,\rho)$ the firms with
productivity above $z$ form a Poisson process with mean $T_{sl}z^{-\theta}$. The number of firms
above $z$ is then Poisson with that mean, so
\begin{equation}
\Prob\Big(\max_{\text{firms in }(l,s,\rho)}\varphi\;\le\;z\Big)
=\Prob\big(\text{no firm above }z\big)
=\exp\!\big(-T_{sl}z^{-\theta}\big),
\label{eq:champion-frechet}
\end{equation}
which is precisely $\Frechet(T_{sl},\theta)$. \emph{One Fr\'echet draw per (sector, region,
variety) is the champion of a continuum of firms} --- not an approximation to it, but its exact
law. Drawing $M$ firms explicitly and taking the maximum would converge to \eqref{eq:champion-frechet}
as $M\to\infty$, at the cost of $M$ times the work and an extra layer of Monte-Carlo error, and
would return the same object in the limit. So the simulation structure is unchanged from the
baseline:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=\arabic*.]
\item one Fr\'echet draw per (sector, region, variety) $=$ that region's champion cost
\eqref{eq:region-cost};
\item Ricardian selection across regions, $\min_{l} w_{l}\tau_{lrs}/\varphi_{l\rho s}$, per
downstream buyer;
\item a region ``supplies'' a variety if it wins it for at least one buyer.
\end{enumerate}
This is the content of Section~\ref{sec:selection} and of Question~1
(Section~\ref{sec:q1}): keeping the continuum of firms is what makes the per-variety competition
collapse to a single closed-form draw. \textbf{Granularity changes the number of varieties, not
how a variety is competed for.}

\subsection{Where the finite $N_{s}$ enters: integrate the Binomial, do not simulate it}
\label{sec:compute-rb}

The baseline used $N_{\rho}$ draws as a \emph{numerical} stand-in for a continuum of varieties.
Under granularity $N_{s}$ is \emph{structural} and finite. It is tempting to conclude that the
simulation must now draw exactly $N_{s}$ varieties. It must not, and the reason is worth stating
carefully because it is what keeps the estimator usable.

The estimator matches \emph{expected} moments $\E[m(\theta)]$ against one observed realization.
There are two ways to compute that expectation.

\begin{description}[topsep=3pt,itemsep=3pt,leftmargin=1.6em]
\item[(a) Simulate economies.] Draw $R$ independent economies, each with exactly $N_{s}$
varieties, compute the moment in each, average. Cost $R\times N_{s}$ draws; Monte-Carlo error
$O(1/\sqrt{R})$.
\item[(b) Integrate the variety count analytically.] Across varieties the win-somewhere events
are i.i.d.\ (draws are independent across $\rho$), so \emph{conditional on the per-variety
win-somewhere probability $q_{ls}$}, the supplier count is exactly
\begin{equation}
K_{ls}\;\sim\;\Bin\big(N_{s},\,q_{ls}\big),
\qquad
\Prob(K_{ls}=0)=(1-q_{ls})^{N_{s}} .
\label{eq:compute-binomial}
\end{equation}
Only $q_{ls}$ --- a \emph{one-variety} expectation --- needs simulating, and it can be estimated
with as many draws $N_{\rho}$ as we like, entirely independently of $N_{s}$
(Lemma~\ref{lem:q-free} below proves that independence rather than assuming it). Every granular
object then follows in closed form.
\end{description}

Route (b) is not an approximation to route (a): it is the exact integral that (a) approximates.
It is a Rao--Blackwellization --- the Binomial is integrated out instead of being sampled --- and
it matters for two reasons.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item \emph{Noise.} With $N_{s}$ of order $10^{2}$--$10^{3}$, route (a) makes every moment carry
granular sampling noise of the same order of magnitude as the granularity itself, and that noise
is \emph{entangled} with the parameter of interest: a smaller $N_{s}$ means both more granularity
and a noisier moment. Route (b) has noise only in $\hat q$, controlled by $N_{\rho}$, and that
noise does not scale with $N_{s}$.
\item \emph{Cost.} Route (b) costs the same as the baseline regardless of $N_{s}$, and lets
$N_{s}$ be varied --- as the profiling of Section~\ref{sec:compute-profile} does, many times per
loss evaluation --- without re-simulating anything.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{What route (b) gives up: nothing that is targeted.} Remark~\ref{rem:dependence} noted
that supplier counts are positively dependent \emph{across} buyers and hence across cells within
one realized economy, and \eqref{eq:compute-binomial} is a per-cell marginal. But every moment the
estimator targets is a \emph{mean} --- the empty-ZE share $\bar G_{s}(0)$ is a mean over ZE, the
regression score is a weighted sum over cells --- and expectations of means are means of
expectations \emph{whatever the dependence}. The cross-cell dependence therefore affects only the
\emph{variance} of the moments, never their expectation. And that variance is not something route
(a) would need to supply either: the empirical moment's sampling variability is the bootstrap
$\Sigma_{\text{data}}$ (Appendix~\ref{app:estimation}), and the variability of \emph{our estimate}
of $\E[m]$ is $\Sigma_{\text{sim}}$, obtained by re-drawing $\hat q$. Neither requires simulating
the joint law at $N_{s}$.

\paragraph{A validation gate rather than an assumption.} The equivalence of (a) and (b) is
checkable and should be checked once: at $\hat N_{s}$, simulate $R$ economies with exactly
$\hat N_{s}$ varieties, average their moments, and confirm they match the closed-form values to
$O(1/\sqrt{R})$. That gate turns the argument above into a test.

\subsection{What $q_{ls}$ depends on --- and why it is free of $N_{s}$}
\label{sec:compute-qfree}

Route (b) rests on one claim: the per-variety win-somewhere probability $q_{ls}$ can be estimated
once, with many draws, and then reused for \emph{any} $N_{s}$. That claim deserves a proof rather
than an assertion, because if $q_{ls}$ moved with $N_{s}$ the whole construction would collapse
into a fixed point.

\begin{lemma}[$q_{ls}$ is free of $N_{s}$]
\label{lem:q-free}
Fix a variety $\rho$. Origin $l$ wins it for buyer $r$ iff
$c_{l\rho s}(r)<c_{l'\rho s}(r)$ for all $l'$, i.e.\ iff
\begin{equation}
\frac{w_{l}\tau_{lrs}}{\varphi_{l\rho s}}<\min_{l'\neq l}\frac{w_{l'}\tau_{l'rs}}{\varphi_{l'\rho s}} .
\label{eq:win-comparison}
\end{equation}
The win-somewhere event is the union of \eqref{eq:win-comparison} over $r$, so
\begin{equation}
q_{ls}=q_{ls}\big(T_{s\cdot},\,w,\,\tau,\,\theta\big)
\end{equation}
and nothing else. In particular the variety count $N_{s}$, the sector price index $P_{sr}$,
downstream costs $c_{r}$ and expenditures $E_{sr}$ do \textbf{not} appear.
\end{lemma}

The economics of the lemma is that \eqref{eq:win-comparison} is a statement about \emph{relative
costs}, whereas $N_{s}$ and $P_{sr}$ are aggregators built \emph{after} the winners are known.
Expenditure allocates value across winners; it does not choose them. The love-of-variety channel
$P_{sr}\propto N_{s}^{1/(1-\nu_{s})}$ therefore propagates to $c_{r}$, $y_{r}$, $E_{sr}$ --- and
stops there. Note that this is stronger than the normalization of Section~\ref{sec:q2}(a):
$q_{ls}\perp N_{s}$ holds \emph{whether or not} the love-of-variety term is normalized out. The
normalization decides only whether the \emph{value} block moves with $N_{s}$; it is irrelevant to
the extensive margin.

\begin{quote}
\textbf{The one condition that would break Lemma~\ref{lem:q-free}: endogenous upstream wages.}
If $w_{l}$ cleared an upstream labour market, then $N_{s}\to P_{sr}\to$ downstream demand $\to$
upstream labour demand $\to w_{l}$, and $w$ enters \eqref{eq:win-comparison} directly. With $w$
taken as data (the current specification), the channel is absent. If wages are ever endogenized,
route (b) becomes a fixed point in $(q,N_{s})$ and must be re-derived.
\end{quote}

\paragraph{This is not ``solve at $N_{s}=\infty$, then re-simulate''.} A natural but incorrect
reading of route (b) is that one estimates the model in the continuum limit and afterwards
re-runs it at a finite $N_{s}$. That would be a two-stage approximation with no guarantee. What
route (b) does is different: $N_{\rho}$ and $N_{s}$ live on \emph{different axes}. $N_{\rho}$ is
a Monte-Carlo sample size used to estimate a \emph{per-variety} probability; $N_{s}$ is a
structural count that multiplies independent repetitions of that single-variety experiment. The
analogy is exact: to compute the probability of at least one head in $N_{s}=10$ tosses of a coin
of unknown bias $q$, one does not simulate ten tosses many times --- one estimates $q$ from $10^{6}$
tosses and evaluates $1-(1-q)^{10}$ in closed form. The $10^{6}$ tosses are not ``the coin in the
limit''; they are how $q$ is learned.

\paragraph{One maintained assumption, stated --- and it is not free.} With a finite $N_{s}$ the
realised within-sector price index \eqref{eq:price-index} is a \emph{random} finite CES aggregate.
The self-normalised weights make $\E[P_{sr}^{1-\nu_{s}}]$ invariant to the variety count --- that
much is Option~1 of Section~\ref{sec:q2}(a), for free. But $P_{sr}$ is a \emph{convex} transform of
that average, so the realised index is biased upward by Jensen, and the bias decays only as fast
as the average concentrates. At the estimated calibration $\theta=1$, $\nu_{s}=1.5$ the regularity
condition of \eqref{eq:app-cv}, $\theta>2(\nu_{s}-1)$, reads $1>1$: it \textbf{fails at equality},
$(c^{*})^{1-\nu_{s}}$ has infinite variance, and the average converges at $\sqrt{N/\log N}$ rather
than $\sqrt{N}$. Simulating the exact object:

\textbf{certainty-equivalent} reading --- downstream firms optimise against

therefore \emph{granular sourcing with an expected-price-index general equilibrium}. Two things
follow. Most of the $6\%$ would not have been identifying variation anyway: a price-level shift is
observationally equivalent to a technology shift (Appendix~\ref{app:price}), so $\Omega^{s}$ and
$A$ would absorb it and only the cross-sector pattern of $N_{s}$ would survive as signal --- the
assumption records that rather than letting a slowly-converging Jensen term be mistaken for
technology. And by Lemma~\ref{lem:q-free} none of it touches $q_{ls}$ or the extensive margin, so
the identification of $\alpha$ and $N_{s}$ is unaffected either way. What the assumption \emph{must
not} do is survive into counterfactuals: the love-of-variety propagation channel lives in the
realised index, so any counterfactual moving the variety set has to use it (Appendix~\ref{app:price}).

\subsection{Two different regressions: cell level and firm level}
\label{sec:compute-collapse}

Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} is a statement about the \emph{cell}: it describes
$\Prob(K_{ls}\ge1)$, the event that ZE $l$ hosts at least one supplier, and it delivers a cloglog
on the \textbf{supplier} indicator with slope $-\theta\alpha$. The regression the estimator
actually matches is a different object --- it is run at the \textbf{firm} level, with the firm's
own productivity as a control --- and its exact form is the mirror image. Both are correct; they
are simply not the same regression, and confusing them inverts the outcome convention.

\begin{proposition}[Exact firm-level reduced form]
\label{prop:firm-level}
Identify a firm with a (cell, variety) champion, and let $z$ be its productivity. Its delivered
cost to buyer $a$ is $w_{l}\tau_{las}/z$, and it supplies iff every competitor's delivered cost is
higher. The competitors' minimum cost is Weibull with scale
$\Phi^{-l}_{sa}=\sum_{l'\neq l}T_{sl'}(w_{l'}\tau_{l'as})^{-\theta}$, so
\begin{equation}
\Prob\big(\text{firm supplies}\mid z\big)
=\exp\!\Big(-\Phi^{-l}_{sa}\,\big(w_{l}\tau_{las}\big)^{\theta}\,z^{-\theta}\Big),
\label{eq:firm-win}
\end{equation}
and therefore
\begin{equation}
\Prob\big(\text{firm does \emph{not} supply}\mid z\big)=1-\exp\!\big(-\exp(\eta)\big),
\qquad
\eta=\underbrace{\ln\Phi^{-l}_{sa}}_{\text{FE}}+\theta\ln w_{l}
\;+\;\theta\alpha\,\ln d_{la}\;-\;\theta\,\ln z .
\label{eq:firm-cloglog}
\end{equation}
That is a cloglog on the \textbf{\texttt{not\_supply}} indicator, with coefficient
$\boxed{+\theta\alpha}$ on log distance and $\boxed{-\theta}$ on log productivity. The variety
count $N_{s}$ does not appear.
\end{proposition}

Verified by simulating the exact object ($\theta=1$, $\alpha=0.30$, firms as (cell $\times$
variety) champions, one dominant buyer per area):

\midrule

supplier indicator   & $-0.426$ & $+1.452$\\

\caption{At the firm level the \texttt{not\_supply} convention recovers both structural

by the unit of observation.}
\label{tab:firm-convention}
\end{table}

Three consequences.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\alph*)]
\item \emph{The firm-level regression is exactly free of $N_{s}$.} Equation
\eqref{eq:firm-cloglog} contains no variety count: conditioning on the firm removes it, where at
the cell level it entered through the fixed effect and the invariance held only up to
specification error. So the division of labour is exact --- block~4 carries $\alpha$, the count
block carries $N_{s}$.
\item \emph{The productivity coefficient is a free over-identifying test.} It should equal
$-\theta$. With $\theta$ calibrated at $1$, a coefficient far from $-1$ says either that $\theta$
is wrong or that the mapping between observed firms and model champions is broken. Extending the
$\beta$ block of $\Sigma$ to include it would let $\theta$ be estimated rather than calibrated.
\item \emph{The design cannot be collapsed to one row per cell.} $\ln z$ varies across varieties
\emph{within} a cell, so the row structure is genuinely (variety $\times$ cell). The cell-level
object $\Prob(K_{ls}\ge1)=1-(1-q_{ls})^{N_{s}}$ is a \emph{different} moment; it is what the count
block uses, not what the regression estimates.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[Which unit the observed firms correspond to]
\label{rem:firm-mapping}
Proposition~\ref{prop:firm-level} identifies each observed firm with a (cell, variety) champion.
Read literally that gives every cell exactly $N_{s}$ firms, whereas observed firm counts vary by
orders of magnitude. Closing that gap requires either accepting a sample mismatch --- the model's
$z$ are maxima over a continuum and so are shifted right relative to the data's firms --- or giving
the model a firm count, e.g.\ a productivity floor $\bar z_{s}$ making the count in $(l,s,\rho)$
$\Pois(T_{sl}\bar z_{s}^{-\theta})$. The latter reinstates precisely the Poisson-firm-count device
that Section~\ref{sec:q1} set aside: \emph{choosing a firm-level empirical moment is what forces
it back in}. The $\ln z$ coefficient of (b) above is the cheapest diagnostic of which situation
one is in.
\end{remark}

\subsection{How $N_{s}$ is obtained: profiling, not calibration}
\label{sec:compute-profile}

$N_{s}$ is \emph{estimated}, by the same weighted objective as every other parameter. It is
simply \emph{concentrated out} rather than searched over jointly. Write the parameter vector as
$\theta=(\theta_{c},N)$ with $\theta_{c}=(\Omega^{L},\Omega^{s},A,\alpha,T)$ continuous and
$N=(N_{1},\dots,N_{S})\in\mathbb{N}^{S}$, and let $r(\theta)$ be the moment residual. Then
\begin{equation}
\widehat N(\theta_{c})
=\argmin_{N\in\prod_{s}[N^{\mathrm{lo}}_{s},N^{\mathrm{hi}}_{s}]\cap\mathbb{N}}
\;r(\theta_{c},N)'\,W\,r(\theta_{c},N),
\qquad
\hat\theta_{c}=\argmin_{\theta_{c}} \;r\big(\theta_{c},\widehat N(\theta_{c})\big)'W\,r\big(\theta_{c},\widehat N(\theta_{c})\big).
\label{eq:profile-Ns}
\end{equation}
This is the textbook concentrated (profiled) extremum estimator: $(\hat\theta_{c},\widehat
N(\hat\theta_{c}))$ is \emph{identical} to the joint minimiser over $(\theta_{c},N)$. Nothing is
fixed outside the estimation; the objective is the same $r'Wr$ throughout. Two practical
properties make \eqref{eq:profile-Ns} cheap: the inner minimisation needs \emph{no
re-simulation} (Lemma~\ref{lem:q-free} --- $\hat q$ is held fixed while $N$ varies), and
$\widehat N$ is an integer at every evaluation, so integrality is never relaxed and never
rounded.

\subsection{With the supplier indicator, $\ln N_{s}$ is absorbed by the fixed effect}
\label{sec:compute-convention}

The outcome convention decides how much work \eqref{eq:profile-Ns} actually has to do. Write the
cell-level probability \eqref{eq:binomial} in the cloglog index of the \textbf{supplier}
indicator:
\begin{equation}
\eta_{ls}
=\ln\Big(-\ln\Prob(K_{ls}=0)\Big)
=\underbrace{\ln N_{s}}_{\text{constant within }s}
\;+\;\ln\big(-\ln(1-q_{ls})\big),
\label{eq:eta-separable}
\end{equation}
\emph{exactly} --- and exactly, note, under the Binomial as well as the Poissonized form, since
$\ln\!\big(\!-\!\ln(1-q)^{N_{s}}\big)=\ln N_{s}+\ln(-\ln(1-q))$ identically. Because $\ln N_{s}$
is constant within every area$\times$sector group, it is \textbf{absorbed by the fixed effect}.
The distance coefficient is therefore invariant to $N_{s}$, and equals $-\theta\alpha$ ---
Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact}, now visible as an algebraic identity rather than an
approximation.

run once at the optimum.

other configuration examined in this note (the LPM factor $\lambda$ of
Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}(ii), the frailty factor of Lemma~\ref{lem:attenuation}, the censoring factor
of Lemma~\ref{lem:censoring}).
\item \textbf{The invariance is conditional on specification.} It is exact only if the distance
term is flexible enough to fit the true trade-cost profile; under misspecification a few percent
of $N_{s}$-dependence returns. This is an argument for the binned specification
($N_{\mathrm{REG}}=4$) over the single power-law coefficient, and for checking the drift
empirically rather than assuming it away.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[The outcome convention is dictated by the unit, not chosen]
\label{rem:convention-data}
The cloglog link is not symmetric under outcome reversal, so the convention cannot be flipped post
hoc. But it is also not a free choice: Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} fixes it as the
supplier indicator for a \emph{cell}-level regression, and Proposition~\ref{prop:firm-level} fixes
it as \texttt{not\_supply} for a \emph{firm}-level one. Since the estimator matches a firm-level
regression, \texttt{not\_supply} is the correct convention and the empirical target needs no
regeneration.
\end{remark}

\section{What else must change}
\label{sec:q2}

The finite variety count and the area-level comparative advantage propagate to five other
parts of the model. None is fatal; each is a deliberate modeling choice that must be made
consistently.

\paragraph{(a) The within-sector price index and love of variety.}
With a continuum of varieties, the sector-$s$ price index $P_{sr}$ is deterministic. With
$N_{s}$ varieties it is a finite CES aggregate,
\begin{equation}
P_{sr}=\Big[\textstyle\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}} p_{\rho rs}^{\,1-\nu_{s}}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_{s}}},
\qquad p_{\rho rs}=\frac{1}{\mu}\min_{l} c_{l\rho s}(r),
\label{eq:price-index}
\end{equation}
whose expectation carries a mechanical variety term,
$\E\!\big[P_{sr}^{\,1-\nu_{s}}\big]=N_{s}\,\Gamma\!\big(1+\tfrac{1-\nu_{s}}{\theta}\big)\,
\mu^{\nu_{s}-1}\,\Phi_{sr}^{-\frac{1-\nu_{s}}{\theta}}$, so that
$P_{sr}\propto N_{s}^{\,1/(1-\nu_{s})}$ (derived in Appendix~\ref{app:price}).
Thus $N_{s}$ mechanically moves the sector price level
(love of variety) and, through it, every value-based moment (labor share, industry shares
$\pi_{s}$, regional shares $\pi_{r}$). Two clean options:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{(Recommended)} Normalize the love-of-variety term out by redefining the sector
scale (e.g. $T_{s,a}\mapsto T_{s,a}N_{s}^{\theta/(1-\nu_{s})}$ or an offsetting sector-share
renormalization) so that $\E[P_{sr}]$ is invariant to $N_{s}$. Then $N_{s}$ is identified
\emph{solely} from the extensive-margin count \eqref{eq:moment}, and the value moments are
numerically unchanged from the baseline. This keeps $N_{s}$ from being confounded with
productivity/technology levels. Note that the rescaling is common across origins within a
sector, hence invisible to the sourcing shares \eqref{eq:share-area} and to the whole
identification scheme of Table~\ref{tab:division}.
\item Follow LMM's general-equilibrium embedding (Online Appendix~B.3): assume a continuum of
sectors and let downstream firms optimize against the \emph{expected} price index
$\E[P_{sr}]$, neglecting the granular randomness. Aggregate value moments then average the
granularity out, but $N_{s}$ retains a footprint on price levels and is jointly identified from
prices and counts. This is more faithful but couples $N_{s}$ to the value block.
\end{enumerate}
Option~1 is the parsimonious choice and matches the current code's separation between the
value moments and the extensive-margin block.

\paragraph{(b) Value moments are now expectations; the $\gamma$ target is an average share ---
and is matched at the \emph{area} level.}
Conditional on winning, the price a buyer pays for a variety is distributed identically across
origins (the standard EK property), so the \emph{expected} expenditure share on origin $l$
still equals the probability share $\gamma_{lrs}$ of \eqref{eq:share-area}. Hence the
value-share moment is matched in expectation exactly as before, and its empirical target
should be read as the \emph{average realized share}. Two changes follow from
Assumption~\ref{as:geo}:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item The \emph{just-identified} share moments are the \textbf{area aggregates}
$\gamma_{s,a}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\gamma_{ls}$, one per active $(s,a)$ --- these are what
the profiling step \eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn} inverts. The ZE-level shares remain in the moment
vector, but as \emph{over-identifying} content: they test the within-area distance profile
implied by \eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}. Keeping both, with the efficient weight matrix
deciding how much each is worth, is the right default.
\item The second moment is new: the realized share is now a random variable whose dispersion
also carries information about $N_{s}$ (a thinner sector has noisier shares).
Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion} makes this precise: the realized value shares behave like a
multinomial draw of \emph{effective sample size} $N_{s}/(1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2})$, so their
variance is $\propto 1/N_{s}$ and a bootstrap over suppliers identifies $N_{s}$ directly. The
estimator can ignore this extra content and rely on \eqref{eq:moment}, or exploit it for
efficiency.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{(c) The active set and the reference normalization: masking moves \emph{up} one
tier.}
In the baseline, region-sector pairs with zero empirical sourcing are dropped up front
(\texttt{T\_MASK}) because the continuum model cannot rationalize a zero. Under the granular
model with area-level comparative advantage the correct rule is \emph{tier-dependent}, and
this is the single most consequential change to the code's data layer:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{At the ZE tier, stop masking.} A ZE with no supplier that sits inside an active
attraction area is an \emph{endogenous outcome} of \eqref{eq:binomial}: it has
$T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}>0$ and contributes the informative event $\{K_{ls}=0\}$ to the count moment
\eqref{eq:moment} and to the regression. These are exactly the status-$2$ (``control'') pairs,
which now become model objects rather than exogenous zeros.
\item \textbf{At the area tier, keep masking.} An attraction area with no supplier at all gets
no parameter and enters no moment (Section~\ref{sec:activeset-area}).
\item \textbf{Parameter block.} The estimated $T$ block is indexed by active $(s,a)$ pairs,
not active $(s,\ZE)$ pairs. The per-sector reference normalization becomes one reference
\emph{area}, $T_{s,\mathrm{ref}(s)}=1$; $\gamma$ and hence $(1-\gamma)^{N_{s}}$ are invariant
to the within-sector scale of $T$, so $N_{s}$ is identified given the normalized $T$ exactly as
before.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{(d) Joint identification of $(\alpha,N_{s})$ and the distance regression.}
The distance regression (block~4) and the count moment \eqref{eq:moment} are now two views of
the \emph{same} endogenous extensive margin, and Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact} says
what each sees. Distance raises $\tau_{lrs}=d_{lr}^{\alpha}$, lowers $\gamma_{lrs}$, and thus
raises the zero probability: the trade-cost elasticity $\alpha$ governs how the extensive
margin varies \emph{with distance within an area}, while $N_{s}$ governs its overall
\emph{level}, which the fixed effect strips from the regression. Matching the regression slope
and the count level therefore identifies $\alpha$ and $N_{s}$ separately
(Table~\ref{tab:division}), and makes the block-4 extensive margin an exact model object
rather than the FKG approximation currently used. This resolves, rather than sidesteps, the
identification concern flagged in the code's caveats about the FKG-biased $\alpha$ column:
with genuine granular zeros, the extensive margin is computed exactly (by simulation) instead
of approximated, and with area-level $T$ the distance gradient is no longer absorbable by the
comparative-advantage block.

\paragraph{(e) The geography inputs and the profiling step.}
Two mechanical requirements. First, the model needs the ZE$\to$area map explicitly as a
primitive (in the implementation it already exists as the nearest-downstream assignment used
to build the fixed effect: the map and the regression's fixed effect must be the \emph{same}
object, or the alignment argument of Section~\ref{sec:motivation-fe} fails). Second, the
$\gamma$-inversion / Sinkhorn profiling must be re-expressed on area aggregates
\eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn}: one multiplicative update per active $(s,a)$, with the within-area
split recomputed from distance at the current $\alpha$. The system remains square and retains
its contraction structure (Appendix~\ref{app:df}).

\section{Answers to the three questions}
\label{sec:answers}

\subsection*{Question 1: Can I keep the continuum of firms for each variety?}
\label{sec:q1}

\textbf{Yes --- and it is the correct choice, not merely an admissible one.} The granularity
that generates empty regions comes entirely from making the number of \emph{varieties} finite;
it does not require discretizing firms. Keeping a continuum of firms within each ZE and
variety buys three things:
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{Closed-form per-variety selection.} The continuum collapses each ZE's
within-ZE competition into a single Fr\'echet champion cost \eqref{eq:region-cost}, so the
probability that ZE $l$ wins a variety is \emph{exactly} the EK share $\gamma_{lrs}$
\eqref{eq:per-variety-win}. You retain the entire analytical sourcing apparatus of the baseline
model unchanged; only the number of independent draws of that competition changes from a
continuum to $N_{s}$.
\item \emph{A clean firm$\to$variety mapping.} Because the iceberg cost scales all ZE-$l$
firms equally, the ZE-$l$ champion is the unique ZE-$l$ firm that ever serves any buyer
sourcing that variety from $l$ (Section~\ref{sec:mapping}). This is exactly what makes the
observed firm count $N$ translate into the variety bounds $N/\Nd\le N_{s}\le N$.
\item \emph{No need for a Pareto lower bound or a Poisson firm count.} LMM obtain granularity by
making the \emph{number of firms} itself Poisson (with a Pareto productivity floor). Your route
--- a fixed $N_{s}$ with a continuum of firms per variety --- is the ``fixed number of EK draws''
granular model \citep{eks2012}. It has the practical advantages that $N_{s}$ is a single clean
integer parameter per sector and the extensive margin is an exact Binomial \eqref{eq:binomial}.
\end{enumerate}
The one conceptual point to keep straight is that with a continuum of firms you do not literally
``count firms'': the empirical firm is the (variety $\times$ winning ZE) champion, and the
observable count is the pooled per-ZE supplier count of Section~\ref{sec:mapping}.

\subsection*{Question 2: At what geographic level should comparative advantage be defined?}
\label{sec:q3}

\textbf{At the attraction area $\times$ sector level --- the level of the regression's fixed
effect --- with a shared scale across the ZE it contains.} The argument is a chain of four
links.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \emph{The data only speak within areas.} The extensive-margin regression carries an
attraction area $\times$ sector fixed effect. Under the cloglog link, areas with no supplier
contribute \emph{exactly nothing} to $\hat\alpha$; under a LPM they contribute only a known
attenuation factor $\lambda$ \eqref{eq:attenuation}. Either way $\alpha$ is a
\emph{within-area} estimand (Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}).
\item \emph{So the model should not be asked for more.} Defining $T$ at the area level means
empty areas need no comparative-advantage parameter at all: they are structural zeros, exactly
as they are absorbed observations. The estimated block is $\{T_{s,a}\}_{a\in\Areas^{+}_{s}}$
(Section~\ref{sec:activeset-area}).
\item \emph{And within areas the model then says something sharp.} With $T$ constant and wages
normalized, the entire within-area extensive margin --- which ZE hosts a supplier and which
does not --- is governed by distance alone \eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}. That is
first-order, confound-free identification of $\alpha$, and it is what the empirical
specification is measuring. Indeed the model's implied specification \emph{is} the empirical
one: a cloglog on log distance with an area$\times$sector fixed effect and slope $-\theta\alpha$
(Proposition~\ref{prop:cloglog-exact}).
\item \emph{With two side benefits.} The $T$ block shrinks by the average number of ZE per
area, and --- because the share moments now outnumber the $T$ parameters --- profiling $T$ out
of the search no longer annihilates $\alpha$'s identification
(Section~\ref{sec:profiling}).
\end{enumerate}
The cost is the restriction itself: within-area heterogeneity other than distance (above all
\textbf{size}) must be either absent, or modeled with observed covariates and a common loading
\eqref{eq:T-area-cov}, or integrated out as a \emph{random} deviation
$T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}\nu_{sl}$ with dispersion $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ calibrated on the ZE-level shares
(Section~\ref{sec:eps}). The one thing that must \emph{not} be done is to make the deviation a
free parameter per ZE: that restores exact just-identification and annihilates $\alpha$, is
undefined at the empty ZE, and is biased there by selection on $\nu$
(Section~\ref{sec:eps-trap}). Test the restriction before imposing it
(Section~\ref{sec:risk}), and treat the estimated $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ as the formal measure of how
wrong it is.

\subsection*{Question 3: Should I change anything else?}

\textbf{Yes --- five consistent changes, summarized in Section~\ref{sec:q2}:}
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Price index.} Replace the continuum CES aggregate by the finite sum
\eqref{eq:price-index}, and normalize out the mechanical love-of-variety term so that $N_{s}$ is
identified from counts, not from price levels (Section~\ref{sec:q2}a).
\item \textbf{Value moments in expectation, aggregated at the area level.} Read the shares as
average realized shares (unchanged in expectation); treat the \emph{area aggregates} as the
just-identified targets and the ZE-level shares as over-identifying content; optionally use the
share dispersion as extra information on $N_{s}$ (Section~\ref{sec:q2}b).
\item \textbf{Active set: mask at the area tier, not the ZE tier.} Stop masking ZE-level
empirical zeros inside active areas --- they are now endogenous and are the core content of the
count moment and of the regression --- while keeping empty attraction areas out of the
parameter block entirely. Index $T$ by active $(s,\text{area})$ pairs and normalize a reference
area per sector (Section~\ref{sec:q2}c).
\item \textbf{Identification and estimation.} Add $N_{s}$ (one per sector) to the parameter
vector; add the count moment \eqref{eq:moment} over a few thresholds; recognize that $\alpha$
comes from the within-area distance gradient and $N_{s}$ from the zero \emph{level}, and compute
the extensive margin \emph{exactly by simulation} rather than by the FKG approximation
(Section~\ref{sec:q2}d).
\item \textbf{Geography inputs and profiling.} Make the ZE$\to$attraction-area map a model
primitive, identical to the regression's fixed-effect grouping, and re-express the $\gamma$
inversion on area aggregates \eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn} (Section~\ref{sec:q2}e).
\item \textbf{(Optional, recommended second stage) A random ZE-level deviation.} Relax
$T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}$ to $T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}\nu_{sl}\prod_{k}x_{kl}^{\eta_{k}}$ with
$\E[\nu]=1$, $\mathrm{Var}(\nu)=\sigma^{2}_{s}$ --- a mean-one Gamma making the supplier count
negative binomial \eqref{eq:negbin} --- and identify $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ from the within-area share
dispersion together with the count over-dispersion. Keep it a distribution, never a per-ZE
parameter (Section~\ref{sec:eps}).
\end{enumerate}
Everything else --- the downstream nested-CES block, the wage normalization, the Fr\'echet shape
$\theta$, the trade-cost parametrization $\tau=d^{\alpha}$, and the value-share and aggregate
moments in expectation --- carries over from the baseline without modification.

\section*{Implementation note}

In the SMM engine the changes are local and additive.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=\arabic*.]
\item \textbf{Parameter block.} Replace the active-$(s,\ZE)$ $T$ block by an active-$(s,a)$
block. The ZE-level $T$ used by the solver is then a \emph{gather} through the ZE$\to$area map,
$T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}$ (times the observed covariate factor \eqref{eq:T-area-cov} if used) ---
i.e. one extra indexing layer between the parameter vector and the model, leaving the solver
untouched. Active areas are those containing at least one status-$1$ ZE; status-$2$
(``control'') ZE inside an active area now \emph{inherit} $T_{s,a}$ instead of being assigned
$T\equiv0$, which is what makes their zeros endogenous.
\item \textbf{Draws.} Instead of approximating the continuum with a large draw count, draw
exactly $N_{s}$ varieties per sector (or $\Pois(N_{s})$ under Remark~\ref{rem:poisson}), run the
existing per-variety Ricardian selection, and (i) accumulate value shares as before (they still
average to the EK shares) and (ii) tally, per origin ZE, the number of varieties won across the
downstream industry to form the count moment \eqref{eq:moment}.
\item \textbf{Regression moment.} Unchanged in construction --- the same cloglog with the same
sector $\times$ nearest-downstream fixed effect and the same distance regressor, now run on a
simulated extensive margin that is a genuine granular zero/one outcome rather than an FKG
approximation. Whatever controls the empirical specification includes must be included here
too, and must appear in \eqref{eq:T-area-cov}.
\item \textbf{Profiling.} Re-express the $\gamma$ inversion on area aggregates
\eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn}: the update is one multiplicative step per active $(s,a)$ against the
aggregated target, with the within-area split recomputed from distance at the current $\alpha$.
The system stays square and its contraction structure is inherited from the ZE-level version.
\item \textbf{Analytical/GMM path.} A closed form survives: the per-market extensive margin is
the exact Binomial \eqref{eq:binomial} (exponential under Poissonization), and the
industry-wide count \eqref{eq:model-moment} is a Poisson-Binomial (a plain Poisson under
Poissonization) whose threshold probabilities are available in closed form in the single-market
case and by a low-dimensional integral over the champion draws otherwise. $N_{s}$ enters
smoothly through these expected counts, so no integer programming is required inside the
optimizer.
\item \textbf{Pre-flight check.} Before any of the above, run the within-area diagnostic of
Section~\ref{sec:risk}(2) on the observed shares. It costs nothing and it tells you whether
\eqref{eq:T-area} needs the covariate extension \eqref{eq:T-area-cov}.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Degrees of freedom under area-level comparative advantage}
\label{app:df}

This appendix collects the counting that makes Sections~\ref{sec:profiling} and
\ref{sec:risk} precise. Fix a sector $s$; let $A^{+}=|\Areas^{+}_{s}|$ be the number of active
attraction areas, $R_{a}$ the number of ZE in area $a$, and $R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}\le R_{a}$ the
number of those ZE with a strictly positive observed share.

\paragraph{Parameters.} The $T$ block has $A^{+}-1$ free elements after the reference
normalization $T_{s,\mathrm{ref}}=1$ (versus
$\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}}R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}-1$ under ZE-level comparative advantage). Adding the
common covariate loadings of \eqref{eq:T-area-cov} adds $\dim(\eta)$, a small number shared
across all sectors and areas.

\paragraph{Share moments.} The value block supplies $\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}}R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}$
share moments (minus one per sector for adding-up). Hence
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}}\big(R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}-1\big)}_{\text{over-identifying restrictions}}
=\underbrace{\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}}R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}}_{\text{share moments}}
-\underbrace{A^{+}}_{T\text{ parameters}} ,
\label{eq:app-df}
\end{equation}
i.e. $R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}-1$ testable restrictions per active area. Each restriction is a
within-area statement: given the area aggregate, the split of the area's sourcing across its ZE
must follow the distance profile \eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}. Under ZE-level $T$ the
right-hand side of \eqref{eq:app-df} is zero --- the source of the just-identification that
lets $T^{*}(\alpha)$ absorb any $\alpha$.

\paragraph{Profiling.} The inversion \eqref{eq:area-sinkhorn} imposes only the $A^{+}$ area
aggregates, matching the $A^{+}$ unknowns exactly. Writing the multiplicative update in the
Sinkhorn form used by the ZE-level inversion,
\begin{equation}
T^{+}_{s,a}
=T_{s,a}\cdot\Bigg(
\frac{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\hat\gamma_{ls}}
{\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\gamma_{ls}(T,\alpha)}\Bigg)^{\!\delta},
\qquad a\in\Areas^{+}_{s},
\label{eq:app-sinkhorn}
\end{equation}
the map has the same structure as before --- a positive, homogeneous-of-degree-one
multiplicative rescaling, ref-normalized per sector at each pass --- because
$\gamma_{ls}(T,\alpha)$ is linear in $T_{s,a}$ in the numerator and enters the common
denominator $\Phi_{sr}$ through the area aggregate only. Aggregating the update over the ZE of
an area \emph{cannot degrade} the contraction: it is the original map composed with a
projection onto a coarser partition, and the Birkhoff contraction coefficient of the coarsened
row-scaling is bounded by that of the original. The measured contraction rates of the ZE-level
inversion therefore transfer as upper bounds, and the endogenous-expenditure (GE) channel is
unchanged.

\paragraph{Extensive-margin moments.} The count moment \eqref{eq:moment} adds one family per
sector, and the regression \eqref{eq:cloglog} adds $N_{\mathrm{REG}}$ coefficients, against
one new parameter $N_{s}$ per sector: the extensive-margin block is over-identified, and its
Hansen $J$ is a genuine test of the granular mechanism.

\paragraph{With a random ZE-level deviation.} Relaxing Assumption~\ref{as:geo} as in
Section~\ref{sec:eps} adds $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ (one scalar per sector) and $\dim(\eta)$ loadings
shared across all sectors. The right-hand side of \eqref{eq:app-df} therefore falls by
\emph{one per sector}, not one per ZE:
\begin{equation}
\#\{\text{over-identifying restrictions}\}
=\sum_{a\in\Areas^{+}}\big(R^{\mathrm{act}}_{a}-1\big)-1-\tfrac{1}{S}\dim(\eta),
\end{equation}
and their content changes character: instead of pinning each ZE's share on the distance
profile, they pin the \emph{distribution} of within-area deviations around it. The count block
gains $\sigma^{2}_{s}$ as a second parameter but also gains the higher thresholds $n\ge1$
(the over-dispersion) as moments, so it stays over-identified. Two boundary cases bracket the
design: $\sigma^{2}_{s}=0$ is the pure Assumption~\ref{as:geo} of \eqref{eq:app-df}, while a free
$\varepsilon_{sl}$ per ZE drives the right-hand side to zero --- exactly the just-identification
that Section~\ref{sec:eps-trap} rejects.

\section{The granular dispersion of sourcing shares and the identification of $N_{s}$}
\label{app:dispersion}

This appendix answers the question: \emph{once the sourcing share is an average share, how does
its dispersion identify $N_{s}$?} The short answer is that the realized shares behave like a
multinomial draw whose \emph{effective sample size} is $N_{s}$, so their sampling variance is
$\propto 1/N_{s}$. A bootstrap over suppliers estimates exactly this variance and therefore pins
$N_{s}$ down. Nothing in this appendix depends on the tier at which $T$ is defined --- it is a
statement about the variety count, and it applies verbatim to ZE-level and to area-aggregated
shares (with the aggregated share of area $a$ inheriting the same $1/N_{s}$ scaling with
$\gamma$ replaced by $\gamma_{s,a}$).

\subsection{The realized value share as a self-normalized sum}

Fix a market $(r,s)$ with $N_{s}$ varieties. For variety $\rho$ let
$I_{\rho}\equiv\one\{\text{origin $l$ wins }\rho\}$ and let $v_{\rho}$ be the buyer's expenditure
on $\rho$. Under CES, expenditure on a variety is proportional to its (winning) price raised to
$1-\nu_{s}$,
\begin{equation}
v_{\rho}=\Big(\frac{p_{\rho}}{P_{sr}}\Big)^{1-\nu_{s}}E_{sr},
\qquad
p_{\rho}=\frac{c^{*}_{\rho}}{\mu},
\qquad
c^{*}_{\rho}=\min_{l'} c_{l'\rho s}(r),
\label{eq:app-value}
\end{equation}
with $E_{sr}$ total sector-$s$ spending. The \emph{realized} value share going to origin $l$ is the
self-normalized sum
\begin{equation}
\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs}
=\frac{\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}} v_{\rho}\,I_{\rho}}{\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}} v_{\rho}}
=\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}} \omega_{\rho}\,I_{\rho},
\qquad
\omega_{\rho}\equiv\frac{v_{\rho}}{\sum_{\rho'} v_{\rho'}},
\quad
\sum_{\rho}\omega_{\rho}=1.
\label{eq:app-realized}
\end{equation}

The crucial EK property is \emph{conditional independence}: the price at which a variety is bought
is distributed identically regardless of \emph{which} origin wins it (the min over origins of
independent Fr\'echet costs is independent of the argmin). Hence the value weights
$\{\omega_{\rho}\}$ are independent of the winner indicators $\{I_{\rho}\}$, and across varieties
$I_{\rho}\sim\text{Bernoulli}(\gamma_{lrs})$ i.i.d.

\subsection{Conditional mean and variance}

Condition on the weights $\bm{\omega}$. Because $\E[I_{\rho}]=\gamma_{lrs}$ and the $I_{\rho}$ are
i.i.d.\ and independent of $\bm{\omega}$,
\begin{align}
\E\big[\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs}\mid\bm{\omega}\big]
&=\gamma_{lrs}\sum_{\rho}\omega_{\rho}=\gamma_{lrs},
\label{eq:app-mean}\\[2pt]
\mathrm{Var}\big[\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs}\mid\bm{\omega}\big]
&=\gamma_{lrs}(1-\gamma_{lrs})\sum_{\rho}\omega_{\rho}^{2}
=\gamma_{lrs}(1-\gamma_{lrs})\,\mathcal{H},
\label{eq:app-condvar}
\end{align}
where $\mathcal{H}\equiv\sum_{\rho}\omega_{\rho}^{2}$ is the Herfindahl index of the value weights.
Equation \eqref{eq:app-mean} confirms $\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs}$ is unbiased for the EK probability
$\gamma_{lrs}$ (the ``average share'' statement of the main text); the identifying content is in
the variance \eqref{eq:app-condvar}.

\subsection{The Herfindahl scales as $1/N_{s}$}

For i.i.d.\ values, $\mathcal{H}=\big(\sum_{\rho} v_{\rho}^{2}\big)/\big(\sum_{\rho} v_{\rho}\big)^{2}$
concentrates, by the law of large numbers, around
\begin{equation}
\E[\mathcal{H}]\;\approx\;\frac{1}{N_{s}}\,\frac{\E[v^{2}]}{\E[v]^{2}}
\;=\;\frac{1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}}{N_{s}},
\qquad
\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}\equiv\frac{\mathrm{Var}(v)}{\E[v]^{2}}.
\label{eq:app-herf}
\end{equation}
Taking expectations of \eqref{eq:app-condvar} and using \eqref{eq:app-herf},
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\mathrm{Var}\big(\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs}\big)
\;\approx\;
\frac{\gamma_{lrs}(1-\gamma_{lrs})}{N_{s}}\,\big(1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}\big).
\;}
\label{eq:app-var}
\end{equation}
The dispersion of the realized share is inversely proportional to the number of varieties. This is
the mathematical content of ``a thinner sector has noisier shares'': granular sampling noise in the
sourcing shares is a direct, monotone read on $N_{s}$.

\subsection{The value-dispersion factor is a model constant}

The nuisance $1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}$ is not free: it is pinned down by the EK price distribution.
The minimum delivered cost is Weibull, $\Prob(c^{*}\le x)=1-\exp(-\Phi_{sr}x^{\theta})$
(Appendix~\ref{app:price}), whose power moments are
$\E[(c^{*})^{k}]=\Phi_{sr}^{-k/\theta}\,\Gamma(1+k/\theta)$ for $k>-\theta$. Since
$v\propto (c^{*})^{1-\nu_{s}}$ by \eqref{eq:app-value}, the $\Phi_{sr}$ factors cancel in the ratio
and
\begin{equation}
1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}
=\frac{\E[(c^{*})^{2(1-\nu_{s})}]}{\E[(c^{*})^{1-\nu_{s}}]^{2}}
=\frac{\Gamma\!\big(1+\tfrac{2(1-\nu_{s})}{\theta}\big)}
{\Gamma\!\big(1+\tfrac{1-\nu_{s}}{\theta}\big)^{2}},
\qquad\text{finite iff } \theta>2(\nu_{s}-1).
\label{eq:app-cv}
\end{equation}
Given the calibrated $(\theta,\nu_{s})$, \eqref{eq:app-cv} is a known constant (e.g.\ $\approx 1.03$
for $\theta=4,\nu_{s}=1.5$), so \eqref{eq:app-var} maps the observed share dispersion into $N_{s}$
without any additional free parameter. The regularity condition $\theta>2(\nu_{s}-1)$ is the usual
requirement that the CES price index have a finite second moment.

\subsection{Effective sample size and the full covariance}

Collecting origins, the winner vector $(I_{\rho 1},\dots,I_{\rho R})$ is multinomial$(1;\bm{\gamma})$
for each variety, so \eqref{eq:app-condvar} generalizes to
\begin{equation}
\mathrm{Cov}\big(\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs},\widehat{\gamma}_{l'rs}\big)
\;\approx\;
\frac{1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}}{N_{s}}\,
\big(\gamma_{lrs}\,\delta_{ll'}-\gamma_{lrs}\gamma_{l'rs}\big).
\label{eq:app-cov}
\end{equation}
This is exactly the covariance of a multinomial proportion vector with $\gamma$ probabilities and
\emph{effective sample size}
\begin{equation}
N_{s}^{\mathrm{eff}}=\frac{N_{s}}{1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}}.
\label{eq:app-ess}
\end{equation}
In words: \emph{the granular sourcing shares are distributed as a multinomial sample of size
$N_{s}^{\mathrm{eff}}$}. $N_{s}$ is the effective number of independent draws behind the sourcing
vector, and any dispersion statistic of the shares measures it. Aggregating to the area level
($\widehat{\gamma}_{s,a}=\sum_{l\in\mathcal{L}_{a}}\widehat{\gamma}_{lrs}$) simply collapses
\eqref{eq:app-cov} onto the coarser partition, with the same $1/N_{s}$ factor --- so the
area-aggregated targets of Section~\ref{sec:q2}b carry the same information about $N_{s}$, at
lower dispersion.

\subsection{Connection to the bootstrap and identification}

The proposed bootstrap --- resample the observed supplier links and recompute the shares ---
is an empirical estimator of precisely the sampling variance in \eqref{eq:app-var}: it perturbs the
finite set of granular units behind the share, so its dispersion reflects how many such units there
are. Equating the bootstrap variance to \eqref{eq:app-var},
\begin{equation}
\widehat{N}_{s}
=\big(1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2}\big)\cdot
\frac{\gamma_{ls}(1-\gamma_{ls})}{\widehat{\mathrm{Var}}_{\text{boot}}(\gamma_{ls})},
\label{eq:app-identify}
\end{equation}
delivers $N_{s}$ (or, pooling over $l$ and $r$, a GMM moment matching the two sides). Two cautions.
\emph{(i)} The resampling unit must be the same object in the model and the data: if the bootstrap
resamples firm-\emph{links} rather than varieties, the relevant count is the link count $N$, which
exceeds $N_{s}$ by up to the factor $\Nd$ (Section~\ref{sec:mapping}); use the model's
link-level variance, or resample at the variety level, to keep the units aligned. \emph{(ii)}
Equation \eqref{eq:app-var} is the leading $1/N_{s}$ term; for very thin sectors the exact
finite-$N_{s}$ Binomial/multinomial variance should be used, which the simulation delivers
automatically. Both cautions leave the core result intact: \emph{share dispersion is a
$1/N_{s}$ signal, and it is what makes $N_{s}$ identified even from the value block alone.}

\subsection{Two corrections once comparative advantage deviates within areas}
\label{app:disp-corrections}

Everything above holds \emph{unconditionally} and under Assumption~\ref{as:geo} taken exactly.
Two things change once one relaxes it as in Section~\ref{sec:eps}, and both cut against using
\eqref{eq:app-var} or \eqref{eq:app-identify} as plug-in formulas.

\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt,label=(\roman*)]
\item \textbf{Contamination.} Observed share dispersion now mixes granular sampling noise
($\propto1/N_{s}$) with genuine dispersion of comparative advantage
($\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon,s}$): in logs,
$\mathrm{Var}_{\text{within}}(\ln\hat\gamma)=\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon,s}+(1-\gamma)(1+\mathrm{CV}_{v}^{2})/(N_{s}\gamma)$,
which is \eqref{eq:eps-decomp}. Attributing all of it to $1/N_{s}$, as
\eqref{eq:app-identify} does, \emph{understates} $N_{s}$ --- by an unbounded factor if
$\sigma^{2}_{\varepsilon}$ is large. The two terms are distinguishable in principle, since only
the sampling term scales with $1/\gamma$.
\item \textbf{Truncation, which is worse.} The feasible sample contains only ZE with
$\hat\gamma_{ls}>0$, and that selection removes exactly the low draws carrying most of the
dispersion of $\ln\hat\gamma$. Table~\ref{tab:eps-mc} quantifies it: in the granular regime
($N_{s}\bar\gamma\lesssim10$, i.e.\ a non-negligible empty-ZE share --- the regime this note is
about) the additive decomposition is wrong by up to a factor of three, and the \emph{sign} of the
error flips with $N_{s}$. The truncation and the sampling inflation partly cancel, in a way that
is not signable a priori.
\end{enumerate}

Consequently \eqref{eq:app-var}--\eqref{eq:app-identify} should be read as the theory of why
share dispersion is informative, not as an estimator. In practice, match the dispersion moment
through the \emph{simulation}, which reproduces the sampling, the truncation, and the selection
of \eqref{eq:eps-selection} automatically, and identify $(N_{s},\sigma^{2}_{s})$ jointly from the
three moments listed in Section~\ref{sec:eps-calibration}.

\section{The expected within-sector price index, love of variety, and the normalization}
\label{app:price}

This appendix derives the price-index expectation quoted in Section~\ref{sec:q2}(a), shows that the
proposed normalization is internally consistent, and explains why the love-of-variety term it
removes is nonetheless economically meaningful. Throughout, $T$ may be read at either tier; under
Assumption~\ref{as:geo} substitute $T_{sl}=T_{s,a(l)}$ and note that the normalization acts on the
sector-common scale, hence identically at both tiers.

\subsection{The distribution of the minimum delivered cost}

For variety $\rho$ in market $(r,s)$, origin $l$'s champion cost is
$c_{l\rho s}(r)=w_{l}\tau_{lrs}/\varphi_{l\rho s}$ with
$\varphi_{l\rho s}\sim\Frechet(T_{sl},\theta)$, i.e.\ $\Prob(\varphi_{l\rho s}\le z)=\exp(-T_{sl}z^{-\theta})$.
Then
\begin{equation}
\Prob\big(c_{l\rho s}(r)>x\big)
=\Prob\!\Big(\varphi_{l\rho s}<\tfrac{w_{l}\tau_{lrs}}{x}\Big)
=\exp\!\big(-T_{sl}(w_{l}\tau_{lrs})^{-\theta}x^{\theta}\big),
\end{equation}
and, by independence across origins, the minimum cost $c^{*}_{\rho}=\min_{l}c_{l\rho s}(r)$ is
Weibull:
\begin{equation}
\Prob\big(c^{*}_{\rho}>x\big)=\prod_{l}\Prob\big(c_{l\rho s}(r)>x\big)
=\exp\!\big(-\Phi_{sr}\,x^{\theta}\big),
\qquad
\Phi_{sr}=\sum_{l}T_{sl}(w_{l}\tau_{lrs})^{-\theta}.
\label{eq:app-weibull}
\end{equation}
Its power moments follow from the substitution $u=\Phi_{sr}x^{\theta}$:
\begin{equation}
\E\big[(c^{*}_{\rho})^{k}\big]
=\int_{0}^{\infty}x^{k}\,\Phi_{sr}\theta x^{\theta-1}e^{-\Phi_{sr}x^{\theta}}\,dx
=\Phi_{sr}^{-k/\theta}\,\Gamma\!\Big(1+\tfrac{k}{\theta}\Big),
\qquad k>-\theta.
\label{eq:app-moment}
\end{equation}

\subsection{The expected price index}

The within-sector CES price index over the $N_{s}$ varieties is
$P_{sr}^{1-\nu_{s}}=\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}} p_{\rho}^{1-\nu_{s}}$ with $p_{\rho}=c^{*}_{\rho}/\mu$.
The $c^{*}_{\rho}$ are i.i.d.\ across $\rho$, so by \eqref{eq:app-moment} with $k=1-\nu_{s}$,
\begin{equation}
\E\big[P_{sr}^{1-\nu_{s}}\big]
=N_{s}\,\E\big[p_{\rho}^{1-\nu_{s}}\big]
=N_{s}\,\mu^{\nu_{s}-1}\,\E\big[(c^{*}_{\rho})^{1-\nu_{s}}\big]
=N_{s}\,\Gamma\!\Big(1+\tfrac{1-\nu_{s}}{\theta}\Big)\,\mu^{\nu_{s}-1}\,\Phi_{sr}^{-\frac{1-\nu_{s}}{\theta}},
\label{eq:app-EPindex}
\end{equation}
which is the expression in the main text. Defining the certainty-equivalent price index
$\widetilde{P}_{sr}\equiv\big(\E[P_{sr}^{1-\nu_{s}}]\big)^{1/(1-\nu_{s})}$,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{P}_{sr}
=N_{s}^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_{s}}}\,
\underbrace{\Gamma\!\Big(1+\tfrac{1-\nu_{s}}{\theta}\Big)^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_{s}}}\mu^{-1}\,
\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}}_{\text{continuum EK price, }N_{s}\text{-free}} .
\label{eq:app-loveofvariety}
\end{equation}
Because $1-\nu_{s}<0$, the factor $N_{s}^{1/(1-\nu_{s})}$ is \emph{decreasing} in $N_{s}$: more
varieties lower the sector price index. This is the standard CES love-of-variety channel, here
generated by the granular variety count rather than by entry.

\subsection{Why the normalization is consistent}

The normalization proposed in Section~\ref{sec:q2}(a) rescales each sector's comparative-advantage
vector by a common (across origins) factor,
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{T}_{s,a}=T_{s,a}\cdot N_{s}^{\,\kappa},
\qquad
\kappa=\frac{\theta}{1-\nu_{s}}\;(<0).
\label{eq:app-normalization}
\end{equation}
Under \eqref{eq:app-normalization}, $\Phi_{sr}\mapsto N_{s}^{\kappa}\Phi_{sr}$, so
$\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}\mapsto N_{s}^{-\kappa/\theta}\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}$, and the two $N_{s}$-powers
in \eqref{eq:app-loveofvariety} cancel exactly:
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{P}_{sr}\;\propto\;N_{s}^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_{s}}-\frac{\kappa}{\theta}}
=N_{s}^{0}=\text{const in }N_{s}.
\end{equation}
The normalization is \emph{consistent} for three reasons, each of which is exactly the property
that makes it harmless for everything except the price level.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{It acts on a gauge the sourcing shares do not see.} The share in
\eqref{eq:share-area} is a \emph{ratio} of $T$'s within a sector and is therefore invariant to any
common rescaling $T_{s,a}\mapsto N_{s}^{\kappa}T_{s,a}$. Hence the extensive margin
$(1-\gamma_{lrs})^{N_{s}}$, the count moment \eqref{eq:moment}, the within-area contrast
\eqref{eq:within-contrast-norm}, and the share-dispersion result of
Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion} are all \emph{unchanged} by \eqref{eq:app-normalization}. The
normalization moves only the price \emph{level}, never the sourcing geometry.
\item \textbf{The gauge is already free in the estimator.} The code fixes one reference region per
sector ($T_{s,\mathrm{ref}}=1$ --- one reference \emph{area} under Assumption~\ref{as:geo})
precisely because the within-sector scale of $T$ is not identified by the shares. The
love-of-variety factor $N_{s}^{1/(1-\nu_{s})}$ is exactly this unidentified scale
re-appearing in the price level; \eqref{eq:app-normalization} simply chooses the scale so that the
price level is held fixed as $N_{s}$ varies. Equivalently, one absorbs $N_{s}^{1/(1-\nu_{s})}$ into
the sector technology share $\Omega^{s}$ (or the productivity level $A$) that already carries the
sector price level in the value moments.
\item \textbf{It restores numerical equivalence with the baseline value block.} With
\eqref{eq:app-normalization}, every value-based moment (labor share, $\pi_{s}$, $\pi_{r}$) takes the
same value as in the continuum model at the same $(\Omega,A,\alpha,T\text{-normalized})$, so adding
granularity does not perturb the blocks that granularity has nothing to say about. $N_{s}$ is then
identified \emph{only} by the objects that genuinely depend on it --- the count moment and the share
dispersion.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Why the love-of-variety term is nonetheless economically relevant}

Consistency does \emph{not} mean the removed term is economically empty. Equation
\eqref{eq:app-loveofvariety} says a region-sector with more varieties has a genuinely lower price
index and higher real income: the elasticity
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \ln \widetilde{P}_{sr}}{\partial \ln N_{s}}=\frac{1}{1-\nu_{s}}<0
\label{eq:app-welfare}
\end{equation}
is the marginal gain from variety, a real welfare object of the same nature as in
Krugman/Melitz/ACR. Three implications follow.
\begin{enumerate}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{The cross-section cannot separate $N_{s}$ from technology using prices alone.}
Because \eqref{eq:app-normalization} is observationally equivalent in the value block to a shift in
$T$-scale (equivalently $\Omega^{s}$/$A$), a sector that is cheap because it has many varieties looks
identical, in expenditure data, to one that is cheap because it is technologically productive. This
observational equivalence is \emph{why} the granular count/dispersion moments are indispensable:
they are the only data that break it. The normalization does not destroy information --- it records
that the information about $N_{s}$ was never in the value levels to begin with.
\item \textbf{It is a level normalization, not a slope restriction.} \eqref{eq:app-normalization}
fixes the price \emph{level} at the calibration point; it does not alter the counterfactual
\emph{response} \eqref{eq:app-welfare} of prices to a change in the variety set. Provided one maps
back to the physical primitive $T$ (undoing \eqref{eq:app-normalization}) before running any
counterfactual that moves $N_{s}$, the love-of-variety channel is fully preserved. Holding
$\widetilde{T}$ (rather than $T$) fixed in such a counterfactual would silence it --- a modeling
error to avoid.
\item \textbf{It is a live propagation channel for spatial comovement.} The paper's object of
interest is how a shock in one place propagates elsewhere through sourcing. A demand or supply shock
that removes suppliers/varieties in one region raises $P_{sr}$ downstream through exactly
\eqref{eq:app-welfare}, i.e.\ through the granular extensive margin. This channel is absent in the
continuum model (where the variety mass is fixed and the extensive margin is degenerate) and is
precisely the new content of the granular model. Normalizing the term out is therefore appropriate
for \emph{identification} but the term itself must be retained --- as a function of the endogenous,
shock-dependent number of active varieties --- in any \emph{counterfactual}.
\end{enumerate}
In sum, the normalization is a change of units that isolates $N_{s}$'s identification in the count
and dispersion moments; the love-of-variety term it neutralizes in the cross-section is a genuine
welfare and comovement channel that reappears the moment the variety set is allowed to move.

\section{Choosing the count thresholds and bootstrapping the extensive-margin moment}
\label{app:estimation}

The count moment \eqref{eq:moment} adds one family of moments per sector,
$G_{s}(K)=\#\{l\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}:K_{ls}\le K\}$ (equivalently, its share form
$\bar G_{s}(K)=G_{s}(K)/R^{+}_{s}$ with $R^{+}_{s}=|\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}|$). This appendix settles
two practical questions: which thresholds $K$ to match, and how to bootstrap their covariance.

\subsection{Which thresholds identify $N_{s}$}
\label{app:threshold}

Write the model share as $\bar G_{s}(K;N_{s})=\tfrac{1}{R^{+}_{s}}\sum_{l}F(K;N_{s},q_{ls})$, where
$q_{ls}$ is origin $l$'s per-variety win probability (pinned by the value block, independent of
$N_{s}$) and $F$ is the count CDF. Using the Poisson form
$K_{ls}\sim\Pois(\mu_{ls})$ with $\mu_{ls}=N_{s}q_{ls}$ (exact under
Remark~\ref{rem:poisson}; otherwise a Poisson-Binomial evaluated by simulation), the sensitivity of
the moment is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \bar G_{s}(K)}{\partial N_{s}}
=-\frac{1}{R^{+}_{s}}\sum_{l}q_{ls}\,\frac{e^{-\mu_{ls}}\mu_{ls}^{K}}{K!},
\label{eq:app-sensitivity}
\end{equation}
which, region by region, peaks at $K\approx\mu_{ls}=N_{s}q_{ls}$ and decays on either side. Two
consequences:
\begin{itemize}[nosep,topsep=2pt]
\item \textbf{Large $K$ saturates.} $\bar G_{s}(K)\to 1$ and \eqref{eq:app-sensitivity} $\to 0$:
no identifying variation. A share pinned near $1$ also has a small, poorly-estimated variance, so
the optimal weight matrix loads it with a large, unstable weight. Such thresholds must be trimmed.
\item \textbf{The signal is in the left tail.} The informative thresholds are those near where the
marginal (remote/small) origins flip from inactive to active. The empty-ZE share
$\bar G_{s}(0)$ is the most interpretable and typically near-optimal moment --- it \emph{is} the
extensive margin the model was built to explain.
\end{itemize}
Formally, the threshold that carries the most information about $N_{s}$ maximizes the
signal-to-noise (Fisher-information) ratio
\begin{equation}
K^{\star}_{s}=\argmax_{K}\ \mathcal{I}_{s}(K),
\qquad
\mathcal{I}_{s}(K)=\frac{\big[\partial_{N_{s}}\bar G_{s}(K)\big]^{2}}{\mathrm{Var}\big(\hat G_{s}(K)\big)},
\label{eq:app-info}
\end{equation}
with the denominator taken from the bootstrap of Section~\ref{app:bootstrap}. $\mathcal{I}_{s}(K)$
is maximized at an \emph{interior} share, roughly $\bar G_{s}(K)\in[0.1,0.9]$, away from the
degenerate $0$ and the saturated $1$. Because $N_{s}$ --- and hence the saturation point ---
differs across sectors, $K$ should be chosen \emph{per sector} (e.g.\ at a fixed low quantile of
the sector's empirical count distribution, or simply $K=0$). One well-chosen $K$ just-identifies
$N_{s}$; a short ladder $K\in\{0,1,2\}$ over-identifies it and supplies a Hansen-style check that
the whole granular curve fits (in the spirit of LMM's several $h(M)/h(1)$ ratios), while the
optimal weight matrix combines them --- provided saturated thresholds are dropped so the covariance
stays well-conditioned.

\subsection{How to bootstrap the moment}
\label{app:bootstrap}

The count moment is a proportion over the geography \emph{of active attraction areas}, empty ZE
included, since those empty ZE are the $K=0$ mass. This dictates the resampling design.

\paragraph{Denominator: all candidate ZE inside active areas.} Define
$\hat G_{s}(K)=\tfrac{1}{R^{+}_{s}}\#\{l\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}:\hat K_{ls}\le K\}$ over
$\mathcal{L}^{+}_{s}=\bigsqcup_{a\in\Areas^{+}_{s}}\mathcal{L}_{a}$, with $R^{+}_{s}$ held
\emph{fixed}. Two errors to avoid, in opposite directions. Restricting the computation to ZE that
host a supplier (as is appropriate for the value-share block) computes a different object --- a
share \emph{among} active ZE --- whose variance is not the variance of the extensive margin: the
empty ZE must enter the denominator. Conversely, including ZE from \emph{empty} attraction areas
inflates the denominator with mechanically-explained zeros that carry no information about
$(\alpha,N_{s})$ and that the regression's fixed effect has already discarded
(Lemma~\ref{lem:fe}); they must stay out. The correct denominator is exactly the support the
cloglog uses.

\paragraph{Resampling unit: supplier firm-links, not the control group.} Bootstrap with
replacement from the observed supplier firms (pooled over the geography of active areas),
recompute the per-ZE counts $\hat K_{ls}^{*}$ and $\hat G_{s}(K)^{*}$ each replication, holding
$R^{+}_{s}$ fixed. A ZE with one or two firms can then draw zero and \emph{flip into the empty set}
--- precisely the extensive-margin variance required --- and this arises entirely from resampling
\emph{suppliers}. Under a firm-to-firm dataset that is the \emph{universe} of the downstream
industry's suppliers (as in LMM's universe of French exporters), an observed-zero ZE is a true
zero: it stays zero in every replication and contributes deterministically to $\hat G_{s}(0)$.
Adding non-supplier (``control-group'') firms as resampling units would then inject variance
corresponding to no real sampling process and should be avoided. Non-suppliers enter only under a
different noise source: if the data are a \emph{sample} of the firm universe (resample the whole
observed firm list), or if the moment's uncertainty is about which downstream buyers are observed
(resample \emph{buyers}, so a ZE's count changes as different buyers reveal different suppliers).
Neither applies to the structural granular interpretation with a fixed universe. Note that the
\emph{composition} of $\Areas^{+}_{s}$ is itself an outcome of the same sampling: if an area's only
supplier can be resampled away, the area would leave $\Areas^{+}_{s}$ and change the support. Holding
$\Areas^{+}_{s}$ fixed at its observed value --- which is what the fixed-effect estimator does --- is
the coherent convention, and should be applied identically in the bootstrap.

\paragraph{Bootstrap jointly with the value block.} The same supplier firms generate both the
value shares and the counts $K_{ls}$, so a single joint bootstrap --- resample firms
once, recompute \emph{both} moment families per replication --- is needed to estimate the
cross-covariance blocks of $\Sigma_{\text{data}}$ that the efficient weight matrix
$W=(\Sigma_{\text{data}}+\Sigma_{\text{sim}})^{-1}$ requires. The simulation-noise term
$\Sigma_{\text{sim}}$ continues to come from re-simulation as in the baseline. In particular, the
value-share block may keep the existing supplier-only, active-ZE bootstrap (its object is
conditional on activity; cf.\ Appendix~\ref{app:dispersion}), while the count block uses the
active-area denominator above --- the two are drawn in the same resampling loop. If the
area-aggregated shares of Section~\ref{sec:q2}b are used as targets, their covariance comes out of
the same loop by summing within areas before recomputing.
