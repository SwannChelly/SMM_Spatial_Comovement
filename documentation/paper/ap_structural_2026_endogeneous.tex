

In this appendix, we derive the model, give details on the structural estimation and inference. 

\subsection{Derivation}

\label{apsec:structural}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The economy is divided into $R+1$ regions, populated by two types of firms: producers of a final good and producers of intermediate goods used as inputs in the production of the final good. The $R+1^{\text{th}}$ region represents the rest of the world. The analysis primarily focuses on the matching of firms across domestic regions, controlling for competition from foreign firms using their shares in total sales as reference. We assume that each domestic region hosts at most one representative firm in the downstream sector, identified by its region of origin. Intermediate goods are produced across $S$ sectors, with each region potentially hosting many suppliers in each sector. We define a market $(s,r)$ as the collection of firms located in region $r$ that belong to upstream sector $s$.\\

Domestic regions are partitioned into attraction areas,
$\mathcal{R}=\bigsqcup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{R}_{a}$, where $\mathcal{R}_{a}$ is the set of
regions whose nearest downstream producer is the anchor of area $a$, and $a(r')$ denotes the
area of region $r'$.


\paragraph{Demand for final manufacturing products.} Firms in the downstream sector each own the blueprint to produce a unique differentiated variety of the final good. The market for the final good is characterized by monopolistic competition and iso-elastic demand functions:
$$q_r=\left(\frac{p_r}{P}\right)^{\varepsilon-1}\frac{E}{P}\delta_r$$
where $q_r$ denotes the quantity demanded of the variety produced in region $r$ at price $p_r$, given nominal consumption $E$ and the downstream sector's price index $P$. $\varepsilon$ measures the price elasticity of sales. Finally, $\delta_r$ is an exogenous component of demand, that varies across downstream suppliers and will later be used to simulate demand shocks and their diffusion along production linkages. To simplify the analysis, we abstract from foreign demand and assume that the domestic market for final consumption goods is perfectly integrated. 



\paragraph{Technology in the downstream sector.} Production of final goods requires the assembly of intermediates, combined with labor, under a nested CES structure:
\begin{equation}
y_{r}=A_{r}\Big[\chi_{L}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\ell_{r}^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}}
+(1-\chi_{L})^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\big(q^{I}_{r}\big)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}}\Big]^{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}},
\end{equation}
where $\ell_{r}$ is labor demand and $q^{I}_{r}$ the firm's intermediate input consumption. The parameter $\chi_{L}$ governs the share of labor in total production costs, while $\lambda$ is the elasticity of substitution between labor and intermediates. Firm productivity $A_{r}$ is exogenous. Intermediate consumption is itself a nested CES aggregator across sectors and, within each sector, across varieties:

\begin{equation}
q^I_r = \Big[\sum_{s=1}^S \chi_s^{\frac{1}{\nu}}\, q_{rs}^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\Big]^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}},
\qquad
q_{rs} = N_s^{-\frac{1}{\nu_s-1}}
\Big[\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_s} q_{rs}(\rho)^{\frac{\nu_s-1}{\nu_s}}\Big]^{\frac{\nu_s}{\nu_s-1}},
\label{eq:app-ces}
\end{equation}

where $\chi_{s}$ governs the share of sector $s$ in intermediate consumption, $\nu$ is the elasticity of substitution across sectors and $\nu_{s}$ that across varieties within a sector.

In \cite{EatonKortum2002}, each sector is a continuum of varieties of unit mass. Here, it contains $N_{s}$ varieties. This is the only change relative to the continuum formulation. The factor $N_{s}^{-1/(\nu_{s}-1)}$ makes the associated price index an average of $p_{rs}(\rho)^{1-\nu_{s}}$ over varieties, the discrete counterpart of the Eaton--Kortum integral. It removes the love-of-variety effect, so that the price level does not depend mechanically on $N_{s}$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:equilibrium}). Because the factor is common to all varieties, it leaves every sourcing share, and hence the entire extensive margin, unchanged.

\paragraph{Technology in upstream sectors.} Intermediate goods are produced with labor using a constant-returns-to-scale technology. Wages in sector $s$ and region $r'$ are denoted $w_{r's}$ and taken as given. As in \cite{EatonKortum2002} we assume that varieties produced within and across regions are perfect substitutes and that productivity draws are independent. The departure from the continuum case is that each sector contains $N_{s}\in\mathbb{N}$ horizontally differentiated varieties $\rho=1,\dots,N_{s}$. Within each variety $\rho$ and each region $r'$ there is a continuum of firms whose productivities above $z$ form a Poisson process with mean $T_{r's}z^{-\theta}$. Draws are independent across varieties, regions and sectors.\\

The continuum of firms is what keeps the model tractable. The number of firms above $z$ in $(r',s,\rho)$ is Poisson with mean $T_{r's}z^{-\theta}$, so the most productive one satisfies 

\begin{equation}
\Pr\big(z_{r'\rho s}\le z\big)=\Pr(\text{no firm above }z)=e^{-T_{r's}z^{-\theta}},
\label{eq:app-champion}
\end{equation}

that is, $z_{r'\rho s}$ is exactly $\mathrm{Fréchet}(T_{r's},\theta)$. One Fréchet draw per (region, variety) pair is the champion of a continuum of firms. \\

In the baseline specification, comparative advantage is area-specific up to the density of firms:
\begin{equation}
T_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}\,n_{r's}^{\kappa}, \qquad r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a},
\label{eq:app-density}
\end{equation}
with one parameter $T_{as}$ per (sector, area), $n_{r's}$ the number of firms of sector $s$ observed in region $r'$ in 2019, held fixed, and $\kappa$ a density elasticity common to all sectors. $T_{as}$ is a productivity per firm, net of density; with $\kappa=1$ we recover the \cite{EatonKortum2002} reading in which $T$ counts potential producers, and a region without firm of the sector ($n_{r's}=0$) has $T_{r's}=0$. The density term matters because potential suppliers are concentrated around assembly sites: within an area, $n_{r's}$ is roughly halved between 0--50 km and beyond 200 km in both industries. A model with $T_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}$ would read this decline as a trade cost and overstate $\alpha$ --- with $n\propto d^{\delta}$ it would read a gradient $d^{-(\theta\alpha-\kappa\delta)}$ instead of $d^{-\theta\alpha}$ --- which in turn overstates the share of a shock retained near the shocked site and understates the share going to dense upstream areas, wherever the site is.

Area-specific comparative advantage does not make regions within an area interchangeable. Each runs its own Ricardian competition for each variety, and two regions at different distances from a buyer, or with different firm densities, have different win probabilities. What it removes is unobserved technological heterogeneity within an area, which is what allows the model to explain the same variation the extensive-margin regression uses: within an area, distance identifies $\alpha$ and density identifies $\kappa$.

\paragraph{Foreign suppliers.}\label{app:model-foreign-setup} The rest of the world, region $R+1$, competes for every variety of sector $s$ through one foreign champion, drawn from a Fréchet distribution of scale $\Phi^F_s$ and shape $\theta$. We observe neither foreign wages nor the location of foreign plants, so the foreign delivered cost has no wage or distance component of its own: everything that makes foreign inputs cheap or expensive for French buyers is absorbed in $\Phi^F_s$. It follows that the foreign bid for a variety is the same for every buyer,
\begin{equation}
p^F_{\rho s}=\big(z^F_{\rho s}\big)^{-1},\qquad \Pr\big(z^F_{\rho s}\le z\big)=e^{-\Phi^F_sz^{-\theta}},
\label{eq:app-foreign-bid}
\end{equation}
and that a buyer imports the variety whenever this bid is strictly below the best French delivered cost. Imported varieties enter the buyer's price index and its spending, but we have no balance-sheet information on foreign suppliers, so they create no firm-level linkage. In a sector without imports in the input--output table, $\Phi^F_s=0$ and the model reduces to its domestic version.


\paragraph{The network draw.}\label{app:network-draw}

Because the number of varieties is finite, the network is random, unlike in \cite{EatonKortum2002}. With a continuum of varieties, the law of large numbers makes the realized network deterministic. With $N_{s}$ varieties, it does not. A realization of the network, denoted $\Omega$, is one draw of the champion productivities $\{z_{r'\rho s}\}$ for every region, variety and sector. It determines, for each buyer and each variety, which region wins the variety and at which price. Two draws of $\Omega$ select different winners, and therefore different networks. We write $\mathbb{E}_{\Omega}$ for the expectation over realizations. The expectation of a realized object is its value in the expected network.
 
We use the following convention. Probabilities, and objects evaluated at the expected price index, do not depend on $\Omega$: they are the \cite{EatonKortum2002} expressions, and we write them as usual. Sector-level objects that depend on which supplier wins each variety carry the argument $(\Omega)$. Variety-level objects, indexed by $\rho$, are realizations by nature and are not marked. Table~\ref{tab:app-draw} summarizes the classification. 

One consequence of the introduction of a fixed number of varieties is that statistics that are linear in the flows, such as area sourcing shares or the average sourcing distance, take the same value in the expected network and on average across realizations. Nonlinear statistics, such as concentration indices, do not: they depend on the dispersion of realizations around the expected network.


\paragraph{Equilibrium.}\label{app:equilibrium}
The resolution follows the same steps as in \cite{EatonKortum2002}, applied variety by variety. Given the Fréchet assumption, the minimum price at which suppliers from $(r',s)$ can serve market $r$ with a given variety is distributed according to
 
\begin{equation}
G_{r'rs}(p)=\Pr\Big(\frac{w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs}}{z_{r'\rho s}}\le p\Big)
=1-\exp\left\{-T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}p^\theta\right\}.
\end{equation}
 
Taking the minimum across potential supplier locations yields the distribution of the price at which a downstream firm in region $r$ buys a given variety:
\begin{equation}
G_{rs}(p) =1-\prod_{r'=1}^{R+1}\left[1-G_{r'rs}(p)\right] =1-\exp\left\{-\Phi_{rs}p^\theta\right\},
\end{equation}
 
where
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{rs} = \sum_{r'=1}^{R}T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}+\Phi^F_s = \underbrace{\sum_{a'}T_{a's} \sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a'}} n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}}_{\equiv\,\Phi^{dom}_{rs}}+\Phi^F_s.
\label{eq:app-Phi}
\end{equation}

The probability that a downstream firm in region $r$ sources a given variety from market $(r',s)$, and the probability that it imports it, are therefore
\begin{equation}
p_{r'rs} = \frac{ T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta} }{ \Phi_{rs} },
\qquad
f_{rs}=\frac{\Phi^F_s}{\Phi_{rs}}=1-\sum_{r'=1}^{R}p_{r'rs}.
\label{eq:app-share}
\end{equation}
Because the foreign competitor is common to all buyers, it scales every domestic probability of buyer $r$ by the same factor $1-f_{rs}$: the geography of domestic sourcing \emph{conditional on buying in France}, $p_{r'rs}/(1-f_{rs})=T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}/\Phi^{dom}_{rs}$, does not depend on $\Phi^F_s$. What $\Phi^F_s$ moves is the level of the price index and the leakage of each buyer's demand abroad, which is larger for buyers far from French suppliers ($\Phi^{dom}_{rs}$ small).
 
In the continuum, or the \cite{EatonKortum2002} model, such probability is also the sourcing share $\gamma_{r'rs} = \frac{x_{r'rs}}{x_{rs}}$ with $x_{r'rs}$ the total purchase of $r$ for goods of sector $s$ sold by the champion in $r'$ and $x_{rs}$ the total purchase of $r$ for goods of sector $s$. These are probabilities over the draw $\Omega$ and they do not depend on its realization, nor on $N_s$: each variety runs its own Ricardian competition, with the same odds. \\

In \cite{EatonKortum2002}, the sourcing probability coincides with the sourcing share. With a finite number of varieties, the two coincide only in expectation. For a given realization, each variety is won by a single region. The share of buyer $r$'s sector-$s$ spending going to region $r'$ is therefore a random variable:
\begin{equation}
\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)=\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_s} v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)\,\mathbf{1}\{r' \text{ wins } \rho \text{ for } r \mid \Omega \},
\label{eq:app-omega}
\end{equation}
where $v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)$ is the share of buyer $r$'s sector-$s$ spending allocated to variety $\rho$: $v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)=\frac{p_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}}{\sum_{\rho'=1}^{N_s} p_{rs}(\rho',\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}}$. 

Under the Fréchet assumption, the distribution of the winning price does not depend on the identity of the winner, $G_{r'rs}(p)=G_{rs}(p)$. Within a variety, the winner is therefore independent of the winning price. Draws are also independent across varieties. Hence the indicators in \eqref{eq:app-omega} are independent of the spending shares $v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)$. The weights $v_{rs}$ depend only on prices, the indicators only on winners, and $\sum_\rho v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)=1$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)\big]=\gamma_{r'rs},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\Omega\big(\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)\big)=V_s\,\gamma_{r'rs}(1-\gamma_{r'rs}),
\qquad
V_s=\mathbb{E}_\Omega\Big[\sum_\rho v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)^2\Big]\ge \frac{1}{N_s}.
\label{eq:app-expected-share}
\end{equation}
The sourcing share $\gamma_{r'rs}$ thus describes the expected network, for any $N_s$. The variance measures how far a realization departs from it. It vanishes as $N_s\to\infty$, where realized and expected networks coincide and the model collapses to \cite{EatonKortum2002}.


For a given realization, the sectoral CES price index
\begin{equation*}
p_{rs}(\Omega)=\Big[\frac{1}{N_s}\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_s} p_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_s}}
\end{equation*}
is random. In the spirit of \citet{lenoir2023search}, we assume that sector-level decisions are taken before the realization is known, on the expected index \swann{Ici c'est important d'avoir en tête que dans le papier avec Clémence l'espérance est utilisée pour l'aléa correspondant au tirage des fournisseurs potentiels. Là $\Omega$ est le tirage des productivités (on tire un nombre fini d'entreprises)}. Within the sector, spending is then allocated across varieties at realized prices, through $v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)$. The model thus has two layers. The upper layer (sector spending, price indices, output) is deterministic. The lower layer (which region supplies which variety) is random. The sectoral index is the \cite{EatonKortum2002} price index:
\begin{equation}
p_{rs} \equiv \Big[\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[p_{rs}(\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}\big]\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_s}} = \Bigg[ \Gamma\Big(\frac{\theta+1-\nu_s}{\theta}\Big) \Bigg]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_s}} \Phi_{rs}^{-1/\theta}.
\label{eq:app-pindex}
\end{equation}
Because the within-sector index is an average, $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[p_{rs}(\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}]$ does not depend on $N_s$\footnote{\swann{Pas utile: The finite number of varieties affects the level of the CES price index through a standard love-of-variety term. Writing the within-sector aggregator as an average normalizes this term to one. Equivalently, this amounts to rescaling the technology parameter according to 
\begin{equation}

\end{equation}

The remaining price indices are
 
\begin{equation}
p_{r} = \Big[\sum_s\chi_s p_{rs}^{1-\nu}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}, \qquad c_r = \Big[\chi_Lw_r^{1-\lambda} +(1-\chi_L)p_r^{1-\lambda}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\lambda}} .
\end{equation}
 
Finally, realized bilateral intermediate input purchases are $X_{r'rs}(\Omega)=\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)\,x_{rs}$. Their expectation is the \cite{EatonKortum2002} flow:
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:input_flows}
X_{r'rs} \equiv \mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[X_{r'rs}(\Omega)\big] = \gamma_{r'rs}x_{rs} = \gamma_{r'rs} \chi_s(1-\chi_L) \left(\frac{p_{rs}}{p_r}\right)^{1-\nu} \left(\frac{p_r}{c_r}\right)^{1-\lambda} \frac{c_ry_r}{A_r}.
\end{equation}
 
The expected flows describe the expected network. 
 
 

 
\paragraph{Granularity and the count moment.}\label{app:cloglog-count} The finite number of varieties $N_s$ allows us to endogenize the occurrence of regions with no suppliers. Let $p_{r's}$ be the probability that region $r'$ wins a given variety of sector $s$ for at least one downstream buyer:
\begin{equation}
p_{r's}\equiv\Pr\Big(\bigcup_{r}\{r' \text{ wins } \rho \text{ for } r\}\Big).
\label{eq:app-pwin}
\end{equation}
Like $p_{r'rs}$, it does not depend on $\Omega$. It has no tractable closed form (see Section~\ref{app:model-moments}). The win events of $r'$ across buyers share the same champion draw, so they are positively dependent and $p_{r's}\le 1-\prod_r(1-\gamma_{r'rs})$. We evaluate it by simulation.
 
The number of firms in $(r',s)$ that supply the downstream industry is the number of varieties that $r'$ wins for at least one buyer. It depends on the draw. Varieties are independent, so
\begin{equation}
K_{r's}(\Omega)\sim\mathrm{Bin}\big(N_s,p_{r's}\big),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\Pr\big(K_{r's}(\Omega)>0\big)=1-(1-p_{r's})^{N_s}.
\label{eq:app-K}
\end{equation}
This is the probability that enters the cell-level regression \eqref{eq:app-cell-cloglog}.

Considering $\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}$ as the set of regions within active attraction areas, the empirical moment is the share of those regions hosting no suppliers, $\bar G_{s}(0)(\Omega)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}|}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}}\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}(\Omega)=0\}$. The data provide one realization. Its expectation is\footnote{Let $Z_{r'}(\rho,\Omega)=\prod_r\big(1-\mathbf{1}\{r' \text{ wins } \rho \text{ for } r \mid \Omega\}\big)$. It equals one if region $r'$ wins variety $\rho$ for no buyer. Region $r'$ hosts no supplier if and only if this holds for every variety, so that $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}(\Omega)=0\}=\prod_{\rho=1}^{N_s} Z_{r'}(\rho,\Omega)$. Then
\begin{align*}
\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[\bar G_{s}(0)(\Omega)\big]
&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}|}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}}\mathbb{E}_\Omega\Big[\prod_{\rho=1}^{N_s} Z_{r'}(\rho,\Omega)\Big]
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}|}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}}\prod_{\rho=1}^{N_s}\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[Z_{r'}(\rho,\Omega)\big] \\
&=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}|}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}}(1-p_{r's})^{N_{s}}.
\end{align*}
The first equality uses the linearity of the expectation. The second uses the independence of productivity draws across varieties. The third uses the symmetry of varieties: $T_{r's}$ and $\tau_{r'rs}$ do not depend on $\rho$, so $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[Z_{r'}(\rho,\Omega)]=1-p_{r's}$ for every $\rho$. The correlation across buyers, which arises because the same champion can serve several buyers, is contained in $p_{r's}$. Hence $p_{r's}\neq 1-\prod_r(1-\gamma_{r'rs})$ in general.}
\begin{equation}

\label{eq:app-count}
\end{equation}
 
At fixed $p_{r's}$, this moment is decreasing in $N_s$, which allows for the identification of the variety count. \\
 
Furthermore, because iceberg trade costs do not alter the relative ranking of firms within a region, each variety generates exactly one distinct supplying firm per winning origin. If $N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s}$ is the number of distinct upstream firms observed to supply the downstream industry, then $N_{s}$ must fall within the range defined by:
\begin{equation}
\frac{N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s}}{R^{D}q_s}\;\le\;N_{s}\;\le\;\frac{N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s}}{q_s},
\label{eq:app-bounds}
\end{equation}
where $R^{D}$ is the number of downstream buyers and $q_s$ the probability that a variety is won by a French region for at least one buyer ($q_s=1$ without imports). Only the $N_sq_s$ varieties won in France can generate an observed supplier. The upper bound is reached when every such variety is sourced from a single French origin, while the lower bound occurs when each of the $R^{D}$ buyers sources it from a different origin. Like $p_{r's}$, $q_s$ is a probability of the expected network, evaluated on the simulated varieties before $N_s$ is chosen, so the bounds involve no circularity. \paragraph{The extensive margin of trade.}\label{app:cloglog} To estimate the elasticity of trade cost to the distance we use the extensive margin of trade. Taking the perspective of the supplier, the probability of being the lowest-cost supplier in market $(r,s)$ for a given variety, given location $r'$ and productivity $z$, is
\begin{equation}
\rho_{r'rs}(z)=e^{-\Phi_{rs}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{\theta}z^{-\theta}}
=e^{-T_{r's}\gamma^{-1}_{r'rs}z^{-\theta}} .
\label{eq:app-rho}
\end{equation}
 
Like $\gamma_{r'rs}$, this is a probability over the competitors' draws. It depends neither on the realization $\Omega$ nor on $N_s$.
 
In the limit where there is only one downstream region ($r = a(r')$), the elasticity of trade cost to the distance can be recovered directly from the supply dummy. Indeed, Equation~\eqref{eq:app-rho} implies that $\Pr(\text{no supply} \mid z) = 1 - \exp(-\exp(\eta))$ where $\eta = \ln \Phi_{a(r')s} + \theta \ln w_{r's} + \theta \alpha \ln d_{r'a(r')} - \theta \ln z$. Consequently, the coefficient on the distance of a complementary log log design is the product $\alpha\theta$:
 
$$
\Pr(\text{no supply} \mid z) = 1 - \exp(-\exp(FE_{a(r')s} + \theta \alpha \ln d_{r'a(r')} - \theta \ln z))
$$

This firm-level regression has no exact model counterpart. In the data, a firm supplies if it is the most productive firm of its variety in its region \emph{and} if it wins the variety; the model only describes the first firm, the champion of \eqref{eq:app-champion}, and the probability of being that champion, which depends on the density of the region, has no equivalent in it. We therefore target its cell-level analogue: one observation per (region, sector) cell, and the outcome $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$, ``region $r'$ hosts at least one supplier'', which is defined identically in the data and in the model. Section~\ref{app:cloglog-count} below shows that $\Pr(K_{r's}>0)=1-(1-p_{r's})^{N_s}$. With a single buyer, $p_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}d_{r'a}^{\alpha})^{-\theta}/\Phi_{as}$, and the cloglog delivers a decomposition linear in $\ln d$ and $\ln n$:
\begin{equation}
\ln\big(-\ln \Pr(K_{r's}=0)\big)=\ln N_s+\ln\big(-\ln(1-p_{r's})\big)\simeq FE_{a(r')s}+\kappa\ln n_{r's}-\theta\alpha\ln d_{r'a(r')},
\label{eq:app-cell-cloglog}
\end{equation}
where the approximation $-\ln(1-p)\simeq p$ is very good because $p_{r's}$ is small. The sector$\times$area fixed effect absorbs $T_{a(r')s}$, $\Phi_{as}$ and $\ln N_s$, so in population the coefficients do not depend on the variety count --- a property of the cloglog link that a logit or a linear probability model would not have.\footnote{We prefer the cloglog on presence to a PPML on the number of suppliers. The model counts varieties won, the data count firms, and several firms of a dense region can supply the same variety. The number of suppliers absorbs this multiplicity; presence does not.} We collect the two coefficients in
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_{distance},\ \beta_{\kappa}),
\label{eq:app-beta}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{distance}$, the coefficient on $\ln d_{r'a(r')}$ (or the distance bins), identifies $\alpha$, and $\beta_{\kappa}$, the coefficient on $\ln n_{r's}$, identifies $\kappa$. Both are read on the variation within an area, where $T_{a(r')s}$ is fixed and only trade costs and density can move the probability of supplying. With distance bins, $\ln\tau$ is piecewise linear in $\ln d$ with one elasticity $\alpha_k$ per segment between bin centres, $\alpha_1\le\dots\le\alpha_K$, which nests $\tau=d^{\alpha}$.

\subsection{Structural estimation: the moments}
\label{app:model-moments}

We use six sets of theoretical moments, observed in the data using a combination of IO tables and micro-level data.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Aggregate labor share at the level of the industry:}
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde\chi_{L}\equiv\frac{\sum_{r}w_{r}\ell_{r}}{\sum_{r}c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}
    =\sum_{r}\frac{c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}{\sum_{r}c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}\,\chi_{L}
    \left(\frac{w_{r}}{c_{r}}\right)^{1-\lambda}.
    \end{equation}
    In the model it is a sales-weighted average of firm-level labor shares. In the data we use the     share of labor costs in total costs from IO tables.
    
    \item \textbf{Aggregate share of sector $s$ in the industry's input purchases:}
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde\chi_{s}\equiv\frac{\sum_{r}p_{rs}q_{rs}}{\sum_{r}\sum_{s}p_{rs}q_{rs}}
    =\sum_{r}\frac{p_{r}q^{I}_{r}}{\sum_{r}p_{r}q^{I}_{r}}\,\chi_{s}
    \left(\frac{p_{rs}}{p_{r}}\right)^{1-\nu}.
    \end{equation}
    In the data we use technical coefficients for the downstream industry from IO tables.
    
    \item \textbf{The share of each region in downstream sales:}
    \begin{equation}
    \pi_{r}\equiv\frac{c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}{\sum_{r}c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}     =\left(\frac{p_{r}}{P}\right)^{-\varepsilon}     =\frac{(c_{r}A_{r}^{-1})^{-\varepsilon}}{\sum_{r}(c_{r}A_{r}^{-1})^{-\varepsilon}},
    \end{equation}
    recovered from sales data on the population of downstream producers.
    
    \item \textbf{The extensive-margin coefficients} $\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_{distance},\beta_{\kappa})$ of \eqref{eq:app-beta}: the cell-level cloglog \eqref{eq:app-cell-cloglog} of $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ on the distance to the anchor of the area, in logs or in the distance bins of Equation~\eqref{eq:reduced_form_micro}, and on $\ln n_{r's}$, with sector$\times$area fixed effects; groups without variation in the outcome are dropped. The regression set $\mathcal{A}$ is the cells of the active attraction areas, on both the model and the data side.
    
    \item \textbf{Area-aggregated sourcing shares and import shares.} The share of the downstream industry's \emph{total} purchases of sector $s$, domestic and imported, sourced from area $a$, and the share imported:
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde\gamma_{as}\equiv\frac{\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a}}\sum_{r}X_{r'rs}}{\sum_{r}x_{rs}}
    =\sum_{r}\frac{x_{rs}}{\sum_{r''}x_{r''s}}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a}}p_{r'rs},
    \qquad
    \tilde f_{s}\equiv\frac{\sum_{r}X_{Frs}}{\sum_{r}x_{rs}}=\sum_{r}\frac{x_{rs}}{\sum_{r''}x_{r''s}}\,f_{rs},
    \label{eq:app-gamma-area}
    \end{equation}
    with $X_{Frs}$ the imports of buyer $r$ from sector $s$, so that $\sum_a\tilde\gamma_{as}+\tilde f_s=1$. The import share $\hat f_s$ is one minus the domestic share of the downstream industry's purchases of sector $s$ in the input--output table. In the data we reconstitute firm-level sales to the downstream industry using sales-share data, $x_{i(r',s)d}=\mathbf{1}(\mathrm{Sup}_{i(r',s)})\,a^{D}_{d\,i(r',s)}\,x_{i(s,r')}$, sum over all firms in the area and normalize by the sum over all French firms of sector $s$; this domestic split is then scaled by $1-\hat f_s$, so that $\hat\gamma_{as}$ is a share of total purchases, as in the model. The aggregation runs over \emph{every} region of the area, including those hosting no supplier, on both the model and the data side.

    \item \textbf{The count moment} $\bar G_{s}(0)$ of \eqref{eq:app-count}: the share of regions inside active attraction areas that host no supplier of sector $s$.
\end{enumerate}

Moments 1--3 and 5 are the same objects as in the continuum version of the model, with 5 now aggregated to the area. Moments 4 and 6 are what the granular structure adds.

The two groups differ in how their expectation can be computed. Moments 1--3 and 5 are \emph{linear in the network}. Because buyers set sector spending on the expected price index \eqref{eq:app-pindex}, moments 1--3 do not depend on $\Omega$ at all, and by \eqref{eq:app-expected-share} the expected area sourcing share equals its value in the expected network, for any $N_s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[\gamma_{as}(\Omega)\big]=\sum_r \frac{x_{rs}}{\sum_{r''}x_{r''s}}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_a}\gamma_{r'rs}.
\label{eq:app-gamma-linear}
\end{equation}
Moment 6 is not linear in the network --- it is a product of indicators over varieties --- but its expectation \eqref{eq:app-count} is a closed-form function of $N_s$ and of the win-anywhere probabilities $p_{r's}$. Moment 4 has neither property: the cloglog coefficients are a nonlinear function of a whole realized network, so $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$ differs from the coefficient of the expected network and must be computed by Monte Carlo.\footnote{This is the point made by \cite{gaubertitskhohi2021}, p.~8: ``the population mean $\Phi_z$ is not, in general, equivalent to the asymptotic value of $\Lambda_z$ as $N_z\to\infty$''.} The probabilities $p_{r's}$ themselves have a closed form by inclusion--exclusion over the set of buyers, $p_{r's}=\sum_{S\neq\emptyset}(-1)^{|S|+1}P_{r'}(S)$ with $P_{r'}(S)=T_{r's}/\big(T_{r's}+\sum_{k\neq r'}T_{ks}\max_{r\in S}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'r}/w_{ks}\tau_{kr})^{\theta}\big)$, but it has $2^{R^D}-1$ terms, which is out of reach with twenty buyers. This is why the estimator is a simulated method of moments.

\paragraph{Notes on foreign parameters.}
\label{app:model-foreign}
We have no firm-level data on foreign suppliers, so the rest of the world is summarised, sector by sector, by the single scale $\Phi^F_s$ of Section~\ref{app:model-foreign-setup}. It is disciplined by one moment, the import share $\hat f_s$ of the input--output table, which enters the area block 5 as one more entry per importing sector. The pair $(\Phi^F_s,\hat f_s)$ is just-identified, exactly as each pair $(T_{as},\hat\gamma_{as})$, and $\Phi^F_s$ is calibrated jointly with comparative advantage (Section~\ref{app:model-estimation}, Step~1). Three properties of this treatment are worth stating.

First, $\Phi^F_s$ has the same units as the area-level $T_{as}$: the per-sector normalisation $T_{a^{ref}_s s}=1$ fixes both, the reference area being the largest \emph{domestic} area, and $\Phi^F_s$ is the Fréchet scale of a French region of the reference area with one firm ($n_{r's}=1$) at unit wage and unit trade cost. Its level therefore carries no information by itself; only its ratio to $\Phi^{dom}_{rs}$ does, through $f_{rs}$.

Second, imports change the moments in three places only. Block 2 is computed on total spending, domestic and imported, which is the base of the input--output technical coefficients. Block 5 is a share of total purchases, with the import share on its foreign row. And the bounds \eqref{eq:app-bounds} on $N_s$ apply to the varieties won in France. Blocks 1, 3, 4 and 6 are defined on French objects, which the foreign competitor affects only through prices and through the varieties it takes away from French regions.

Third, the foreign competitor shifts every buyer's domestic probabilities proportionally, so it leaves the within-France geography of sourcing unchanged at given $T$. It is not neutral for the estimation, however: since buyers far from French suppliers import more, a common $\Phi^F_s$ makes $f_{rs}$ vary across buyers, which reweights the buyers in $\tilde\gamma_{as}$ and in the win-anywhere probabilities $p_{r's}$. In the counterfactuals we hold each buyer's import share $f_{rs}$ at its estimate, by rescaling $\Phi^F_s$ buyer by buyer with the change in $\Phi^{dom}_{rs}$, so that the counterfactual regimes move the domestic network only.

\paragraph{Calibrated parameters.}
\label{app:model-calibration}
The remaining parameters $\{\varepsilon,\theta,\lambda,\nu,\{\nu_{s}\},\{w_{r's}\}\}$ are calibrated, and the firm counts $\{n_{r's}\}$ are observed. We use the estimates from \cite{Fontagn2022} for $\varepsilon$: the sales elasticity for motor vehicles and aerospace is respectively $-9$ and $-16$. The shape $\theta$ of the Fréchet distribution is calibrated following \cite{antras_margins_2017}. Elasticities of substitution in production follow the literature, notably \cite{baqaee_macroeconomic_2019}: $\lambda=0.5$ for the elasticity between labor and intermediate inputs and $\nu=0.9$ across sectors. Local wages are calibrated using labor cost information from employer--employee data in 2019 (DADS/BTS). The number of firms $n_{r's}$ of each sector in each region is counted in the same year and held fixed, consistently with the short-run horizon of the counterfactuals.



\subsection{Estimation}
\label{app:model-estimation}

The model's parameters are

\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\big\{\alpha,\;\kappa\big\}}_{\text{searched over}}
\;\cup\;
\underbrace{\big\{\{T_{as}\},\;\{\Phi^F_s\},\;\chi_{L},\;\{\chi_{s}\},\;\{A_{r}\},\;\{N_{s}\}\big\}}_{\text{calibrated within the model}},
\label{eq:app-param-split}
\end{equation}

but only the first group is searched over. Comparative advantage, the foreign scales, the downstream technology parameters $(\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\})$ and the variety count are calibrated within the model: given any candidate value of $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$, each is pinned by the model itself rather than left free. Comparative advantage is pinned by the area sourcing shares, the foreign scales by the import shares, the downstream technology parameters by the labor share, the sector shares and the regional sales shares, and the variety count by the share of empty regions. They are not fixed ahead of the estimation at some external target, and they are not searched over either --- they are recovered, inside the simulation of the economy that $\mu$ implies. This is exact rather than approximate: minimizing the criterion jointly over all parameters and minimizing it over $\mu$ with the other parameters set to their within-model values give the same estimate. Since these moments are matched exactly, the parameters searched over are identified by the extensive-margin coefficients $\boldsymbol\beta$ alone: $\beta_{distance}$ by the decline of supplier presence with distance within an area, $\beta_\kappa$ by its rise with density within an area. We describe first how an economy is simulated at a given $\mu$, then the search over $\mu$.

\subsubsection{Simulation algorithm.} Every evaluation of the criterion is solved in the same order. Given the calibrated elasticities and a candidate parameter vector, we calibrate comparative advantage and the downstream technology, simulate the Ricardian competition to obtain the win-anywhere probabilities, calibrate the variety count, and assemble the moments:
\begin{equation}
\mu=\big\{\alpha,\;\kappa\big\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\big\{\{T_{as}\},\{\Phi^F_s\},\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\}\big\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\{p_{r's}\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\{N_{s}\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\tilde M(\mu) .
\label{eq:app-recursion}
\end{equation}
The arrows run one way only. Comparative advantage is calibrated without knowing the variety count, and the variety count does not revise comparative advantage, so there is no fixed point between $T$ and $N$ to solve --- a recursion, not a simultaneous system. Below we explain each step of this algorithm for which the pseudo-code is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate}.

\paragraph{Step 1: comparative advantage, foreign scale and downstream technology.}
For a given $\mu$, we recover the area-level productivity parameters $T_{as}$ and the foreign scales $\Phi^F_s$ that exactly match the observed shares of total purchases, $\hat\gamma_{as}$ for every active area and $\hat f_s$ for the rest of the world. Given trade costs $\tau_{r'rs}=d_{r'r}^{\alpha}$ and the density elasticity $\kappa$, this is a matrix-scaling problem whose pseudo-code is in Algorithm~\ref{alg:invertT}. The foreign competitor is treated as one more column of $T$: it is an ``area'' whose market access is the same for every buyer. We solve the problem using the multiplicative update

\begin{equation}
T^{+}_{as} = T_{as} \left( \frac{\hat\gamma_{as}} {\gamma_{as}(T,\Phi^F)} \right)^{\varsigma},
\qquad
\Phi^{F+}_{s} = \Phi^F_{s} \left( \frac{\hat f_{s}} {f_{s}(T,\Phi^F)} \right)^{\varsigma},
\qquad \varsigma\in(0,1], \label{eq:app-sinkhorn}
\end{equation}

followed by the division of the whole row of sector $s$, foreign scale included, by $T^{+}_{a^{ref}_s s}$. The normalization of one domestic reference area per sector removes the indeterminacy in the common scale of $(T_{\cdot s},\Phi^F_s)$; an import share cannot serve as the reference, since $\Phi^F_s$ is measured against domestic productivity. The uniqueness of this inversion follows from the structure of the sourcing matrix. Holding expenditure weights fixed, the model-implied shares can be written as

\begin{equation}
\gamma_{as} = \sum_r \omega_{rs} \frac{T_{as}B_{ar}} {\sum_{a'}T_{a's}B_{a'r}+\Phi^F_s}, \qquad
f_{s} = \sum_r \omega_{rs} \frac{\Phi^F_s} {\sum_{a'}T_{a's}B_{a'r}+\Phi^F_s}, \qquad
B_{ar} = \sum_{r'\in\mathcal R_a} n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta},
\end{equation}

with $\sum_r\omega_{rs}=1$. The foreign column of the kernel is $B_{Fr}=1$ for every buyer. Because trade costs are finite, every area can potentially serve every buyer, so the augmented kernel $[B\,;\,\mathbf 1']$ is a strictly positive matrix; the density term only changes its entries, and a region without firm of the sector simply drops out of $B_{ar}$. The recovery of $(T_{\cdot s},\Phi^F_s)$ is therefore a Sinkhorn matrix-scaling problem, whose fixed point exists and is unique up to the sector-specific multiplicative normalization. The reference-area normalization removes this remaining degree of freedom, implying that there is a unique vector of comparative advantages and foreign scales consistent with the observed shares.

The expenditure weights $\omega_{rs}$ depend on prices and sales, hence on $(T,\Phi^F)$ and on the downstream technology parameters, which are calibrated in the same loop. Given $(T,\Phi^F)$, the expected price indices $p_{rs}$ of \eqref{eq:app-pindex}, which include the foreign competitor through $\Phi_{rs}$, do not depend on $(\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\})$, and these parameters are just-identified by moments 1--3. Writing $s_{rs}=\chi_sp_{rs}^{1-\nu}/\sum_k\chi_kp_{rk}^{1-\nu}$ for the sector split of intermediate spending and $\ell_r=\chi_L(w_r/c_r)^{1-\lambda}$ for the labor cost share, $\chi_L$ and $\{\chi_s\}$ solve
\begin{equation}
\sum_r\hat\pi_r\ell_r=\hat\chi_L,
\qquad
\frac{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}}{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)}=\hat\chi_s,
\label{eq:app-tech}
\end{equation}
jointly, by Newton's method, since the weights $\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)$ move with $\{\chi_s\}$ through $p_r$. Downstream productivity then follows in closed form from sales, $\pi_r\propto\delta_r(c_r/A_r)^{\varepsilon}$, that is $A_r\propto c_r(\hat\pi_r/\delta_r)^{-1/\varepsilon}$ with $A_1=1$. Because sales are held at their observed shares $\hat\pi_r$, the expenditure weights are $\omega_{rs}=\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}$ and do not involve $A_r$. The aggregate shares are solved at these \emph{total} price indices, and block 2 is a share of total spending, so imports enter the calibration of $\{\chi_s\}$ exactly as they enter the input--output coefficients. Each iteration of \eqref{eq:app-sinkhorn} therefore computes the price indices at the current $(T,\Phi^F)$, solves \eqref{eq:app-tech} at these prices, updates the weights and then $(T,\Phi^F)$. This introduces a feedback from $(T,\Phi^F)$ into expenditure shares, so the full algorithm is a fixed-point iteration. We use damping in \eqref{eq:app-sinkhorn} and stop when no comparative-advantage parameter or foreign scale moves by more than $10^{-9}$ in logs and the aggregate shares are solved. The starting value of $\Phi^F_s$ follows from the domestic starting value of $T$: with a common $\tilde\Phi_s=\sum_r\hat\pi_r\Phi^{dom}_{rs}$, the import share is $\Phi^F_s/(\tilde\Phi_s+\Phi^F_s)$, so $\Phi^{F(0)}_s=\hat f_s\tilde\Phi_s/(1-\hat f_s)$. Holding sales at their target also removes the demand loop through which, with fixed downstream technology, a higher $T$ lowers input prices, raises sales and sends demand back to the same area. At the estimated parameters, the inversion converges rapidly from dispersed starting values and recovers the calibrated productivity parameters to numerical precision. A candidate $\mu$ for which it does not converge is rejected rather than evaluated. \swann{Le paramètre de damping est fixé à 0.5. Au départ j'avais mis 0.9 mais quand on a une élasticité de la demande forte comme dans l'aéronautique, la convergence du point fixe n'était pas possible car il y avait beaucoup d'oscillation autour du point fixe.}

\paragraph{Step 2: Win probabilities.}
For each sector, we simulate $m$ varieties and draw one Fréchet champion for each (region, variety) pair \footnote{The uniform draws entering the inverse-CDF transformation $\varphi_{r'\rho s}=T_{r's}^{1/\theta}(-\log u_{r'\rho s})^{-1/\theta}$ are generated using scrambled Sobol sequences rather than independent pseudo-random draws. Scrambled Sobol sequences are low-discrepancy sequences commonly used for variance reduction, improving the convergence rate of simulation-based estimators and reducing simulation noise. We construct one low-dimensional sequence per sector, with regions as dimensions and varieties as points. This preserves the efficiency of low-discrepancy sampling while avoiding the deterioration of its properties in very high dimensions. Since Ricardian competition compares productivity draws only within sectors, using the same construction across sectors does not affect the allocation of winners. We use independent pseudo-random draws when simulating the fake worlds used for the weighting matrix and for inference, where independence across replications is required.
\swann{Si ce n'est pas clair il ne faut pas hésiter à me demander. C'est un point important que je souhaite avoir dans le papier.}}. In each importing sector, we also draw one foreign bid per variety from \eqref{eq:app-foreign-bid}, common to every buyer. Running the Ricardian competition described in \eqref{eq:app-champion}, in which a buyer imports the variety when the foreign bid is below the best French delivered cost, yields the number of simulated varieties won by each region, $k_{r's}$, the corresponding win-anywhere probability $p_{r's}=k_{r's}/m$, and the share $q_s$ of varieties won in France for at least one buyer, which enters the bounds \eqref{eq:app-bounds}.

The simulation size $m$ is only a Monte Carlo device and does not correspond to the model-implied number of varieties $N_s$. This distinction is important because winning a variety depends only on relative production costs: $$ \frac{w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs}}{z_{r'\rho s}} < \min_{r''\neq r'} \frac{w_{r''s}\tau_{r''rs}}{z_{r''\rho s}} . $$ Hence $p_{r's}$ is determined by comparative advantage, density, wages, trade costs and the productivity distribution, but not by the number of varieties, prices or downstream expenditure. It is an object of the expected network, and the number of simulated varieties can therefore be chosen independently of the equilibrium value of $N_s$, provided it is large enough to estimate $p_{r's}$ precisely.

\paragraph{Step 3: Variety count.}
The number of varieties is identified from the probability that a region does not supply any variety in a sector. For a sector with $N_s$ varieties, the population count moment is \eqref{eq:app-count}. In the simulation, we estimate this moment using the winning matrix generated in Step 2. Let $k_{r's}$ denote the number of simulated varieties for which region $r'$ is the winning supplier somewhere. For a candidate number of varieties $n$, we use the combinatorial estimator

\begin{equation}
\tilde G_s(n) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal R_s^+|} \sum_{r'\in\mathcal R_s^+} \frac{\binom{m-k_{r's}}{n}} {\binom{m}{n}} . \label{eq:app-Gclosed}
\end{equation}

For a given simulation draw, the ratio inside the sum is the fraction of subsets of $n$ simulated varieties that are not supplied by region $r'$. Taking expectations over the simulated productivity draws gives

$$ \mathbb{E} \left[ \frac{\binom{m-k_{r's}}{n}} {\binom{m}{n}} \right] = (1-p_{r's})^{n}, $$

so that $\tilde G_s(n)$ is an unbiased estimator of the population moment in \eqref{eq:app-count}\footnote{A natural alternative is to estimate $p_{r's}$ by $\hat p_{r's}=k_{r's}/m$ and use the plug-in estimator $(1-\hat p_{r's})^{N_s}$. Although this estimator is consistent as $m\rightarrow\infty$, it is biased in finite simulations because the transformation $p\mapsto(1-p)^{N_s}$ is nonlinear. In particular, a second-order expansion gives $$ \mathbb{E}\big[(1-\hat p_{r's})^{N_s}\big] -(1-p_{r's})^{N_s} \simeq \frac{N_s(N_s-1)}{2m} p_{r's}(1-p_{r's})^{N_s-1}, $$ which is positive. The combinatorial estimator uses the same simulated winning matrix but avoids applying this nonlinear transformation to the noisy probability estimate, delivering an unbiased estimator of the count moment for any finite $m$.}.

The variety count is then obtained by matching the simulated and empirical count moments:
\begin{equation}
\widehat N_s = \arg\min_{N\in[N_s^{\mathrm{lo}},N_s^{\mathrm{hi}}]\cap\mathbb N} \left(\tilde G_s(N)-\widehat G_s(0)\right)^2 . \label{eq:app-profile}
\end{equation}
Since $\tilde G_s(n)$ is decreasing in $n$, this minimization is a monotone integer search and does not require re-simulating the Ricardian competition for alternative values of $N_s$. The pseudo-algorithm for this step is Algorithm~\ref{alg:countN}.

\paragraph{Step 4: the moments.} All six blocks of Section~\ref{app:model-moments} are computed from the single Ricardian solve of step 2: the value blocks 1--3 and 5 from the realized trade flows, imports included in block 2 and on the foreign row of block 5, block 6 from \eqref{eq:app-Gclosed} evaluated at $\widehat N_{s}$, and block 4 as follows. The model counterpart of the observed coefficients is $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$: the data are one realization of a network with $N_s$ varieties, not the expected network. We estimate it by Monte Carlo at almost no cost. Once the Ricardian competition is solved on the $m$ simulated varieties, it is as if the network had been simulated once with $m\gg N_s$ varieties. We draw $J=100$ networks among them, network $j$ being the first $\widehat N_s$ entries of a fixed random permutation of the $m$ varieties of each sector; on each, the outcome $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ is whether region $r'$ wins one of these varieties for at least one buyer, and the cloglog \eqref{eq:app-cell-cloglog} is run exactly as on the data. Block 4 is the average of the $J$ coefficient vectors,
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(j)}(\mu),
\label{eq:app-beta-mc}
\end{equation}
fits that fail to converge being excluded and counted. Averaging the statistic over networks, rather than computing the statistic of the average network, is what makes $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}$ an estimator of $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$; the networks are nested in $N_s$, so a change in the variety count adds or removes varieties rather than redrawing the network. Pseudo-code is in Algorithm~\ref{alg:cellbeta}.


\subsubsection{Estimation algorithm.} The SMM estimator is defined as

\begin{equation}
\hat\mu=\text{argmin}_{\mu}\;\big(\tilde M(\mu)-\hat M\big)'W\big(\tilde M(\mu)-\hat M\big),
\label{eq:app-smm}
\end{equation}

where $\tilde M$ (resp.\ $\hat M$) denotes the vector of theoretical (resp.\ empirical) moments, and $W$ is a weighting matrix. Moments 1--3, 5 and 6 are matched by the parameters calibrated within the model, so the criterion effectively compares the simulated and observed extensive-margin coefficients, $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$.\footnote{Moments 1--3 are matched at the expected price indices, while the simulated moments use the realized price indices of the $m$ simulated varieties; the gap is pure Monte Carlo error, which the search must not chase, so moments 1--3 are left out of the criterion. Moments 5 and 6 are kept in the criterion of the first step, where they are matched up to Monte Carlo error and the integer step of $N_s$.} Optimal weights assign lower importance to imprecisely estimated moments; we construct them below. The minimization is carried out by Particle Swarm Optimization \citep{pso}, which explores the parameter space through parallel evaluation, with adaptive inertia weights declining from $0.9$ to $0.4$ over iterations and cognitive and social parameters set to $1.5$ and $2.5$ (see Algorithm~\ref{alg:pso}). The estimation proceeds in four steps (see Algorithm~\ref{alg:smm}).

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{A first fit under equal weights.} Starting values are set as follows: $\alpha$ at the extensive-margin distance coefficient divided by $\theta$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:cloglog}); $\kappa$ at the coefficient on $\ln n_{r's}$; and $T_{as}$ at the closed-form inversion of the sourcing shares at these values, with expenditure proxied by the observed sales shares. With distance bins, the starting elasticities $\alpha_k$ are the slopes of $\ln\tau_b=-\hat\beta_b/\theta$ between bin centres. We run the optimizer over $\mu$ with $W$ set to the identity, then refine it with the swarm restarted from the incumbent in a box around it whose size is updated across iterations. This yields $\hat\mu_{1}$.

\item \textbf{The weighting matrix.} At $\hat\mu_{1}$ we simulate fake worlds from the model and set $W$ to the inverse of the covariance of the residual between the coefficients of a fake world and their simulated counterpart, described in the next paragraph:
\begin{equation}
W=\hat\Sigma_u^{-1}.
\label{eq:app-W}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{The efficient fit.} We re-estimate the model with the updated weighting matrix,
initializing at $\hat\mu_{1}$, to obtain $\hat\mu_{2}$.

\item \textbf{Inference.} At $\hat\mu_{2}$ we compute the standard errors by parametric bootstrap, described below.

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The variance--covariance matrix of the moments.}
The weighting matrix is constructed from the uncertainty of both the empirical and simulated moments. The SMM compares two objects, each with its own source of randomness. Writing $\bar M(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_\Omega[M(\mu,\Omega)]$, and $U$ for the draws of the simulator,
\begin{equation}
\hat M=\bar M(\mu^*)+M_{gran},\qquad \tilde M(\mu,U)=\bar M(\mu)+M_{sim}(\mu,U).
\end{equation}
The first noise is granular: nature has drawn one network with $N_s$ varieties. The second is Monte Carlo error: the simulator estimates $\bar M(\mu)$ with a finite number of draws. Both have mean zero and are independent, and the first dominates by far, since a moment of one network with $N_s$ varieties is compared to an average over $m\gg N_s$ simulated varieties and $J$ networks. We abstract from measurement error in the data.

A bootstrap on firms would measure the granular noise poorly: it can remove suppliers from a region but never add one, so a region observed without supplier stays without supplier in every replication. We instead draw the granular noise from the model. The noise also reaches $\boldsymbol\beta$ through the parameters calibrated within the model: $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ are calibrated on the realized sourcing shares, sales and empty-region shares, which come from the same network. To first order the residual of the criterion is
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)-\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\simeq h_\alpha(\mu-\mu^*)-u+\boldsymbol\beta_{sim},\qquad u=\boldsymbol\beta_{gran}-h_\gamma\gamma_{gran}-h_GG_{gran},
\end{equation}
where $h(\mu,\gamma,G)$ is the expected $\boldsymbol\beta$ when the other parameters are calibrated on $(\gamma,G)$. The optimal weighting is $W=\Sigma_u^{-1}$, with $\Sigma_u=\operatorname{Var}(u)+\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol\beta_{sim})$, which differs from the variance of $\boldsymbol\beta_{gran}$ because part of the granular noise in $\boldsymbol\beta$ is absorbed by the recalibration.

We estimate $\Sigma_u$ without computing any derivative. At $\hat\mu_1$, we draw $B_1=1{,}000$ fake worlds: one network $\Omega_b$ with $\widehat N_s$ varieties per sector, drawn with independent pseudo-random numbers. On each, we compute the data moments exactly as on the data --- one cloglog fit $\boldsymbol\beta_b$, the empty-region shares $G_b$, the area sourcing shares $\gamma_b$, the labor share, the sector shares and the sales shares --- recalibrate $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ on them, and simulate $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$ at $\hat\mu_1$ with a simulator $U_b$ proper to the world. The residual
\begin{equation}
e_b=\boldsymbol\beta_b-\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}\big(\hat\mu_1;\,T_b,\chi_{L,b},\chi_{s,b},A_b,N_b,U_b\big)
\end{equation}
is to first order $u_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$, so $\hat\Sigma_u=\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(e_1,\dots,e_{B_1})$. The world's simulator must have its own seed: if it reused the draws of $\Omega_b$, it would reproduce the fake data exactly and the dispersion would vanish; if it were common to all worlds, the simulation noise would be omitted. Worlds in which the cloglog cannot be estimated, or in which a reference area wins nothing, are excluded and counted. $W$ is a square matrix of the dimension of $\boldsymbol\beta$. When $\boldsymbol\beta$ has as many entries as $\mu$ (one distance coefficient), the system is exactly identified and $W$ affects the inference only; it moves the point estimate when distance is binned.

\paragraph{Standard errors.} \label{app:model-standard_errors} \swann{Je décris les différents estimateurs des intervalles de confiance. }
We compute the uncertainty of the estimator by parametric bootstrap: the model is resampled, not the firms, so a fake world can host a supplier in a region that has none in the data. At $\hat\mu_2$ we draw $B_2=500$ fake worlds as above and re-run the whole estimation in each, with $W$ fixed: for each candidate $\mu$, $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ are calibrated on the world's own moments, $\boldsymbol\beta$ is simulated on the world's simulator, and the criterion is minimized by Nelder--Mead starting from $\hat\mu_2$, holding the simulator fixed within the world. Each world yields an estimate $(\mu_b,T_b,\chi_{L,b},\chi_{s,b},A_b,N_b)$, and the counterfactuals of the paper are recomputed at it.

For every parameter and every result, the standard error is the standard deviation across worlds, the 95\% confidence interval is given by the 2.5\% and 97.5\% quantiles, and the bias is the mean across worlds minus the estimate; if the bias is of the order of the standard deviation, the bias-corrected interval $[2\hat\mu-q_{97.5},\,2\hat\mu-q_{2.5}]$ should be used instead. The calibrated objects therefore need no separate treatment. $T$ and $N$ are recalibrated in every world, so their uncertainty combines that of $\hat\mu$ and the granular noise of the moments they are calibrated on, the integer jumps of $N_s$ included, and no derivative of the simulated moments with respect to $T$ --- which would capture the discrete changes of winners rather than smooth changes in moments --- is required. Comparing the dispersion of $\log T_{as}$ across areas in the fake worlds with its value at the estimate measures the upward bias of that dispersion, which contains the variance of the noise.

Two limits are worth stating. The worlds are drawn at $\hat\mu_2$ rather than at the true $\mu^*$, the usual limit of the parametric bootstrap, which is of second order unless the estimator is strongly biased --- something the bootstrap itself reveals. And measurement error in the data, such as non-response to the supplier surveys, is not covered by the procedure and is treated as a separate robustness exercise.

\subsection{Pseudo-code}
\label{app:pseudocode}

\providecommand{\var}[1]{\mathit{#1}}
\makeatletter

This section states the estimator in explicit form, in the version that the code implements. Only the trade-cost elasticity and the density elasticity are searched over,
$$
\mu=(\alpha,\kappa),
$$
with $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_K)$ when trade costs are binned. Every other parameter is calibrated within the model at each evaluation of the criterion: the area-level comparative advantages $\{T_{as}\}$, the foreign scales $\{\Phi^F_s\}$ and the downstream technology parameters $(\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\})$ jointly by a general-equilibrium Sinkhorn inversion, and the variety counts $\{N_s\}$ by a monotone integer search. Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} evaluates the model at a candidate $\mu$; it calls Algorithm~\ref{alg:invertT} (the joint calibration of comparative advantage, foreign scales and downstream technology), Algorithm~\ref{alg:ricardian} (the Ricardian competition with the foreign competitor), Algorithm~\ref{alg:countN} (the variety count) and Algorithm~\ref{alg:cellbeta} (the extensive-margin coefficients). Algorithm~\ref{alg:smm} is the estimator; it calls the swarm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pso}), the weighting matrix from fake worlds (Algorithm~\ref{alg:W}) and the parametric bootstrap (Algorithm~\ref{alg:boot}).

\paragraph{Notation.} The pseudo-code keeps the notation of the paper for every economic object and gives explicit names, in italics, to the objects that only exist inside an algorithm (for instance $\var{market\_access}_{ar}$ or $\var{largest\_log\_step}$). Each comment is a sentence that states the economic content of the step. Throughout, $r'$ is an upstream region (commuting zone), $s$ a sector, $\rho$ a variety; $a(r')$ is the attraction area of $r'$ and $\mathcal{R}_a$ the regions of area $a$; $\mathcal{R}_D$ is the set of downstream buyers; $\mathcal{R}^+_s$ the regions inside the active attraction areas of sector $s$, $\mathcal{A}^+_s$ those areas, $a^{ref}_s$ the largest of them (the reference area), $\mathcal{S}^F$ the sectors with imports in the input--output table, and $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{(r',s): r'\in\mathcal{R}^+_s\}$ the cells entering the extensive-margin regression. Comparative advantage of a cell is
$$
T_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}\,n_{r's}^{\kappa},
$$
with $n_{r's}$ the observed number of firms of sector $s$ in $r'$, and the rest of the world competes in sector $s\in\mathcal{S}^F$ through the scale $\Phi^F_s$, with no wage or trade cost of its own. Trade costs are $\tau_{r'r}=d_{r'r}^{\alpha}$; with $K$ distance bins, $\log\tau_{r'r}$ is continuous and piecewise linear in $\log d_{r'r}$, with slope $\alpha_k$ on the $k$-th segment between bin centres and $\alpha_1\le\dots\le\alpha_K$, so that equal slopes return the power law. The vector of extensive-margin coefficients is
$$
\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_{distance},\ \beta_\kappa),
$$
the cell-level cloglog of $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ on $\log d_{r'a(r')}$ (or the distance bins) and $\log n_{r's}$, with sector $\times$ area fixed effects and the groups without variation dropped. Data targets carry a hat: $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}$; the shares of each sector's \emph{total} purchases, $\hat\gamma_{as}$ for every active area and the import share $\hat f_s$ (so that $\sum_{a}\hat\gamma_{as}+\hat f_s=1$); the empty-cell shares $\widehat G_s(0)$ and the observed supplier counts $N^{obs}_s$; the labour share $\hat\chi_L$, the sector shares $\hat\chi_s$ (on total spending, domestic and imported) and the downstream sales shares $\hat\pi_r$. The calibrated objects
$$
  \bigl\{\theta,\ \varepsilon,\ \lambda,\ \nu,\ \{\nu_s\},\
  \{w_{r's}\},\ \{\delta_r\},\ \{d_{r'r}\},\ \{n_{r's}\}\bigr\}
$$
are fixed once and for all and suppressed from the argument lists. $m$ is the number of simulated varieties per sector and $J$ the number of simulated networks over which $\boldsymbol\beta$ is averaged ($J=100$).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 1 -- simulation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Simulation of the economy at a candidate $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$ (one criterion evaluation)}
\label{alg:simulate}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require candidate $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$; targets $\hat\gamma_{as}$, $\hat f_s$, $(\hat\chi_L,\{\hat\chi_s\},\{\hat\pi_r\})$, $\widehat G_s(0)$ and $N^{obs}_s$; a fixed array of scrambled Sobol draws $u_{r'\rho s}\in(0,1)$ for the French regions and $u^F_{\rho s}\in(0,1)$ for the foreign competitor, $\rho=1,\dots,m$; a fixed set of permutations $\{\sigma^s_j\}_{j\le J}$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$
\Ensure simulated moments $\widetilde{M}(\mu)$, the calibrated $\{T_{as}\},\{\Phi^F_s\},\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\}$ and $\{\widehat N_s\}$; or \textsc{reject}
\vspace{2pt}
\LineComment{Trade costs rise with distance at elasticity $\alpha$, segment by segment when distance is binned.}
\State $\tau_{r'r}\gets d_{r'r}^{\,\alpha}$
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 1 (comparative advantage, foreign scale and downstream technology).}
\LineComment{The joint calibration of $(T,\Phi^F,\chi_L,\chi_s,A)$ is detailed in Algorithm~\ref{alg:invertT}.}
\State \quad $\bigl(\{T_{as}\},\{\Phi^F_s\},\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\},\var{converged}\bigr)\gets\Call{ProfileComparativeAdvantage}{\alpha,\kappa;\ \hat\gamma,\hat f,\hat\chi_L,\hat\chi_s,\hat\pi}$
\LineComment{A candidate is never scored at a comparative advantage that the model does not imply.}
\State \textbf{if not} $\var{converged}$ \textbf{then return} \textsc{reject}
\LineComment{Each region inherits the productivity of its area, scaled up by its density of firms.}
\State $T_{r's}\gets T_{a(r')s}\,n_{r's}^{\kappa}$
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 2 (Ricardian competition, with the foreign competitor).}
\LineComment{Every variety is won, for every buyer, by the cheapest French region or by the foreign competitor (Algorithm~\ref{alg:ricardian}).}
\State \quad $\bigl(\{\var{winner}_{\rho sr}\},\{p_{\rho sr}\},\{\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}\},\{k_{r's}\},\{q_s\}\bigr)\gets\Call{RicardianCompetition}{\{T_{r's}\},\{\Phi^F_s\},\tau;\ u,u^F}$
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Realised prices, sales and flows.} For every $r\in\mathcal{R}_D$:
\LineComment{Imported varieties enter the price index like any other variety.}
\State \quad $P_{rs}\gets\bigl[\tfrac1m\sum_{\rho}p_{\rho s r}^{\,1-\nu_s}\bigr]^{1/(1-\nu_s)}$,\quad
  $P_r\gets\bigl[\sum_s\chi_s P_{rs}^{1-\nu}\bigr]^{1/(1-\nu)}$
\State \quad $c_r\gets\bigl[\chi_L w_r^{1-\lambda}+(1-\chi_L)P_r^{1-\lambda}\bigr]^{1/(1-\lambda)}$,\quad
  $\var{effective\_cost}_r\gets c_r/A_r$
\LineComment{A region's sales share falls with its effective unit cost, with elasticity $\varepsilon<0$.}
\State \quad $\pi_r\gets \var{effective\_cost}_r^{\,\varepsilon}\,\delta_r\big/\sum_{r''}\var{effective\_cost}_{r''}^{\,\varepsilon}\delta_{r''}$
\State \quad $X_{r'rs}\gets$ spending of $r$ on the varieties won by $r'$, and $X_{Frs}\gets$ its spending on imported varieties,
  from~(\ref{eq:input_flows})
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 3 (variety count).}
\LineComment{The number of varieties is set to match the share of empty regions (Algorithm~\ref{alg:countN}).}
\State \quad $\{\widehat{N}_s\}\gets\Call{CalibrateVarietyCount}{\{k_{r's}\},\{q_s\},m;\ \widehat{G}_s(0),N^{obs}_s}$
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 4 (moments).}
\LineComment{They are reported but left out of the criterion.}
\State \quad (1)--(3) $\widetilde\chi_L$, $\widetilde\chi_s$ (on total spending), $\pi_r$, all at the realised prices
\LineComment{The extensive-margin coefficients are averaged over $J$ simulated networks (Algorithm~\ref{alg:cellbeta}).}
\State \quad (4) $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}\gets\Call{ExtensiveMarginCoefficients}{\{\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}\},\{\widehat N_s\},\{\sigma^s_j\}}$
\State \quad (5) $\widetilde{\gamma}_{as}\gets\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_a}\sum_r X_{r'rs}\big/\var{total\_purchases}_s$,\quad
  $\widetilde f_s\gets\sum_r X_{Frs}\big/\var{total\_purchases}_s$,\quad with $\var{total\_purchases}_s=\sum_r\bigl(\sum_{r''}X_{r''rs}+X_{Frs}\bigr)$
\LineComment{The count moment is evaluated at the calibrated number of varieties.}
\State \quad (6) $\widetilde{G}_s(\widehat{N}_s)$
\State \Return $\widetilde{M}(\mu)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 1b -- Ricardian competition
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{RicardianCompetition}: the winner of every variety for every buyer, with the foreign competitor}
\label{alg:ricardian}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require cell productivities $T_{r's}$; foreign scales $\Phi^F_s$; trade costs $\tau_{r'r}$; the fixed draws $u_{r'\rho s}$ and $u^F_{\rho s}$, $\rho=1,\dots,m$
\Ensure the winner and the price of every (variety, buyer) pair, the win indicators $\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}$, the win counts $k_{r's}$ and the shares $q_s$ of varieties won in France
\For{$s=1,\dots,S$ \textbf{and} $\rho=1,\dots,m$}
    \For{$r'\in\mathcal{R}^+_s$}
        \LineComment{The best French firm of each region is a Fr\'echet draw, by the inverse c.d.f.}
        \State $z_{r'\rho s}\gets T_{r's}^{1/\theta}\bigl(-\log(1-u_{r'\rho s})\bigr)^{-1/\theta}$
    \EndFor
    \LineComment{The foreign bid has no distance component, so it is the same for every buyer.}
    \State $\var{foreign\_bid}_{\rho s}\gets\bigl(-\log(1-u^F_{\rho s})/\Phi^F_s\bigr)^{1/\theta}$ if $s\in\mathcal{S}^F$, else $+\infty$
    \For{$r\in\mathcal{R}_D$}
        \State $\var{best\_domestic\_cost}\gets\min_{r'} w_{r's}\tau_{r'r}/z_{r'\rho s}$,\quad
          $\var{best\_domestic\_origin}\gets\arg\min_{r'} w_{r's}\tau_{r'r}/z_{r'\rho s}$
        \If{$\var{foreign\_bid}_{\rho s}<\var{best\_domestic\_cost}$}
            \LineComment{The buyer imports the variety, which creates no domestic linkage.}
            \State $\var{winner}_{\rho sr}\gets F$,\quad $p_{\rho sr}\gets\var{foreign\_bid}_{\rho s}$
        \Else
            \State $\var{winner}_{\rho sr}\gets\var{best\_domestic\_origin}$,\quad $p_{\rho sr}\gets\var{best\_domestic\_cost}$
        \EndIf
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\LineComment{A region supplies a variety as soon as it wins it for one buyer.}
\State $\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}\gets\mathbf{1}\{\var{winner}_{\rho sr}=r'\ \text{for some }r\in\mathcal{R}_D\}$,\quad
  $k_{r's}\gets\sum_\rho\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}$,\quad $p_{r's}\gets k_{r's}/m$
\LineComment{This is the share of varieties won in France, which bounds the variety count.}
\State $q_s\gets\frac1m\sum_\rho\mathbf{1}\{\var{winner}_{\rho sr}\neq F\ \text{for some }r\}$
\State \Return $\{\var{winner}_{\rho sr}\},\{p_{\rho sr}\},\{\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}\},\{k_{r's}\},\{q_s\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}


Four features of Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} are worth emphasizing. First, the arrows run one way, $(\alpha,\kappa)\to(T,\Phi^F,\chi_L,\chi_s,A)\to\{p_{r's},q_s\}\to\{N_s\}\to\boldsymbol\beta$: the calibration of comparative advantage does not use the variety count, and the variety count does not revise it, so Steps~1--3 form a recursion rather than a simultaneous system. Second, the calibration of Step~1 runs on the \emph{expected} sector price indices $P_{sr}=\Gamma_s\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}$, foreign competitor included, while the moments of Step~4 use the \emph{realised} indices of the $m$ simulated varieties. At the calibrated values, the simulated blocks 1--3 therefore differ from their targets only by the Monte Carlo error of the realised indices, which the search must not chase: they leave the criterion. The same holds for $\tilde\gamma_{as}$ and $\tilde f_s$, matched in expectation by the calibration, and for $\widetilde G_s(0)$, matched up to the integer step by the bisection; the moments that carry information on $\mu$ are $\boldsymbol\beta$. Third, $m$ is a Monte Carlo device unrelated to $\widehat{N}_s$: the winner of a variety depends only on relative delivered costs, so $p_{r's}$ and $q_s$ are continuum objects estimated at any $m$ large enough. The code sets $m=\max\bigl(100,\max_s\lceil N^{obs}_s/(1-\hat f_s)\rceil\bigr)$, capped at $2{,}000$, which also bounds every candidate $N_s$. Fourth, the draws $u$, $u^F$ and the permutations $\sigma^s_j$ are held fixed across every evaluation (common random numbers), which makes the criterion a deterministic function of $\mu$ that a swarm can descend; $u$ is one low-dimensional scrambled Sobol net per sector, with regions as dimensions and varieties as points, and $u^F_{\cdot s}$ is one more dimension of the same net, so adding the foreign competitor leaves the domestic draws unchanged. Fake worlds (Algorithms~\ref{alg:W} and~\ref{alg:boot}) are drawn with independent pseudo-random numbers instead.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 2 -- joint calibration of T, Phi^F and the downstream technology
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{ProfileComparativeAdvantage}: comparative advantage, foreign scale and downstream technology by matrix scaling with general-equilibrium expenditure weights}
\label{alg:invertT}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $(\alpha,\kappa)$; targets $\hat\gamma_{as}$, $\hat f_s$ and $(\hat\chi_L,\{\hat\chi_s\},\{\hat\pi_r\})$;
  damping $\varsigma=0.3$; tolerance $\epsilon_T=10^{-9}$; at most $2\,000$ passes; starting values $T^{(0)}_{as}$ and $\Phi^{F(0)}_s$
\Ensure $\{T_{as}\},\{\Phi^F_s\},\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\}$ reproducing $\hat\gamma_{as}$, $\hat f_s$, $\hat\chi_L$, $\hat\chi_s$ and $\hat\pi_r$ in expectation, and a convergence flag
\LineComment{Productivities are only identified relative to the domestic reference area of each sector.}
\State $(T,\Phi^F)\gets(T^{(0)},\Phi^{F(0)})$; then, for each $s$, divide $T_{\cdot s}$ and $\Phi^F_s$ by $T_{a^{ref}_s s}$
\For{$\var{pass}=1,\dots,2\,000$}
    \LineComment{An area reaches a buyer more easily when its regions are dense, cheap and close.}
    \State $\var{market\_access}_{ar}\gets\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_a}n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'r})^{-\theta}$
    \LineComment{The expected price index includes the foreign competitor, common to every buyer.}
    \State $\Phi^{dom}_{sr}\gets\sum_{a}T_{as}\,\var{market\_access}_{ar}$,\quad
      $\Phi_{sr}\gets\Phi^{dom}_{sr}+\Phi^F_s$,\quad
      $P_{sr}\gets\Gamma_s\,\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}$
    \State \textbf{Downstream technology at these prices.} Solve for $(\chi_L,\{\chi_s\})$, $\sum_s\chi_s=1$, by Newton's method on $(\operatorname{logit}\chi_L,\log\chi_s/\chi_1)$, started from the previous pass:
    \State \quad $s_{rs}=\dfrac{\chi_s P_{sr}^{1-\nu}}{\sum_k\chi_kP_{kr}^{1-\nu}}$,\quad
      $c_r=\bigl[\chi_L w_r^{1-\lambda}+(1-\chi_L)P_r^{1-\lambda}\bigr]^{\frac1{1-\lambda}}$,\quad
      $\ell_r=\chi_L\Bigl(\dfrac{w_r}{c_r}\Bigr)^{1-\lambda}$
    \LineComment{Blocks 1 and 2 are matched at the total price indices, so imports count in the sector shares.}
    \State \quad $\sum_r\hat\pi_r\ell_r=\hat\chi_L$,\qquad
      $\dfrac{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}}{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)}=\hat\chi_s,\ s=2,\dots,S$
    \LineComment{Sales are held at their observed shares, so downstream productivity does not enter the weights.}
    \State $\omega_{rs}\gets\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}$
    \LineComment{Both are shares of the sector's total purchases, so they sum to one across areas and imports.}
    \State $\gamma^{model}_{as}\gets\dfrac{\sum_r\omega_{rs}\,T_{as}\,\var{market\_access}_{ar}/\Phi_{sr}}{\sum_r\omega_{rs}}$,\quad
      $f^{model}_{s}\gets\dfrac{\sum_r\omega_{rs}\,\Phi^F_s/\Phi_{sr}}{\sum_r\omega_{rs}}$
    \LineComment{An area that the model under-supplies relative to the data becomes more productive.}
    \State $T^{+}_{as}\gets T_{as}\bigl(\hat\gamma_{as}/\gamma^{model}_{as}\bigr)^{\varsigma}$ if $\hat\gamma_{as}>0$, else $T^{+}_{as}\gets T_{as}$
    \LineComment{The foreign competitor follows the same rule, on the import share.}
    \State $\Phi^{F+}_{s}\gets \Phi^{F}_{s}\bigl(\hat f_{s}/f^{model}_{s}\bigr)^{\varsigma}$ for $s\in\mathcal{S}^F$
    \LineComment{The renormalisation moves every column of the sector, the foreign one included.}
    \State divide $T^{+}_{\cdot s}$ and $\Phi^{F+}_s$ by $T^{+}_{a^{ref}_s s}$ for each $s$
    \State $\var{largest\_log\_step}\gets\max\bigl(\max_{a,s:\ \hat\gamma_{as}>0}|\log T^{+}_{as}-\log T_{as}|,\ \max_{s\in\mathcal{S}^F}|\log \Phi^{F+}_{s}-\log \Phi^F_{s}|\bigr)$
    \State $(T,\Phi^F)\gets(T^{+},\Phi^{F+})$
    \LineComment{The iteration stops when no productivity moves by more than $10^{-9}$ in logs.}
    \State \textbf{if} $\var{largest\_log\_step}<\epsilon_T$ \textbf{then break}
\EndFor
\LineComment{Downstream productivity follows in closed form from the sales shares (block 3).}
\State re-solve \eqref{eq:app-tech} at the final $(T,\Phi^F)$;\quad $A_r\gets c_r\,(\hat\pi_r/\delta_r)^{-1/\varepsilon}$, normalised to $A_1=1$
\State $\var{converged}\gets$ ($\var{largest\_log\_step}<\epsilon_T$) \textbf{and} (the solution of \eqref{eq:app-tech} converged) \textbf{and} (every $A_r$ is finite)
\State \Return $(T,\Phi^F,\chi_L,\{\chi_s\},\{A_r\},\var{converged})$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

With the downstream technology held fixed, the multiplicative update is a Sinkhorn scaling of the strictly positive kernel $[\var{market\_access}\,;\,\mathbf 1']$, in which the foreign competitor is a column whose market access equals one in every buyer --- the density term $n_{r's}^{\kappa}$ only changes the domestic entries --- hence a fixed point unique up to the sector-specific scale removed by the reference-area normalisation. Two choices follow from the units of $\Phi^F_s$. The reference is always a domestic area, since $\Phi^F_s$ is measured against domestic productivity; and the renormalisation divides $\Phi^F_s$ by the same factor as the areas, so the import share is invariant to it. Given $(T,\Phi^F)$, the downstream technology parameters are just-identified by blocks 1--3 through the expected price indices, which do not depend on them; $(\chi_L,\chi_s)$ are solved jointly because the input-share weights $\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)$ move with $\chi_s$ through $P_r$, and $A_r$ follows in closed form since sales are $\pi_r\propto\delta_r(c_r/A_r)^{\varepsilon}$. Pinning sales at their target removes the demand loop through which, with fixed downstream technology, raising $T$ lowers input prices, raises sales and sends demand back to the same area. A zero-target column (an area active in the data that wins nothing, which only happens in a fake world) is held fixed relative to the reference and left out of the stopping rule. The starting value $T^{(0)}$ is the closed-form inversion of the domestic shares at the priors $(\alpha_0,\kappa_0)$ with expenditure proxied by $\hat\pi_r$, and $\Phi^{F(0)}_s=\hat f_s\,\tilde\Phi_s/(1-\hat f_s)$ with $\tilde\Phi_s=\sum_r\hat\pi_r\Phi^{dom}_{sr}$ at that start, the import share of a sector whose buyers all faced the same domestic index. Each worker keeps the last converged $(T,\Phi^F,\chi_L,\chi_s)$ as the next starting value; since the fixed point is unique, a converged result does not depend on it, and a failed warm start is retried from $(T^{(0)},\Phi^{F(0)})$. A particle for which the calibration does not converge is rejected rather than evaluated. \swann{Je n'ai pas réussi à montrer que quand on ajoute le GE feedback on obtient une application contractante (je ne suis pas sûr que ça soit possible d'ailleurs).}

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{CalibrateVarietyCount}: variety count by monotone integer search}
\label{alg:countN}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require win counts $k_{r's}$ and shares of varieties won in France $q_s$ from $m$ simulated varieties; targets
  $\widehat{G}_s(0)$ (the data, or a fake world's own empty-cell shares); observed supplier counts $N^{obs}_s$
\Ensure $\{\widehat{N}_s\}$, and for each sector whether a bound binds
\For{$s=1,\dots,S$}
    \LineComment{Only the varieties won in France can produce an observed supplier.}
    \State $N^{hi}_s\gets\min\bigl(\lceil N^{obs}_s/q_s\rceil,\ \lceil N^{obs}_s/(1-\hat f_s)\rceil,\ m\bigr)$
    \LineComment{Each variety won in France has at most one supplier per buyer.}
    \State $N^{lo}_s\gets\min\bigl(\max(\lceil N^{obs}_s/R^D\rceil,\ \lceil N^{obs}_s/(R^Dq_s)\rceil),\ N^{hi}_s\bigr)$
    \LineComment{This is an unbiased estimator of $\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^+_s|}\sum_{r'}(1-p_{r's})^{n}$.}
    \State define
      $\widetilde{G}_s(n)\gets\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^+_s|}\displaystyle\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^+_s}
      \binom{m-k_{r's}}{n}\Big/\binom{m}{n}$
    \If{$\widetilde{G}_s(N^{lo}_s)\le\widehat{G}_s(0)$}
        \LineComment{Even the smallest admissible count leaves too few empty regions; the lower bound binds.}
        \State $\widehat{N}_s\gets N^{lo}_s$
    \ElsIf{$\widetilde{G}_s(N^{hi}_s)\ge\widehat{G}_s(0)$}
        \LineComment{Even the largest admissible count leaves too many empty regions; the upper bound binds.}
        \State $\widehat{N}_s\gets N^{hi}_s$
    \Else
        \State $(\var{count\_low},\var{count\_high})\gets(N^{lo}_s,N^{hi}_s)$
        \While{$\var{count\_high}-\var{count\_low}>1$}
            \State $\var{count\_mid}\gets\lfloor(\var{count\_low}+\var{count\_high})/2\rfloor$
            \LineComment{More varieties leave fewer regions without a buyer.}
            \State \textbf{if} $\widetilde{G}_s(\var{count\_mid})>\widehat{G}_s(0)$ \textbf{then}
              $\var{count\_low}\gets \var{count\_mid}$ \textbf{else} $\var{count\_high}\gets \var{count\_mid}$
        \EndWhile
        \State $\widehat{N}_s\gets\arg\min_{n\in\{\var{count\_low},\var{count\_high}\}}
          \bigl|\widetilde{G}_s(n)-\widehat{G}_s(0)\bigr|$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\{\widehat{N}_s\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Because $\widetilde{G}_s$ is decreasing in $n$ and is evaluated in closed form from the win counts of Step~2, the search costs $O(\log(N^{hi}_s-N^{lo}_s))$ evaluations and never re-simulates the Ricardian competition. The bounds use $q_s$ from the same draws, which does not depend on $N_s$, so they introduce no circularity; without imports $q_s=1$ and they reduce to $[\lceil N^{obs}_s/R^D\rceil,N^{obs}_s]$. The count is an integer at every evaluation: it is never relaxed to the real line and never rounded. A bound that binds is recorded and reported rather than silently absorbed.

\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{ExtensiveMarginCoefficients}: the extensive-margin coefficients, averaged over $J$ networks}
\label{alg:cellbeta}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require win indicators $\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\rho s}$ of Step~2; variety counts $\{\widehat N_s\}$; permutations $\sigma^s_j$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$, $j=1,\dots,J$
\Ensure $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}$, an estimate of $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$, and the number of failed fits
\For{$j=1,\dots,J$}
    \LineComment{Networks are nested in $N$: one more variety adds a variety, it does not redraw the network.}
    \State network $j$ is made of the varieties $\sigma^s_j(1),\dots,\sigma^s_j(\widehat N_s)$ of each sector
    \LineComment{A region hosts a supplier in network $j$ if it wins one of its varieties for some buyer.}
    \State $\var{supplier\_present}^{(j)}_{r's}\gets\max_{t\le\widehat N_s}\var{win\_indicator}_{r'\sigma^s_j(t)s}$ for $(r',s)\in\mathcal{A}$
    \LineComment{This is the regression run on the data, unchanged.}
    \State $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(j)}\gets$ cloglog of $\var{supplier\_present}^{(j)}$ on $\log d_{r'a(r')}$ (or the bins) and $\log n_{r's}$, with sector$\times$area fixed effects and the groups without variation dropped
\EndFor
\LineComment{Fits that fail to converge or are quasi-separated are excluded and counted.}
\State $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}\gets$ mean of the $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(j)}$ over the converged fits
\State \Return $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each network has exactly $\widehat N_s$ varieties per sector, as the data do, and $\boldsymbol\beta$ is computed on it exactly as on the data; averaging the statistic over networks, rather than computing the statistic of the average network, is what targets $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$ when the observed $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ is a single realisation. Imported varieties are part of each network but make no French region a supplier, so the foreign competitor enters $\boldsymbol\beta$ through the varieties it takes away from distant regions. Drawing the $J$ networks among the $m$ winners already found costs no further Ricardian solve.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 5 -- PSO
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{ParticleSwarm}: particle swarm minimisation of $\mathcal{Q}$ over a box}
\label{alg:pso}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require objective $\mathcal{Q}$; box $[\var{lower},\var{upper}]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$; swarm size
  $P$; iterations $I$; warm start $x_0$; inertia range
  $[\underline{w},\overline{w}]=[0.4,0.9]$; cognitive weight $c_1=1.5$; social weight $c_2=2.5$
\Ensure $x^\star=\arg\min \mathcal{Q}$ over the visited points
\LineComment{The incumbent is always a particle, so a stage never ends worse than it started.}
\State draw $x_1,\dots,x_{P-1}$ from a scrambled Sobol net on $[\var{lower},\var{upper}]$;\;
  $x_P\gets\Pi_{[\var{lower},\var{upper}]}(x_0)$
\State $\var{velocity}_i\gets 0.1\,(\var{upper}-\var{lower})\odot\xi_i$, $\xi_i\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$; \; $\var{own\_best}_i\gets x_i$, $f_i\gets\mathcal{Q}(x_i)$ evaluated in
  parallel; \; $\var{swarm\_best}\gets\arg\min_i f_i$
\For{$t=1,\dots,I$}
    \LineComment{The inertia declines, so the swarm explores first and exploits later.}
    \State $w\gets\overline{w}-(\overline{w}-\underline{w})\,t/I$
    \For{$i=1,\dots,P$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \State draw $\var{noise}_1,\var{noise}_2\sim U[0,1]^d$
        \LineComment{The symbol $\odot$ denotes element-wise multiplication.}
        \State $\var{velocity}_i\gets w\,\var{velocity}_i+c_1\, \var{noise}_1\odot(\var{own\_best}_i-x_i)+c_2\, \var{noise}_2\odot(\var{swarm\_best}-x_i)$
        \State $\var{velocity}_i\gets\Pi_{[-\bar v,\bar v]}(\var{velocity}_i)$, \; $\bar v=0.1\,(\var{upper}-\var{lower})$
        \State $x_i\gets x_i+\var{velocity}_i$; reflect $x_i$ back into the box, halving the
          reflected velocity
        \LineComment{Each evaluation is one call to Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate}, worth $+\infty$ on \textsc{reject}.}
        \State $f\gets\mathcal{Q}(x_i)$
        \State \textbf{if} $f<\mathcal{Q}(\var{own\_best}_i)$ \textbf{then} $\var{own\_best}_i\gets x_i$;\;
          \textbf{if} $f<\mathcal{Q}(\var{swarm\_best})$ \textbf{then} $\var{swarm\_best}\gets x_i$
    \EndFor
    \LineComment{Particles stuck far from the best point are restarted.}
    \State \textbf{if} $t>30$ and $t\equiv 0 \pmod{25}$: redraw uniformly in the box every particle with $\mathcal{Q}(x_i)>1.5\,\mathcal{Q}(\var{swarm\_best})$
\EndFor
\State \Return $\var{swarm\_best}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When trade costs are binned, $\mathcal{Q}$ reads the block $(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_K)$ \emph{sorted}, and the box bounds of that block are sorted too, so every evaluated point satisfies $\alpha_1\le\dots\le\alpha_K$ and stays in the box.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 6 -- weighting matrix from fake worlds
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{WorldWeightMatrix}: the weighting matrix from fake worlds}
\label{alg:W}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\hat\mu_1=(\hat\alpha_1,\hat\kappa_1)$ and its calibrated parameters $(\widehat T,\widehat\Phi^F,\widehat\chi_L,\widehat\chi_s,\widehat A,\widehat N)$; number of worlds $B_1=1\,000$
\Ensure $W$, on $\boldsymbol\beta$ only
\For{$b=1,\dots,B_1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \LineComment{This is one realisation of the economy, as nature drew the data.}
    \State draw a network $\Omega_b$ at $(\hat\mu_1,\widehat T,\widehat\Phi^F,\widehat\chi_L,\widehat\chi_s,\widehat A)$ with $\widehat N_s$ varieties per sector and i.i.d.\ draws (seed $b$)
    \State on $\Omega_b$, as on the data: $\boldsymbol\beta_b$ (\emph{one} cloglog fit), $G_b$, $\gamma_b$ with its import shares $f_b$, $\hat\chi_{L,b}$, $\hat\chi_{s,b}$ (on total spending), $\pi_b$
    \LineComment{Skipped worlds are counted and reported.}
    \State \textbf{skip} the world if the fit of $\boldsymbol\beta_b$ fails or a reference area wins nothing
    \LineComment{The foreign scale is re-calibrated on the world's own import share, and an area with $\gamma_{b,as}=0$ gets $T_{as}=0$.}
    \State $(T_b,\Phi^F_b,\chi_{L,b},\chi_{s,b},A_b)\gets\Call{ProfileComparativeAdvantage}{\hat\alpha_1,\hat\kappa_1;\ \gamma_b,f_b,\hat\chi_{L,b},\hat\chi_{s,b},\pi_b}$
    \State $\var{world\_simulator}_b\gets$ Sobol draws at $m$ randomised by a seed proper to the world, and its own permutations $\sigma^s_j$
    \State run Steps~2--4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} with $\var{world\_simulator}_b$ at $(\hat\mu_1,T_b,\Phi^F_b,\chi_{L,b},\chi_{s,b},A_b)$, with $N_b\gets\Call{CalibrateVarietyCount}{\cdot\,;G_b}$, which gives $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$
    \LineComment{The residual combines the granular noise of the world and the simulation noise.}
    \State $e_b\gets\boldsymbol\beta_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$
\EndFor
\State $\hat\Sigma_u\gets\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(e_1,\dots,e_{B_1})$,\quad $W\gets\hat\Sigma_u^{-1}$
\LineComment{A mean residual away from zero measures the bias of the simulator against a finite world.}
\State report $\bar e$ and its $t$-statistic
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

No derivative is computed: to first order $e_b$ is the composite noise $u_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,MC,b}$, so $\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(e_b)$ estimates $\Sigma_u$, including the part of the granular noise in $\boldsymbol\beta$ absorbed by the recalibration of $T$, $\Phi^F$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ on the same network. The import share of a world is the realised share of its few varieties, so $\Phi^F_b$ is noisy, and that noise reaches $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$ through the varieties the foreign competitor takes. The world's simulator must have its own seed: if it reused the draws of $\Omega_b$, it would reproduce the fake data exactly at $\hat\mu_1$ and the dispersion would vanish; if it were common to all worlds, the simulation noise would be omitted. When $\boldsymbol\beta$ has as many entries as $\mu$ (one distance coefficient), the system is exactly identified and $W$ moves the inference, not the point estimate.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 7 -- parametric bootstrap
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{WorldBootstrap}: inference by parametric bootstrap}
\label{alg:boot}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require an estimate $\hat\mu=(\hat\alpha,\hat\kappa)$ and its calibrated parameters $(\widehat T,\widehat\Phi^F,\widehat\chi_L,\widehat\chi_s,\widehat A,\widehat N)$; a weighting matrix $W$, fixed; number of worlds $B_2=500$
\Ensure standard deviations, 95\% intervals and biases of $\alpha,\kappa,N,T,\Phi^F,\chi_L,\chi_s,A$ and of the counterfactuals
\For{$b=1,\dots,B_2$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State draw $\Omega_b$ and compute $(\boldsymbol\beta_b,G_b,\gamma_b,f_b,\hat\chi_{L,b},\hat\chi_{s,b},\pi_b)$ as in Algorithm~\ref{alg:W}, with a simulator $\var{world\_simulator}_b$ of its own
    \State define, for $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$ in the box ($\alpha$ read sorted):
    \LineComment{The calibration is warm-started within the world.}
    \State \quad $(T,\Phi^F,\chi_L,\chi_s,A)\gets\Call{ProfileComparativeAdvantage}{\alpha,\kappa;\ \gamma_b,f_b,\hat\chi_{L,b},\hat\chi_{s,b},\pi_b}$
    \State \quad $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim}(\mu)\gets$ Steps~2--4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} with $\var{world\_simulator}_b$, and $N\gets\Call{CalibrateVarietyCount}{\cdot\,;G_b}$
    \State \quad $\mathcal{Q}_b(\mu)\gets\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim}(\mu)\bigr)'W\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim}(\mu)\bigr)$, $+\infty$ outside the box or on \textsc{reject}
    \LineComment{The simulator is held fixed within the world, so the criterion is deterministic.}
    \State $\mu_b\gets\arg\min_\mu\mathcal{Q}_b(\mu)$ by Nelder--Mead started at $\hat\mu$
    \State $(T_b,\Phi^F_b,\chi_{L,b},\chi_{s,b},A_b,N_b)\gets$ the calibrated parameters and the count at $\mu_b$
    \LineComment{In every regime each buyer's import share is held at its value in the world.}
    \State recompute the counterfactuals at $(\mu_b,T_b,\Phi^F_b,\chi_{L,b},\chi_{s,b},A_b)$
\EndFor
\State for every parameter and result: standard deviation across worlds, $[q_{2.5},q_{97.5}]$, and bias $=$ mean $-$ estimate
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This is a parametric bootstrap: the model is resampled, not the firms, so a fake world can host a supplier in a region that has none in the data. The whole chain is re-run in each world, including the calibration of $T$, $\Phi^F$ and of the downstream technology on the world's own targets, so the sampling noise of every calibrated object enters the draws. The worlds are drawn i.i.d.\ rather than from the Sobol design of the estimation, whose stratification would understate the variance. An area active in the data that wins nothing in a world gets $T=0$ and is reported as an empty share rather than as a log of zero; a world whose reference area is empty cannot be normalised and is excluded. The counterfactuals (equal $T$, $\tau\equiv1$) rescale the foreign scale buyer by buyer, $\Phi^F_{rs}=\Phi^F_s\,\Phi^{dom}_{rs}(\text{regime})/\Phi^{dom}_{rs}(\text{estimate})$, so that every $f_{rs}$ is held at its estimate and the regimes move the domestic network only. If the bias is of the order of the standard deviation, the bias-corrected interval $[2\hat\mu-q_{97.5},\,2\hat\mu-q_{2.5}]$ should be used instead of the quantiles.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 8 -- estimation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{SMM estimation of $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$}
\label{alg:smm}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require targets $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}$, $\hat\gamma_{as}$, $\hat f_s$, $\widehat{G}_s(0)$, $(\hat\chi_L,\{\hat\chi_s\},\{\hat\pi_r\})$; number of refinement loops $L=20$; swarm size $P=98$; iterations $I_0=200$ (initial fit) and $I=50$ (refinement)
\Ensure $\widehat{\mu}_1$, $\widehat{\mu}_2$, their calibrated parameters, and their bootstrap distributions
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Starting values.} $\alpha_0=-\hat\beta_{distance}/\theta$ from the continuous-distance specification (with bins: the slopes of $\log\tau_b=-\hat\beta_b/\theta$ between bin centres, as a running maximum); $\kappa_0=\hat\beta_\kappa$; $(T^{(0)},\Phi^{F(0)})$ the closed-form inversion of $(\hat\gamma,\hat f)$ at $(\alpha_0,\kappa_0)$. Box: $\alpha\in[0.1\,\alpha_0,\ \min(10\,\alpha_0,1.5)]$, $\kappa\in[-1,3]$.
\vspace{3pt}
\LineComment{Blocks 1--3 are matched by the calibration and left out of the loss.}
\State \textbf{Step 1: first fit under equal weights.} Let
  $\mathcal{Q}_I(\mu)=\bigl\|\widetilde{M}_{4,5,6}(\mu)-\widehat{M}_{4,5,6}\bigr\|^2$, each evaluation running Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate}
\State \quad $\mu\gets\Call{ParticleSwarm}{\mathcal{Q}_I,\var{whole\_box},P,I_0,\mu_0}$
\For{$\var{loop}=1,\dots,L$}
    \LineComment{The search box narrows around the incumbent as the loops proceed.}
    \State $\var{tightness}\gets 0.3+0.6\,(\var{loop}-1)/(L-1)$
    \State $\var{local\_box}\gets\bigl\{\alpha\in[\alpha\cdot\var{tightness},\ \alpha/\var{tightness}],\ \kappa\pm(1-\var{tightness})\cdot 2\bigr\}\cap\var{whole\_box}$
    \State $\mu\gets\Call{ParticleSwarm}{\mathcal{Q}_I,\var{local\_box},P,I,\mu}$
    \LineComment{The loops stop once the incumbent no longer moves.}
    \State \textbf{break if} $\var{loop}>2$ and $\max_k|\Delta\mu_k|/|\mu_k|<10^{-6}$
\EndFor
\State $\widehat{\mu}_1\gets\mu$ and its calibrated parameters $(\widehat T,\widehat\Phi^F,\widehat\chi_L,\widehat\chi_s,\widehat A,\widehat N)$
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 2: the weighting matrix.} $W\gets\Call{WorldWeightMatrix}{\widehat\mu_1}$ \Comment{See Algorithm~\ref{alg:W}.}
\State \quad inference on $\widehat\mu_1$: $\Call{WorldBootstrap}{\widehat\mu_1,\ I}$ \Comment{See Algorithm~\ref{alg:boot}, with the equal weights of Step~1.}
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 3: the efficient fit.} Let
  $\mathcal{Q}_W(\mu)=\bigl(\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)-\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}\bigr)'W
  \bigl(\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)-\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}\bigr)$; run the swarm in a box of tightness $0.5$ around $\widehat\mu_1$ and the refinement loops of Step~1, yielding $\widehat{\mu}_2$ and its calibrated parameters
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 4: inference.} $\Call{WorldBootstrap}{\widehat\mu_2,\ W}$ \Comment{See Algorithm~\ref{alg:boot}.}
\State \Return $\widehat{\mu}_1$, $\widehat{\mu}_2$, their calibrated parameters and bootstrap distributions
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Two implementation notes. First, the calibration within the model divides the work: $\{T_{as}\}$, $\{\Phi^F_s\}$, $\chi_L$, $\{\chi_s\}$ and $\{A_r\}$ are pinned exactly by $(\hat\gamma,\hat f)$ and blocks 1--3, and $\{N_s\}$ by $\widehat G_s(0)$, so the swarm searches a space of dimension $K+1$ (two with a power-law trade cost) and $\mu$ is identified by $\boldsymbol\beta$ alone: $\beta_{distance}$ by the decline of supplier presence with distance within an area, $\beta_\kappa$ by its rise with density within an area. This is why the criterion of Step~3 runs on $\boldsymbol\beta$ only, and why the weighting matrix is $(N_{REG}+1)\times(N_{REG}+1)$. Second, the refinement loops of Step~1 re-run the swarm on $\mu$ warm-started at the incumbent, in a box whose width shrinks across loops; since the incumbent is always a particle, the criterion is monotone across loops by construction.

\subsection{EV eco-score reform: shock construction}\label{app:ev_ecoscore}
 
This appendix describes the construction of the region-specific downstream demand shocks used in Section~4.3, based on the estimates of \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}.
 
\paragraph{Vehicle categories.} We partition the vehicle market into three types: eligible electric vehicles (EEV), which retain the purchase subsidy post-reform; non-eligible electric vehicles (NEV), which lose the subsidy; and internal combustion engine vehicles (ICE). The reform amounts to a price shock
\begin{equation*}
    \Delta \tau_v = \begin{cases}
        0 & \text{if } v = \text{EEV}, \\
        +\sigma & \text{if } v = \text{NEV}, \\
        0 & \text{if } v = \text{ICE},
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
where $\sigma = \text{\euro{4,260}}$ is the lower-bound subsidy loss computed in \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}, Equation~(1).
 
\paragraph{Demand parameters.} The counterfactual share ratios are computed using the three-level nested logit estimated in \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}. The relevant parameters are the price sensitivity $\alpha = 0.087$ (per \euro{1,000}), the upper-nest correlation $\mu_3 = 0.648$ (powertrain level), and the lower-nest scale $\mu_{4,E} = 0.569$ (within-electric type level). Pre-reform market shares are $s_E^{\circ} = 0.169$ (electric share in total registrations) and $\omega^{\text{NEV}} = 0.30$ (NEV share within the electric segment).
 
\paragraph{Counterfactual share ratios.} Following the exact hat-algebra approach of \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}, we compute the type-level dampening factor for non-eligible EVs:
\begin{equation*}
    \Lambda_{\text{NEV}} = \exp\!\left( -\frac{\alpha}{\mu_{4,E}} \, \sigma \right) = \exp\!\left( -\frac{0.087}{0.569} \times 4.26 \right) \approx 0.520,
\end{equation*}
and the electric nest dampening factor:
\begin{equation*}
    \Phi_E = (1 - \omega^{\text{NEV}}) \cdot 1 + \omega^{\text{NEV}} \cdot \Lambda_{\text{NEV}} = 0.70 + 0.30 \times 0.52 \approx 0.856.
\end{equation*}
The counterfactual share ratios $\hat{s}_v \equiv s_v^{\text{post}} / s_v^{\text{pre}}$ are then:
\begin{align}
    \hat{s}_{\text{EEV}} &= \frac{\Phi_E^{\,\mu_{4,E}/\mu_3 - 1}}{s_E^{\circ} \cdot \Phi_E^{\,\mu_{4,E}/\mu_3} + (1 - s_E^{\circ})} \approx 1.002, \label{eq:shat_eev} \\[4pt]
    \hat{s}_{\text{NEV}} &= \Lambda_{\text{NEV}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{EEV}} \approx 0.521, \label{eq:shat_nev} \\[4pt]
    \hat{s}_{\text{ICE}} &= \frac{1}{s_E^{\circ} \cdot \Phi_E^{\,\mu_{4,E}/\mu_3} + (1 - s_E^{\circ})} \approx 1.021. \label{eq:shat_ice}
\end{align}
Non-eligible EVs lose approximately 48\% of their sales, while ICE vehicles gain roughly 2\% from the contraction of the electric segment. Eligible EVs are approximately unchanged: within-nest gains from the exit of NEV competitors are almost exactly offset by the reduction in the overall attractiveness of the electric nest.
 
\paragraph{Regional demand shifters.} Let $\omega_r^v$ denote the share of vehicle type $v \in \{\text{EEV}, \text{NEV}, \text{ICE}\}$ in region $r$'s pre-reform motor vehicle production. The region-specific demand shock is constructed as the production-weighted average of the counterfactual share ratios:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta_r}
    \frac{\delta_r^{\text{post}}}{\delta_r^{\text{pre}}} = \omega_r^{\text{EEV}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{EEV}} + \omega_r^{\text{NEV}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{NEV}} + \omega_r^{\text{ICE}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{ICE}}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:delta_r} generates cross-regional heterogeneity solely through the production composition $(\omega_r^{\text{EEV}}, \omega_r^{\text{NEV}}, \omega_r^{\text{ICE}})$: regions assembling a larger share of disqualified models experience a more negative demand shift, which then propagates upstream through the production network estimated in our structural model.
