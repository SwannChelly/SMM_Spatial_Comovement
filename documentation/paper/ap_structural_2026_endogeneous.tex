\label{apsec:structural}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The economy is divided into $R+1$ regions, populated by two types of firms: producers of a final good and producers of intermediate goods used as inputs in the production of the final good. The $R+1^{\text{th}}$ region represents the rest of the world. The analysis primarily focuses on the matching of firms across domestic regions, controlling for competition from foreign firms using their shares in total sales as reference. We assume that each domestic region hosts at most one representative firm in the downstream sector, identified by its region of origin. Intermediate goods are produced across $S$ sectors, with each region potentially hosting many suppliers in each sector. We define a market $(s,r)$ as the collection of firms located in region $r$ that belong to upstream sector $s$.\\

Domestic regions are partitioned into attraction areas,
$\mathcal{R}=\bigsqcup_{a\in\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{R}_{a}$, where $\mathcal{R}_{a}$ is the set of
regions whose nearest downstream producer is the anchor of area $a$, and $a(r')$ denotes the
area of region $r'$.


\paragraph{Demand for final manufacturing products.} Firms in the downstream sector each own the blueprint to produce a unique differentiated variety of the final good. The market for the final good is characterized by monopolistic competition and iso-elastic demand functions:
$$q_r=\left(\frac{p_r}{P}\right)^{\varepsilon-1}\frac{E}{P}\delta_r$$
where $q_r$ denotes the quantity demanded of the variety produced in region $r$ at price $p_r$, given nominal consumption $E$ and the downstream sector's price index $P$. $\varepsilon$ measures the price elasticity of sales. Finally, $\delta_r$ is an exogenous component of demand, that varies across downstream suppliers and will later be used to simulate demand shocks and their diffusion along production linkages. To simplify the analysis, we abstract from foreign demand and assume that the domestic market for final consumption goods is perfectly integrated. 



\paragraph{Technology in the downstream sector.} Production of final goods requires the assembly of intermediates, combined with labor, under a nested CES structure:
\begin{equation}
y_{r}=A_{r}\Big[\Omega_{L}^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\ell_{r}^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}}
+(1-\Omega_{L})^{\frac{1}{\lambda}}\big(q^{I}_{r}\big)^{\frac{\lambda-1}{\lambda}}\Big]^{\frac{\lambda}{\lambda-1}},
\end{equation}
where $\ell_{r}$ is labor demand and $q^{I}_{r}$ the firm's intermediate input consumption. The parameter $\Omega_{L}$ governs the share of labor in total production costs, while $\lambda$ is the elasticity of substitution between labor and intermediates. Firm productivity $A_{r}$ is exogenous. Intermediate consumption is itself a nested CES aggregator across sectors and, within each sector, across varieties:

\begin{equation}
q^{I}_{r}=\Big[\sum_{s=1}^{S}\Omega_{s}^{\frac{1}{\nu}}q_{rs}^{\frac{\nu-1}{\nu}}\Big]^{\frac{\nu}{\nu-1}},
\qquad
q_{rs}=\Big[\frac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_{s}}q_{rs}(\rho)^{\frac{\nu_{s}-1}{\nu_{s}}}\Big]^{\frac{\nu_{s}}{\nu_{s}-1}},
\label{eq:app-ces}
\end{equation}

where $\Omega_{s}$ governs the share of sector $s$ in intermediate consumption, $\nu$ is the elasticity of substitution across sectors and $\nu_{s}$ that across varieties within a sector.

The only change relative to the continuum formulation is the inner aggregator: the integral over a continuum of varieties is replaced by an average over the $N_{s}$ varieties. Writing it as an average rather than a sum is a normalization of the love-of-variety level, discussed and justified in Appendix "Equilibrium"~\ref{app:equilibrium}; it leaves every sourcing share, and hence the entire extensive margin, unchanged.

Given the nested CES structure, the nominal demand for intermediate products is
\begin{equation}
x_{rs}(\rho)=\Omega_{s}(1-\Omega_{L}) \left(\frac{p_{rs}(\rho)}{p_{rs}}\right)^{1-\nu_{s}} \left(\frac{p_{rs}}{p_{r}}\right)^{1-\nu} \left(\frac{p_{r}}{c_{r}}\right)^{1-\lambda}\frac{c_{r}y_{r}}{A_{r}},
\end{equation}

with $p_{rs}\equiv\big[\tfrac{1}{N_{s}}\sum_{\rho}p_{rs}(\rho)^{1-\nu_{s}}\big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_{s}}}$ the cost index for intermediates in sector $s$, $p_{r}\equiv\big[\sum_{s}\Omega_{s}p_{rs}^{1-\nu}\big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}$ the cost index for intermediates, and $c_{r}\equiv\big[\Omega_{L}w_{r}^{1-\lambda}+(1-\Omega_{L})p_{r}^{1-\lambda}\big]^{\frac{1}{1-\lambda}}$ the unit cost of the firm. In equilibrium $y_{r}=q_{r}$.


\paragraph{Technology in upstream sectors.} Intermediate goods are produced with labor using a constant-returns-to-scale technology. Wages in sector $s$ and region $r'$ are denoted $w_{r's}$ and taken as given. As in \cite{EatonKortum2002} we assume that varieties produced within and across regions are perfect substitutes and that productivity draws are independent. The departure from the continuum case is that each sector contains $N_{s}\in\mathbb{N}$ horizontally differentiated varieties $\rho=1,\dots,N_{s}$. Within each variety $\rho$ and each region $r'$ there is a continuum of firms whose productivities above $z$ form a Poisson process with mean $T_{r's}z^{-\theta}$. Draws are independent across varieties, regions and sectors.\\

The continuum of firms is what keeps the model tractable. The number of firms above $z$ in $(r',s,\rho)$ is Poisson with mean $T_{r's}z^{-\theta}$, so the most productive one satisfies 

\begin{equation}
\Pr\big(z_{r'\rho s}\le z\big)=\Pr(\text{no firm above }z)=e^{-T_{r's}z^{-\theta}},
\label{eq:app-champion}
\end{equation}

that is, $z_{r'\rho s}$ is exactly $\mathrm{Fréchet}(T_{r's},\theta)$. One Fréchet draw per (region, variety) pair is the champion of a continuum of firms. \\

In the baseline specification, comparative advantage is area-specific up to the density of firms:
\begin{equation}
T_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}\,n_{r's}^{\kappa}, \qquad r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a},
\label{eq:app-density}
\end{equation}
with one parameter $T_{as}$ per (sector, area), $n_{r's}$ the number of firms of sector $s$ observed in region $r'$ in 2019, held fixed, and $\kappa$ a density elasticity common to all sectors. $T_{as}$ is a productivity per firm, net of density; with $\kappa=1$ we recover the \cite{EatonKortum2002} reading in which $T$ counts potential producers, and a region without firm of the sector ($n_{r's}=0$) has $T_{r's}=0$. The density term matters because potential suppliers are concentrated around assembly sites: within an area, $n_{r's}$ is roughly halved between 0--50 km and beyond 200 km in both industries. A model with $T_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}$ would read this decline as a trade cost and overstate $\alpha$ --- with $n\propto d^{\delta}$ it would read a gradient $d^{-(\theta\alpha-\kappa\delta)}$ instead of $d^{-\theta\alpha}$ --- which in turn overstates the share of a shock retained near the shocked site and understates the share going to dense upstream areas, wherever the site is.

Area-specific comparative advantage does not make regions within an area interchangeable. Each runs its own Ricardian competition for each variety, and two regions at different distances from a buyer, or with different firm densities, have different win probabilities. What it removes is unobserved technological heterogeneity within an area, which is what allows the model to explain the same variation the extensive-margin regression uses: within an area, distance identifies $\alpha$ and density identifies $\kappa$.


\paragraph{The network draw.}\label{app:network-draw}
A realization of the network, denoted $\Omega$, is one draw of the champion productivities $\{z_{r'\rho s}\}$ for every region, variety and sector. It determines, for each buyer and each variety, which region wins the variety and at which price. With a continuum of varieties, the law of large numbers makes the realized network deterministic. With $N_s$ varieties it is not: two draws of $\Omega$ deliver different winners, and therefore different networks. We write $\mathbb{E}_\Omega$ for the expectation over realizations. The expectation of a realized object is its value in the \textit{expected network}.
 
We use the following convention. Probabilities, and objects evaluated at the expected price index, do not depend on $\Omega$. They are the \cite{EatonKortum2002} expressions, and we write them as usual. Sector-level objects that depend on which supplier wins each variety carry the argument $(\Omega)$. Variety-level objects, indexed by $\rho$, are realizations by nature and are not marked. Table~\ref{tab:app-draw} summarizes the classification.
\paragraph{Equilibrium.}\label{app:equilibrium}
% CHANGED: "variety by variety"
The resolution follows the same steps as in \cite{EatonKortum2002}, applied variety by variety. Given the Fréchet assumption, the minimum price at which suppliers from $(r',s)$ can serve market $r$ with a given variety is distributed according to
 
\begin{equation}
G_{r'rs}(p)=\Pr\Big(\frac{w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs}}{z_{r'\rho s}}\le p\Big)
=1-\exp\left\{-T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}p^\theta\right\}.
\end{equation}
 
Taking the minimum across potential supplier locations yields the distribution of the price at which a downstream firm in region $r$ buys a given variety:
\begin{equation}
G_{rs}(p) =1-\prod_{r'=1}^{R+1}\left[1-G_{r'rs}(p)\right] =1-\exp\left\{-\Phi_{rs}p^\theta\right\},
\end{equation}
 
where
\begin{equation}
\Phi_{rs} = \sum_{r'}T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta} = \sum_{a'}T_{a's} \sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a'}} n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}.
\label{eq:app-Phi}
\end{equation}
 
The probability that a downstream firm in region $r$ sources a given variety from market $(r',s)$ is therefore
\begin{equation}
\gamma_{r'rs} = \frac{ T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta} }{ \Phi_{rs} }.
\label{eq:app-share}
\end{equation}
 
These are probabilities over the draw $\Omega$. They do not depend on its realization, nor on $N_s$: each variety runs its own Ricardian competition, with the same odds.
 
\textit{Realized and expected sourcing shares.} In \cite{EatonKortum2002}, the sourcing probability coincide with the sourcing share. With a finite number of varieties, it only coincide in expectation. For a given network realization, each variety is won by a single region, the share of buyer $r$'s sector-$s$ spending going to region $r'$ is therefore a random variable:
\begin{equation}
\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)=\sum_{\rho=1}^{N_s} v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)\,\mathbf{1}\{r' \text{ wins } \rho \text{ for } r| \Omega \}.
\label{eq:app-omega}
\end{equation}

Under the Fréchet assumption, the distribution of the winning price does not depend on the identity of the winner, $G_{r'rs}(p)=G_{rs}(p)$. Draws are independent across varieties, so the winners are independent of the winning prices. The weights $v_{rs}$ depend only on prices, the indicators only on winners, and $\sum_\rho v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)=1$. Hence
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)\big]=\gamma_{r'rs},
\qquad
\operatorname{Var}_\Omega\big(\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)\big)=V_s\,\gamma_{r'rs}(1-\gamma_{r'rs}),
\qquad
V_s=\mathbb{E}_\Omega\Big[\sum_\rho v_{rs}(\rho,\Omega)^2\Big]\ge \frac{1}{N_s}.
\label{eq:app-expected-share}
\end{equation}
The sourcing probability $\gamma_{r'rs}$ thus describes the expected network, for any $N_s$. The variance measures how far a realization departs from it. It vanishes as $N_s\to\infty$, where realized and expected networks coincide and the model collapses to \cite{EatonKortum2002}.
 
Following \citet{lenoir2023search}, we assume that downstream firms take sector-level decisions on the expected value of the sectoral price index rather than on the realization of the finite-variety CES aggregate. Within the sector, spending is allocated across varieties at realized prices, through $v_{rs}(\rho)$. The sectoral index is therefore the Eaton-Kortum price index: \swann{Ici c'est important d'avoir en tête que dans le papier avec Clémence l'espérance est utilisée pour l'aléa correspondant au tirage des fournisseurs potentiels. Là $\Omega$ est le tirage des productivités (on tire un nombre fini d'entreprises)}
 
\begin{equation}
p_{rs} \equiv \Big[\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[p_{rs}(\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}\big]\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_s}} = \Bigg[ \Gamma\Big(\frac{\theta+1-\nu_s}{\theta}\Big) \Bigg]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu_s}} \Phi_{rs}^{-1/\theta}.
\label{eq:app-pindex}
\end{equation}
 
Because the within-sector aggregator is an average, $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[p_{rs}(\Omega)^{1-\nu_s}]=\mathbb{E}_\Omega[p_{rs}(\rho)^{1-\nu_s}]$ does not depend on $N_s$. Sector spending $x_{rs}$ therefore depends neither on $\Omega$ nor on $N_s$.
 
The finite number of varieties affects the level of the CES price index through a standard love-of-variety term. With a sum rather than an average over the $N_s$ varieties, the sectoral CES price index would include a multiplicative factor $N_s^{1/(1-\nu_s)}$. Writing the within-sector aggregator as an average normalizes this term to one. Equivalently, this amounts to rescaling the technology parameter according to
 
\begin{equation}
T_{as}^{\text{avg}} = N_s^{\frac{\theta}{\nu_s-1}}T_{as}^{\text{sum}} .
\end{equation}


This normalization does not affect any allocation result: the sourcing probability in \eqref{eq:app-share} depends only on ratios of $T_{as}$ within a sector, so the extensive margin, the relative probability of sourcing across areas, and the moments used for estimation are unchanged. The normalization therefore only fixes the price-level gauge of the model.
 
The remaining price indices are
 
\begin{equation}
p_{r} = \Big[\sum_s\Omega_s p_{rs}^{1-\nu}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\nu}}, \qquad c_r = \Big[\Omega_Lw_r^{1-\lambda} +(1-\Omega_L)p_r^{1-\lambda}\Big]^{\frac{1}{1-\lambda}} .
\end{equation}
 
Finally, realized bilateral intermediate input purchases are $X_{r'rs}(\Omega)=\omega_{r'rs}(\Omega)\,x_{rs}$. Their expectation is the \cite{EatonKortum2002} flow:
 
\begin{equation}\label{eq:input_flows}
X_{r'rs} \equiv \mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[X_{r'rs}(\Omega)\big] = \gamma_{r'rs}x_{rs} = \gamma_{r'rs} \Omega_s(1-\Omega_L) \left(\frac{p_{rs}}{p_r}\right)^{1-\nu} \left(\frac{p_r}{c_r}\right)^{1-\lambda} \frac{c_ry_r}{A_r}.
\end{equation}
 
The expected flows describe the expected network. Statistics that are linear in the flows, such as area sourcing shares or the average sourcing distance, take the same value in the expected network and on average across realizations. Nonlinear statistics, such as concentration indices, do not: they depend on the dispersion of realizations around the expected network.
 
 
\paragraph{The extensive margin of trade.}\label{app:cloglog} To estimate the elasticity of trade cost to the distance we use the extensive margin of trade. Taking the perspective of the supplier, the probability of being the lowest-cost supplier in market $(r,s)$ for a given variety, given location $r'$ and productivity $z$, is
\begin{equation}
\rho_{r'rs}(z)=e^{-\Phi_{rs}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{\theta}z^{-\theta}}
=e^{-T_{r's}\gamma^{-1}_{r'rs}z^{-\theta}} .
\label{eq:app-rho}
\end{equation}
 
Like $\gamma_{r'rs}$, this is a probability over the competitors' draws. It depends neither on the realization $\Omega$ nor on $N_s$.
 
In the limit where there is only one downstream region ($r = a(r')$), the elasticity of trade cost to the distance can be recovered directly from the supply dummy. Indeed, Equation~\eqref{eq:app-rho} implies that $\Pr(\text{no supply} \mid z) = 1 - \exp(-\exp(\eta))$ where $\eta = \ln \Phi_{a(r')s} + \theta \ln w_{r's} + \theta \alpha \ln d_{r'a(r')} - \theta \ln z$. Consequently, the coefficient on the distance of a complementary log log design is the product $\alpha\theta$:
 
$$
\Pr(\text{no supply} \mid z) = 1 - \exp(-\exp(FE_{a(r')s} + \theta \alpha \ln d_{r'a(r')} - \theta \ln z))
$$

This firm-level regression has no exact model counterpart. In the data, a firm supplies if it is the most productive firm of its variety in its region \emph{and} if it wins the variety; the model only describes the first firm, the champion of \eqref{eq:app-champion}, and the probability of being that champion, which depends on the density of the region, has no equivalent in it. We therefore target its cell-level analogue: one observation per (region, sector) cell, and the outcome $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$, ``region $r'$ hosts at least one supplier'', which is defined identically in the data and in the model. Section~\ref{app:cloglog-count} below shows that $\Pr(K_{r's}>0)=1-(1-p_{r's})^{N_s}$. With a single buyer, $p_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}d_{r'a}^{\alpha})^{-\theta}/\Phi_{as}$, and the cloglog delivers a decomposition linear in $\ln d$ and $\ln n$:
\begin{equation}
\ln\big(-\ln \Pr(K_{r's}=0)\big)=\ln N_s+\ln\big(-\ln(1-p_{r's})\big)\simeq FE_{a(r')s}+\kappa\ln n_{r's}-\theta\alpha\ln d_{r'a(r')},
\label{eq:app-cell-cloglog}
\end{equation}
where the approximation $-\ln(1-p)\simeq p$ is very good because $p_{r's}$ is small. The sector$\times$area fixed effect absorbs $T_{a(r')s}$, $\Phi_{as}$ and $\ln N_s$, so in population the coefficients do not depend on the variety count --- a property of the cloglog link that a logit or a linear probability model would not have.\footnote{We prefer the cloglog on presence to a PPML on the number of suppliers. The model counts varieties won, the data count firms, and several firms of a dense region can supply the same variety. The number of suppliers absorbs this multiplicity; presence does not.} We collect the two coefficients in
\begin{equation}
\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_{distance},\ \beta_{\kappa}),
\label{eq:app-beta}
\end{equation}
where $\beta_{distance}$, the coefficient on $\ln d_{r'a(r')}$ (or the distance bins), identifies $\alpha$, and $\beta_{\kappa}$, the coefficient on $\ln n_{r's}$, identifies $\kappa$. Both are read on the variation within an area, where $T_{a(r')s}$ is fixed and only trade costs and density can move the probability of supplying. With distance bins, $\ln\tau$ is piecewise linear in $\ln d$ with one elasticity $\alpha_k$ per segment between bin centres, $\alpha_1\le\dots\le\alpha_K$, which nests $\tau=d^{\alpha}$.

 
\paragraph{Granularity and the count moment.}\label{app:cloglog-count} The finite number of varieties $N_s$ allows us to endogenize the occurrence of regions with no suppliers. Let $p_{r's}$ be the probability that region $r'$ wins a given variety of sector $s$ for at least one downstream buyer:
\begin{equation}
p_{r's}\equiv\Pr\Big(\bigcup_{r}\{r' \text{ wins } \rho \text{ for } r\}\Big).
\label{eq:app-pwin}
\end{equation}
Like $\gamma_{r'rs}$, it does not depend on $\Omega$. It has no tractable closed form (see Section~\ref{app:model-moments}). The win events of $r'$ across buyers share the same champion draw, so they are positively dependent and $p_{r's}\le 1-\prod_r(1-\gamma_{r'rs})$. We evaluate it by simulation.
 
The number of firms in $(r',s)$ that supply the downstream industry is the number of varieties that $r'$ wins for at least one buyer. It depends on the draw. Varieties are independent, so
\begin{equation}
K_{r's}(\Omega)\sim\mathrm{Bin}\big(N_s,p_{r's}\big),
\qquad\text{so}\qquad
\Pr\big(K_{r's}(\Omega)>0\big)=1-(1-p_{r's})^{N_s}.
\label{eq:app-K}
\end{equation}
This is the probability that enters the cell-level regression \eqref{eq:app-cell-cloglog}.

Considering $\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}$ as the set of regions within active attraction areas, the empirical moment is the share of those regions hosting no suppliers, $\bar G_{s}(0)(\Omega)=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}|}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}}\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}(\Omega)=0\}$. The data provide one realization. Its expectation is
 
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[\bar G_{s}(0)(\Omega)\big]=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}|}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^{+}_{s}}(1-p_{r's})^{N_{s}}.
\label{eq:app-count}
\end{equation}
 
At fixed $p_{r's}$, this moment is decreasing in $N_s$, which allows for the identification of the variety count. \\
 
Furthermore, because iceberg trade costs do not alter the relative ranking of firms within a region, each variety generates exactly one distinct supplying firm per winning origin. If $N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s}$ is the number of distinct upstream firms observed to supply the downstream industry, then $N_{s}$ must fall within the range defined by:
\begin{equation}
\frac{N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s}}{R^{D}}\;\le\;N_{s}\;\le\;N^{\mathrm{obs}}_{s},
\label{eq:app-bounds}
\end{equation}
where $R^{D}$ is the number of downstream buyers. The upper bound is reached when every variety is globally sourced from a single origin, while the lower bound occurs when each of the $R^{D}$ buyers sources each variety from a different origin.\footnote{We don't impose a lower bound in the estimation. Since supplier information is recorded at the SIREN level, a multi-establishment firm may generate several establishment-level observations, although not all establishments necessarily supply the downstream industry. Hence $N_s^{obs}$ may overstate the number of model-specific supplier champions and therefore the lower bound. If the true $N_s$ lies below this bound, the constraint prevents the extensive-margin moment from adjusting through, pushing $\alpha$ toward zero.} \swann{Ca c'est la théorie. \subsection{Structural estimation: the moments}
\label{app:model-moments}

We use six sets of theoretical moments, observed in the data using a combination of IO tables and micro-level data.

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Aggregate labor share at the level of the industry:}
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde\Omega_{L}\equiv\frac{\sum_{r}w_{r}\ell_{r}}{\sum_{r}c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}
    =\sum_{r}\frac{c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}{\sum_{r}c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}\,\Omega_{L}
    \left(\frac{w_{r}}{c_{r}}\right)^{1-\lambda}.
    \end{equation}
    In the model it is a sales-weighted average of firm-level labor shares. In the data we use the     share of labor costs in total costs from IO tables.
    
    \item \textbf{Aggregate share of sector $s$ in the industry's input purchases:}
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde\Omega_{s}\equiv\frac{\sum_{r}p_{rs}q_{rs}}{\sum_{r}\sum_{s}p_{rs}q_{rs}}
    =\sum_{r}\frac{p_{r}q^{I}_{r}}{\sum_{r}p_{r}q^{I}_{r}}\,\Omega_{s}
    \left(\frac{p_{rs}}{p_{r}}\right)^{1-\nu}.
    \end{equation}
    In the data we use technical coefficients for the downstream industry from IO tables.
    
    \item \textbf{The share of each region in downstream sales:}
    \begin{equation}
    \pi_{r}\equiv\frac{c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}{\sum_{r}c_{r}A_{r}^{-1}y_{r}}     =\left(\frac{p_{r}}{P}\right)^{-\varepsilon}     =\frac{(c_{r}A_{r}^{-1})^{-\varepsilon}}{\sum_{r}(c_{r}A_{r}^{-1})^{-\varepsilon}},
    \end{equation}
    recovered from sales data on the population of downstream producers.
    
    \item \textbf{The extensive-margin coefficients} $\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_{distance},\beta_{\kappa})$ of \eqref{eq:app-beta}: the cell-level cloglog \eqref{eq:app-cell-cloglog} of $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ on the distance to the anchor of the area, in logs or in the distance bins of Equation~\eqref{eq:reduced_form_micro}, and on $\ln n_{r's}$, with sector$\times$area fixed effects; groups without variation in the outcome are dropped. The regression set $\mathcal{A}$ is the cells of the active attraction areas, on both the model and the data side.
    
    \item \textbf{Area-aggregated sourcing shares.} The share of the downstream industry's purchases of sector $s$ sourced from area $a$:
    \begin{equation}
    \tilde\gamma_{as}\equiv\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a}}\frac{\sum_{r}X_{r'rs}}{\sum_{r}\sum_{r''}X_{r''rs}}
    =\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a}}\sum_{r}\frac{\sum_{r''}X_{r''rs}}{\sum_{r''}\sum_{r}X_{r''rs}}\,\gamma_{r'rs}.
    \label{eq:app-gamma-area}
    \end{equation}
    In the data we reconstitute firm-level sales to the downstream industry using sales-share data, $x_{i(r',s)d}=\mathbf{1}(\mathrm{Sup}_{i(r',s)})\,a^{D}_{d\,i(r',s)}\,x_{i(s,r')}$, sum over all firms in the area and normalize by the sum over all firms from sector $s$. The aggregation runs over \emph{every} region of the area, including those hosting no supplier, on both the model and the data side.
    
    \item \textbf{The count moment} $\bar G_{s}(0)$ of \eqref{eq:app-count}: the share of regions inside active attraction areas that host no supplier of sector $s$.
\end{enumerate}

Moments 1--3 and 5 are the same objects as in the continuum version of the model, with 5 now aggregated to the area. Moments 4 and 6 are what the granular structure adds.

The two groups differ in how their expectation can be computed. Moments 1--3 and 5 are \emph{linear in the network}. Because buyers set sector spending on the expected price index \eqref{eq:app-pindex}, moments 1--3 do not depend on $\Omega$ at all, and by \eqref{eq:app-expected-share} the expected area sourcing share equals its value in the expected network, for any $N_s$:
\begin{equation}
\mathbb{E}_\Omega\big[\gamma_{as}(\Omega)\big]=\sum_r \frac{x_{rs}}{\sum_{r''}x_{r''s}}\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_a}\gamma_{r'rs}.
\label{eq:app-gamma-linear}
\end{equation}
Moment 6 is not linear in the network --- it is a product of indicators over varieties --- but its expectation \eqref{eq:app-count} is a closed-form function of $N_s$ and of the win-anywhere probabilities $p_{r's}$. Moment 4 has neither property: the cloglog coefficients are a nonlinear function of a whole realized network, so $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$ differs from the coefficient of the expected network and must be computed by Monte Carlo.\footnote{This is the point made by \cite{gaubertitskhohi2021}, p.~8: ``the population mean $\Phi_z$ is not, in general, equivalent to the asymptotic value of $\Lambda_z$ as $N_z\to\infty$''.} The probabilities $p_{r's}$ themselves have a closed form by inclusion--exclusion over the set of buyers, $p_{r's}=\sum_{S\neq\emptyset}(-1)^{|S|+1}P_{r'}(S)$ with $P_{r'}(S)=T_{r's}/\big(T_{r's}+\sum_{k\neq r'}T_{ks}\max_{r\in S}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'r}/w_{ks}\tau_{kr})^{\theta}\big)$, but it has $2^{R^D}-1$ terms, which is out of reach with twenty buyers. This is why the estimator is a simulated method of moments.

\paragraph{Notes on foreign parameters.}
\label{app:model-foreign}
We do not have the data to estimate parameters relating to foreign firms. Instead we take the share of foreign inputs in sourcing as an exogenous parameter used in the simulated data, so the vector of parameters to be estimated excludes region $R+1$. Moments 1--4 and 6 do not involve $R+1$. For moment 5, with $w_{R+1,s}$ the share of foreign inputs in sector $s$'s input purchases,
\begin{equation}
\tilde\gamma_{as}=(1-w_{R+1,s})\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_{a}}\sum_{r} \frac{\sum_{r''\neq R+1}X_{r''rs}}{\sum_{r''\neq R+1}\sum_{r}X_{r''rs}}\,\tilde\gamma_{r'rs}, \qquad
\tilde\gamma_{r'rs}=\frac{T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}}{\tilde\Phi_{rs}},
\end{equation}
with $\tilde\Phi_{rs}=\sum_{r'=1}^{R}T_{r's}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}$. Foreign competition still enters the win probabilities through $\Phi_{rs}$ in \eqref{eq:app-Phi}; what is not estimated is foreign comparative advantage.

\paragraph{Calibrated parameters.}
\label{app:model-calibration}
The remaining parameters $\{\varepsilon,\theta,\lambda,\nu,\{\nu_{s}\},\{w_{r's}\}\}$ are calibrated, and the firm counts $\{n_{r's}\}$ are observed. We use the estimates from \cite{Fontagn2022} for $\varepsilon$: the sales elasticity for motor vehicles and aerospace is respectively $-9$ and $-16$. The shape $\theta$ of the Fréchet distribution is calibrated following \cite{antras_margins_2017}. Elasticities of substitution in production follow the literature, notably \cite{baqaee_macroeconomic_2019}: $\lambda=0.5$ for the elasticity between labor and intermediate inputs and $\nu=0.9$ across sectors. Local wages are calibrated using labor cost information from employer--employee data in 2019 (DADS/BTS). The number of firms $n_{r's}$ of each sector in each region is counted in the same year and held fixed, consistently with the short-run horizon of the counterfactuals.



\subsection{Estimation}
\label{app:model-estimation}

The model's parameters are

\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\big\{\alpha,\;\kappa\big\}}_{\text{searched over}}
\;\cup\;
\underbrace{\big\{\{T_{as}\},\;\Omega_{L},\;\{\Omega_{s}\},\;\{A_{r}\},\;\{N_{s}\}\big\}}_{\text{calibrated within the model}},
\label{eq:app-param-split}
\end{equation}

but only the first group is searched over. Comparative advantage, the downstream technology parameters $(\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\})$ and the variety count are calibrated within the model: given any candidate value of $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$, each is pinned by the model itself rather than left free. Comparative advantage is pinned by the area sourcing shares, the downstream technology parameters by the labor share, the sector shares and the regional sales shares, and the variety count by the share of empty regions. They are not fixed ahead of the estimation at some external target, and they are not searched over either --- they are recovered, inside the simulation of the economy that $\mu$ implies. This is exact rather than approximate: minimizing the criterion jointly over all parameters and minimizing it over $\mu$ with the other parameters set to their within-model values give the same estimate. Since these moments are matched exactly, the parameters searched over are identified by the extensive-margin coefficients $\boldsymbol\beta$ alone: $\beta_{distance}$ by the decline of supplier presence with distance within an area, $\beta_\kappa$ by its rise with density within an area. We describe first how an economy is simulated at a given $\mu$, then the search over $\mu$.

\subsubsection{Simulation algorithm.} Every evaluation of the criterion is solved in the same order. Given the calibrated elasticities and a candidate parameter vector, we calibrate comparative advantage and the downstream technology, simulate the Ricardian competition to obtain the win-anywhere probabilities, calibrate the variety count, and assemble the moments:
\begin{equation}
\mu=\big\{\alpha,\;\kappa\big\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\big\{\{T_{as}\},\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\}\big\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\{p_{r's}\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\{N_{s}\}
\;\longrightarrow\;\tilde M(\mu) .
\label{eq:app-recursion}
\end{equation}
The arrows run one way only. Comparative advantage is calibrated without knowing the variety count, and the variety count does not revise comparative advantage, so there is no fixed point between $T$ and $N$ to solve --- a recursion, not a simultaneous system. Bellow we explain each step of this algorithm for which the pseudo-code is given in Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate}.

\paragraph{Step 1: comparative advantage and downstream technology.}
For a given $\mu$, we recover the area-level productivity parameters $T_{as}$ that exactly match the observed sourcing shares. Given trade costs $\tau_{r'rs}=d_{r'r}^{\alpha}$ and the density elasticity $\kappa$, this is a matrix-scaling problem which pseudo-code is in Algorithm~\ref{alg:invertT}. We solve it using the multiplicative update

\begin{equation}
T^{+}_{as} = T_{as} \left( \frac{\tilde\gamma_{as}} {\gamma_{as}(T)} \right)^{\varsigma}, \qquad \varsigma\in(0,1], \label{eq:app-sinkhorn}
\end{equation}

where $\tilde\gamma_{as}$ are the observed sourcing shares. The normalization of one reference area per sector removes the indeterminacy in the scale of $T_{as}$. The uniqueness of this inversion follows from the structure of the sourcing matrix. Holding expenditure weights fixed, the model-implied shares can be written as

\begin{equation}
\gamma_{as} = \sum_r \omega_{rs} \frac{T_{as}B_{ar}} {\sum_{a'}T_{a's}B_{a'r}}, \qquad B_{ar} = \sum_{r'\in\mathcal R_a} n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs})^{-\theta}.
\end{equation}

Because trade costs are finite, every area can potentially serve every buyer, so $B$ is a strictly positive matrix; the density term only changes its entries, and a region without firm of the sector simply drops out of $B_{ar}$. The recovery of $T_{as}$ is therefore equivalent to a Sinkhorn matrix-scaling problem. The positivity of $B$ implies that the associated scaling operator is a contraction. Consequently, the fixed point exists and is unique up to a sector-specific multiplicative normalization. The reference-area normalization removes this remaining degree of freedom, implying that there is a unique vector of comparative advantages consistent with the observed sourcing shares.

The expenditure weights $\omega_{rs}$ depend on prices and sales, hence on $T$ and on the downstream technology parameters, which are calibrated in the same loop. Given $T$, the expected price indices $p_{rs}$ of \eqref{eq:app-pindex} do not depend on $(\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\})$, and these parameters are just-identified by moments 1--3. Writing $s_{rs}=\Omega_sp_{rs}^{1-\nu}/\sum_k\Omega_kp_{rk}^{1-\nu}$ for the sector split of intermediate spending and $\ell_r=\Omega_L(w_r/c_r)^{1-\lambda}$ for the labor cost share, $\Omega_L$ and $\{\Omega_s\}$ solve
\begin{equation}
\sum_r\hat\pi_r\ell_r=\tilde\Omega_L,
\qquad
\frac{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}}{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)}=\tilde\Omega_s,
\label{eq:app-tech}
\end{equation}
jointly, by Newton's method, since the weights $\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)$ move with $\{\Omega_s\}$ through $p_r$. Downstream productivity then follows in closed form from sales, $\pi_r\propto\delta_r(c_r/A_r)^{\varepsilon}$, that is $A_r\propto c_r(\hat\pi_r/\delta_r)^{-1/\varepsilon}$ with $A_1=1$. Because sales are held at their observed shares $\hat\pi_r$, the expenditure weights are $\omega_{rs}=\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}$ and do not involve $A_r$. Each iteration of \eqref{eq:app-sinkhorn} therefore computes the price indices at the current $T$, solves \eqref{eq:app-tech} at these prices, updates the weights and then $T$. This introduces a feedback from $T$ into expenditure shares, so the full algorithm is a fixed-point iteration. We use damping in \eqref{eq:app-sinkhorn} and stop when both sourcing shares and comparative-advantage parameters converge. Holding sales at their target also removes the demand loop through which, with fixed downstream technology, a higher $T$ lowers input prices, raises sales and sends demand back to the same area. At the estimated parameters, the inversion converges rapidly from dispersed starting values and recovers the calibrated productivity parameters to numerical precision. A candidate $\mu$ for which it does not converge is rejected rather than evaluated. \swann{Le paramètre de damping est fixé à 0.5. Au départ j'avais mis 0.9 mais quand on a une élasticité de la demande forte comme dans l'aéronautique, la convergence du point fixe n'était pas possible car il y avait beaucoup d'oscillation autour du point fixe.}

\paragraph{Step 2: Win probabilities.}
For each sector, we simulate $m$ varieties and draw one Fréchet champion for each (region, variety) pair \footnote{The uniform draws entering the inverse-CDF transformation $\varphi_{r'\rho s}=T_{r's}^{1/\theta}(-\log u_{r'\rho s})^{-1/\theta}$ are generated using scrambled Sobol sequences rather than independent pseudo-random draws. Scrambled Sobol sequences are low-discrepancy sequences commonly used for variance reduction, improving the convergence rate of simulation-based estimators and reducing simulation noise. We construct one low-dimensional sequence per sector, with regions as dimensions and varieties as points. This preserves the efficiency of low-discrepancy sampling while avoiding the deterioration of its properties in very high dimensions. Since Ricardian competition compares productivity draws only within sectors, using the same construction across sectors does not affect the allocation of winners. We use independent pseudo-random draws when simulating the fake worlds used for the weighting matrix and for inference, where independence across replications is required.
\swann{Si ce n'est pas clair il ne faut pas hésiter à me demander. C'est un point important que je souhaite avoir dans le papier.}}. Running the Ricardian competition described in \eqref{eq:app-champion} yields the number of simulated varieties won by each region, $k_{r's}$, and the corresponding win-anywhere probability $p_{r's}=k_{r's}/m$.

The simulation size $m$ is only a Monte Carlo device and does not correspond to the model-implied number of varieties $N_s$. This distinction is important because winning a variety depends only on relative production costs: $$ \frac{w_{r's}\tau_{r'rs}}{z_{r'\rho s}} < \min_{r''\neq r'} \frac{w_{r''s}\tau_{r''rs}}{z_{r''\rho s}} . $$ Hence $p_{r's}$ is determined by comparative advantage, density, wages, trade costs and the productivity distribution, but not by the number of varieties, prices or downstream expenditure. It is an object of the expected network, and the number of simulated varieties can therefore be chosen independently of the equilibrium value of $N_s$, provided it is large enough to estimate $p_{r's}$ precisely.

\paragraph{Step 3: Variety count.}
The number of varieties is identified from the probability that a region does not supply any variety in a sector. For a sector with $N_s$ varieties, the population count moment is \eqref{eq:app-count}. In the simulation, we estimate this moment using the winning matrix generated in Step 2. Let $k_{r's}$ denote the number of simulated varieties for which region $r'$ is the winning supplier somewhere. For a candidate number of varieties $n$, we use the combinatorial estimator

\begin{equation}
\tilde G_s(n) = \frac{1}{|\mathcal R_s^+|} \sum_{r'\in\mathcal R_s^+} \frac{\binom{m-k_{r's}}{n}} {\binom{m}{n}} . \label{eq:app-Gclosed}
\end{equation}

For a given simulation draw, the ratio inside the sum is the fraction of subsets of $n$ simulated varieties that are not supplied by region $r'$. Taking expectations over the simulated productivity draws gives

$$ \mathbb{E} \left[ \frac{\binom{m-k_{r's}}{n}} {\binom{m}{n}} \right] = (1-p_{r's})^{n}, $$

so that $\tilde G_s(n)$ is an unbiased estimator of the population moment in \eqref{eq:app-count}\footnote{A natural alternative is to estimate $p_{r's}$ by $\hat p_{r's}=k_{r's}/m$ and use the plug-in estimator $(1-\hat p_{r's})^{N_s}$. Although this estimator is consistent as $m\rightarrow\infty$, it is biased in finite simulations because the transformation $p\mapsto(1-p)^{N_s}$ is nonlinear. In particular, a second-order expansion gives $$ \mathbb{E}\big[(1-\hat p_{r's})^{N_s}\big] -(1-p_{r's})^{N_s} \simeq \frac{N_s(N_s-1)}{2m} p_{r's}(1-p_{r's})^{N_s-1}, $$ which is positive. The combinatorial estimator uses the same simulated winning matrix but avoids applying this nonlinear transformation to the noisy probability estimate, delivering an unbiased estimator of the count moment for any finite $m$.}.

The variety count is then obtained by matching the simulated and empirical count moments:
\begin{equation}
\widehat N_s = \arg\min_{N\in[N_s^{\mathrm{lo}},N_s^{\mathrm{hi}}]\cap\mathbb N} \left(\tilde G_s(N)-\widehat G_s(0)\right)^2 . \label{eq:app-profile}
\end{equation}
Since $\tilde G_s(n)$ is decreasing in $n$, this minimization is a monotone integer search and does not require re-simulating the Ricardian competition for alternative values of $N_s$. The pseudo-algorithm for this step is Algorithm~\ref{alg:countN}.

\paragraph{Step 4: the moments.} All six blocks of Section~\ref{app:model-moments} are computed from the single Ricardian solve of step 2: the value blocks 1--3 and 5 from the realized trade flows, block 6 from \eqref{eq:app-Gclosed} evaluated at $\widehat N_{s}$, and block 4 as follows. The model counterpart of the observed coefficients is $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$: the data are one realization of a network with $N_s$ varieties, not the expected network. We estimate it by Monte Carlo at almost no cost. Once the Ricardian competition is solved on the $m$ simulated varieties, it is as if the network had been simulated once with $m\gg N_s$ varieties. We draw $J=100$ networks among them, network $j$ being the first $\widehat N_s$ entries of a fixed random permutation of the $m$ varieties of each sector; on each, the outcome $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ is whether region $r'$ wins one of these varieties for at least one buyer, and the cloglog \eqref{eq:app-cell-cloglog} is run exactly as on the data. Block 4 is the average of the $J$ coefficient vectors,
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)=\frac{1}{J}\sum_{j=1}^{J}\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(j)}(\mu),
\label{eq:app-beta-mc}
\end{equation}
fits that fail to converge being excluded and counted. Averaging the statistic over networks, rather than computing the statistic of the average network, is what makes $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}$ an estimator of $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$; the networks are nested in $N_s$, so a change in the variety count adds or removes varieties rather than redrawing the network. Pseudo-code is in Algorithm~\ref{alg:cellbeta}.


\subsubsection{Estimation algorithm.} The SMM estimator is defined as

\begin{equation}
\hat\mu=\text{argmin}_{\mu}\;\big(\tilde M(\mu)-\hat M\big)'W\big(\tilde M(\mu)-\hat M\big),
\label{eq:app-smm}
\end{equation}

where $\tilde M$ (resp.\ $\hat M$) denotes the vector of theoretical (resp.\ empirical) moments, and $W$ is a weighting matrix. Moments 1--3, 5 and 6 are matched by the parameters calibrated within the model, so the criterion effectively compares the simulated and observed extensive-margin coefficients, $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)$ and $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}$.\footnote{Moments 1--3 are matched at the expected price indices, while the simulated moments use the realized price indices of the $m$ simulated varieties; the gap is pure Monte Carlo error, which the search must not chase, so moments 1--3 are left out of the criterion. Moments 5 and 6 are kept in the criterion of the first step, where they are matched up to Monte Carlo error and the integer step of $N_s$.} Optimal weights assign lower importance to imprecisely estimated moments; we construct them below. The minimization is carried out by Particle Swarm Optimization \citep{pso}, which explores the parameter space through parallel evaluation, with adaptive inertia weights declining from $0.9$ to $0.4$ over iterations and cognitive and social parameters set to $1.5$ and $2.5$ (see Algorithm~\ref{alg:pso}). The estimation proceeds in four steps (see Algorithm~\ref{alg:smm}).

\begin{enumerate}

\item \textbf{A first fit under equal weights.} Starting values are set as follows: $\alpha$ at the extensive-margin distance coefficient divided by $\theta$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:cloglog}); $\kappa$ at the coefficient on $\ln n_{r's}$; and $T_{as}$ at the closed-form inversion of the sourcing shares at these values, with expenditure proxied by the observed sales shares. With distance bins, the starting elasticities $\alpha_k$ are the slopes of $\ln\tau_b=-\hat\beta_b/\theta$ between bin centres. We run the optimizer over $\mu$ with $W$ set to the identity, then refine it with the swarm restarted from the incumbent in a box around it whose size is updated across iterations. This yields $\hat\mu_{1}$.

\item \textbf{The weighting matrix.} At $\hat\mu_{1}$ we simulate fake worlds from the model and set $W$ to the inverse of the covariance of the residual between the coefficients of a fake world and their simulated counterpart, described in the next paragraph:
\begin{equation}
W=\hat\Sigma_u^{-1}.
\label{eq:app-W}
\end{equation}

\item \textbf{The efficient fit.} We re-estimate the model with the updated weighting matrix,
initializing at $\hat\mu_{1}$, to obtain $\hat\mu_{2}$.

\item \textbf{Inference.} At $\hat\mu_{2}$ we compute the standard errors by parametric bootstrap, described below.

\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{The variance--covariance matrix of the moments.}
The weighting matrix is constructed from the uncertainty of both the empirical and simulated moments. The SMM compares two objects, each with its own source of randomness. Writing $\bar M(\mu)=\mathbb{E}_\Omega[M(\mu,\Omega)]$, and $U$ for the draws of the simulator,
\begin{equation}
\hat M=\bar M(\mu^*)+M_{gran},\qquad \tilde M(\mu,U)=\bar M(\mu)+M_{sim}(\mu,U).
\end{equation}
The first noise is granular: nature has drawn one network with $N_s$ varieties. The second is Monte Carlo error: the simulator estimates $\bar M(\mu)$ with a finite number of draws. Both have mean zero and are independent, and the first dominates by far, since a moment of one network with $N_s$ varieties is compared to an average over $m\gg N_s$ simulated varieties and $J$ networks. We abstract from measurement error in the data.

A bootstrap on firms would measure the granular noise poorly: it can remove suppliers from a region but never add one, so a region observed without supplier stays without supplier in every replication. We instead draw the granular noise from the model. The noise also reaches $\boldsymbol\beta$ through the parameters calibrated within the model: $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ are calibrated on the realized sourcing shares, sales and empty-region shares, which come from the same network. To first order the residual of the criterion is
\begin{equation}
\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)-\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}\simeq h_\alpha(\mu-\mu^*)-u+\boldsymbol\beta_{sim},\qquad u=\boldsymbol\beta_{gran}-h_\gamma\gamma_{gran}-h_GG_{gran},
\end{equation}
where $h(\mu,\gamma,G)$ is the expected $\boldsymbol\beta$ when the other parameters are calibrated on $(\gamma,G)$. The optimal weighting is $W=\Sigma_u^{-1}$, with $\Sigma_u=\operatorname{Var}(u)+\operatorname{Var}(\boldsymbol\beta_{sim})$, which differs from the variance of $\boldsymbol\beta_{gran}$ because part of the granular noise in $\boldsymbol\beta$ is absorbed by the recalibration.

We estimate $\Sigma_u$ without computing any derivative. At $\hat\mu_1$, we draw $B_1=1{,}000$ fake worlds: one network $\Omega_b$ with $\widehat N_s$ varieties per sector, drawn with independent pseudo-random numbers. On each, we compute the data moments exactly as on the data --- one cloglog fit $\boldsymbol\beta_b$, the empty-region shares $G_b$, the area sourcing shares $\gamma_b$, the labor share, the sector shares and the sales shares --- recalibrate $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ on them, and simulate $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$ at $\hat\mu_1$ with a simulator $U_b$ proper to the world. The residual
\begin{equation}
e_b=\boldsymbol\beta_b-\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}\big(\hat\mu_1;\,T_b,\Omega_{L,b},\Omega_{s,b},A_b,N_b,U_b\big)
\end{equation}
is to first order $u_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$, so $\hat\Sigma_u=\widehat{\operatorname{Cov}}(e_1,\dots,e_{B_1})$. The world's simulator must have its own seed: if it reused the draws of $\Omega_b$, it would reproduce the fake data exactly and the dispersion would vanish; if it were common to all worlds, the simulation noise would be omitted. Worlds in which the cloglog cannot be estimated, or in which a reference area wins nothing, are excluded and counted. $W$ is a square matrix of the dimension of $\boldsymbol\beta$. When $\boldsymbol\beta$ has as many entries as $\mu$ (one distance coefficient), the system is exactly identified and $W$ affects the inference only; it moves the point estimate when distance is binned.

\paragraph{Standard errors.} \label{app:model-standard_errors} \swann{Je décris les différents estimateurs des intervalles de confiance. }
We compute the uncertainty of the estimator by parametric bootstrap: the model is resampled, not the firms, so a fake world can host a supplier in a region that has none in the data. At $\hat\mu_2$ we draw $B_2=500$ fake worlds as above and re-run the whole estimation in each, with $W$ fixed: for each candidate $\mu$, $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ are calibrated on the world's own moments, $\boldsymbol\beta$ is simulated on the world's simulator, and the criterion is minimized by Nelder--Mead starting from $\hat\mu_2$, holding the simulator fixed within the world. Each world yields an estimate $(\mu_b,T_b,\Omega_{L,b},\Omega_{s,b},A_b,N_b)$, and the counterfactuals of the paper are recomputed at it.

For every parameter and every result, the standard error is the standard deviation across worlds, the 95\% confidence interval is given by the 2.5\% and 97.5\% quantiles, and the bias is the mean across worlds minus the estimate; if the bias is of the order of the standard deviation, the bias-corrected interval $[2\hat\mu-q_{97.5},\,2\hat\mu-q_{2.5}]$ should be used instead. The calibrated objects therefore need no separate treatment. $T$ and $N$ are recalibrated in every world, so their uncertainty combines that of $\hat\mu$ and the granular noise of the moments they are calibrated on, the integer jumps of $N_s$ included, and no derivative of the simulated moments with respect to $T$ --- which would capture the discrete changes of winners rather than smooth changes in moments --- is required. Comparing the dispersion of $\log T_{as}$ across areas in the fake worlds with its value at the estimate measures the upward bias of that dispersion, which contains the variance of the noise.

Two limits are worth stating. The worlds are drawn at $\hat\mu_2$ rather than at the true $\mu^*$, the usual limit of the parametric bootstrap, which is of second order unless the estimator is strongly biased --- something the bootstrap itself reveals. And measurement error in the data, such as non-response to the supplier surveys, is not covered by the procedure and is treated as a separate robustness exercise.

\subsection{Pseudo-code}
\label{app:pseudocode}

This section states the estimator in explicit form, in the version that the code implements. Only the trade-cost elasticity and the density elasticity are searched over,
$$
\mu=(\alpha,\kappa),
$$
with $\alpha=(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_K)$ when trade costs are binned. Every other parameter is calibrated within the model at each evaluation of the criterion: the area-level comparative advantages $\{T_{as}\}$ and the downstream technology parameters $(\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\})$ jointly by a general-equilibrium Sinkhorn inversion, and the variety counts $\{N_s\}$ by a monotone integer search. Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} evaluates the model at a candidate $\mu$; it calls Algorithm~\ref{alg:invertT} (the joint calibration of comparative advantage and downstream technology), Algorithm~\ref{alg:countN} (the variety count) and Algorithm~\ref{alg:cellbeta} (the extensive-margin coefficients). Algorithm~\ref{alg:smm} is the estimator; it calls the swarm (Algorithm~\ref{alg:pso}), the weighting matrix from fake worlds (Algorithm~\ref{alg:W}) and the parametric bootstrap (Algorithm~\ref{alg:boot}).

Throughout, $r'$ is an upstream region (commuting zone), $s$ a sector, $\rho$ a variety; $a(r')$ is the attraction area of $r'$ and $\mathcal{R}_a$ the regions of area $a$; $\mathcal{R}_D$ is the set of downstream buyers; $\mathcal{R}^+_s$ the regions inside the active attraction areas of sector $s$, and $\mathcal{A}\subseteq\{(r',s): r'\in\mathcal{R}^+_s\}$ the cells entering the extensive-margin regression. Comparative advantage of a cell is
$$
T_{r's}=T_{a(r')s}\,n_{r's}^{\kappa},
$$
with $n_{r's}$ the observed number of firms of sector $s$ in $r'$. Trade costs are $\tau_{r'r}=d_{r'r}^{\alpha}$; with $K$ distance bins, $\log\tau_{r'r}$ is continuous and piecewise linear in $\log d_{r'r}$, with slope $\alpha_k$ on the $k$-th segment between bin centres and $\alpha_1\le\dots\le\alpha_K$, so that equal slopes return the power law. The vector of extensive-margin coefficients is
$$
\boldsymbol\beta=(\beta_{distance},\ \beta_\kappa),
$$
the cell-level cloglog of $\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ on $\log d_{r'a(r')}$ (or the distance bins) and $\log n_{r's}$, with sector $\times$ area fixed effects and the groups without variation dropped. On the data side the targets are $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}$, the area sourcing shares $\tilde\gamma_{as}$ (normalised to sum to one per sector), the empty-cell shares $\widehat G_s(0)$ with the bounds $[N^{lo}_s,N^{hi}_s]$, the labour share $\tilde\Omega_L$, the sector shares $\tilde\Omega_s$ and the downstream sales shares $\hat\pi_r$. The calibrated objects
$$
  \bigl\{\theta,\ \varepsilon,\ \lambda,\ \nu,\ \{\nu_s\},\
  \{w_{r's}\},\ \{\delta_r\},\ \{d_{r'r}\},\ \{n_{r's}\}\bigr\}
$$
are fixed once and for all and suppressed from the argument lists. $m$ is the number of simulated varieties per sector and $J$ the number of simulated networks over which $\boldsymbol\beta$ is averaged ($J=100$).

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 1 -- simulation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{Simulation of the economy at a candidate $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$ (one criterion evaluation)}
\label{alg:simulate}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require candidate $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$; targets $\tilde\gamma_{as}$, $(\tilde\Omega_L,\{\tilde\Omega_s\},\{\hat\pi_r\})$, $\widehat G_s(0)$ and $[N^{lo}_s,N^{hi}_s]$; a fixed array of scrambled Sobol draws $u_{r'\rho s}\in(0,1)$, $\rho=1,\dots,m$; a fixed set of permutations $\{\sigma^s_j\}_{j\le J}$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$
\Ensure simulated moments $\widetilde{M}(\mu)$, the calibrated $\{T_{as}\},\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\}$ and $\{\widehat N_s\}$; or \textsc{reject}
\vspace{2pt}
\State Trade costs: $\tau_{r'r}\gets d_{r'r}^{\,\alpha}$ (piecewise in $\log d$ when binned)
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 1 (comparative advantage and downstream technology).}
\State \quad $\bigl(\{T_{as}\},\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\}\bigr)\gets\Call{CalibrateT}{\alpha,\kappa;\ \tilde\gamma,\tilde\Omega_L,\tilde\Omega_s,\hat\pi}$
  \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:invertT}}
\State \textbf{if} the calibration has not converged \textbf{then return} \textsc{reject}
  \Comment{never scored at a $T$ the model does not imply}
\State $T_{r's}\gets T_{a(r')s}\,n_{r's}^{\kappa}$
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 2 (Ricardian competition).}
\For{$s=1,\dots,S$ \textbf{and} $\rho=1,\dots,m$}
    \For{$r'\in\mathcal{R}^+_s$}
        \State $z_{r'\rho s}\gets T_{r's}^{1/\theta}\bigl(-\log(1-u_{r'\rho s})\bigr)^{-1/\theta}$
          \Comment{Fr\'echet inverse c.d.f.}
    \EndFor
    \For{$r\in\mathcal{R}_D$}
        \State $r^\star(\rho,s,r)\gets\arg\min_{r'}\; w_{r's}\tau_{r'r}/z_{r'\rho s}$,\quad
          $p_{\rho s r}\gets w_{r^\star s}\tau_{r^\star r}/z_{r^\star\rho s}$
    \EndFor
\EndFor
\State $\iota_{r'\rho s}\gets\mathbf{1}\{r'=r^\star(\rho,s,r)\ \text{for some }r\in\mathcal{R}_D\}$,\quad
  $k_{r's}\gets\sum_\rho\iota_{r'\rho s}$,\quad $p_{r's}\gets k_{r's}/m$
  \Comment{win-anywhere counts}
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Realised prices, sales and flows.} For every $r\in\mathcal{R}_D$:
\State \quad $P_{rs}\gets\bigl[\tfrac1m\sum_{\rho}p_{\rho s r}^{\,1-\nu_s}\bigr]^{1/(1-\nu_s)}$,\quad
  $P_r\gets\bigl[\sum_s\Omega_s P_{rs}^{1-\nu}\bigr]^{1/(1-\nu)}$
\State \quad $c_r\gets\bigl[\Omega_L w_r^{1-\lambda}+(1-\Omega_L)P_r^{1-\lambda}\bigr]^{1/(1-\lambda)}$,\quad
  $\tilde c_r\gets c_r/A_r$
\State \quad $y_r\gets \tilde c_r^{\,\varepsilon}\,\delta_r\big/\sum_{r''}\tilde c_{r''}^{\,\varepsilon}\delta_{r''}$,\quad
  $\pi_r\gets y_r$
\State \quad $X_{r'rs}\gets$ expenditure of $r$ on the varieties won by $r'$,
  from~(\ref{eq:input_flows})
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 3 (variety count).}
\State \quad $\{\widehat{N}_s\}\gets\Call{CalibrateN}{\{k_{r's}\},m;\ \widehat{G}_s(0),[N^{lo}_s,N^{hi}_s]}$
  \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:countN}}
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Step 4 (moments).}
\State \quad (1)--(3) $\widetilde\Omega_L$, $\widetilde\Omega_s$, $\pi_r$ on the realised prices
  \Comment{reported; not in the criterion}
\State \quad (4) $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}\gets\Call{CellBeta}{\{\iota_{r'\rho s}\},\{\widehat N_s\},\{\sigma^s_j\}}$
  \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:cellbeta}}
\State \quad (5) $\widetilde{\gamma}_{as}\gets\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_a}\sum_r X_{r'rs}\big/\sum_{r''}\sum_r X_{r''rs}$
\State \quad (6) $\widetilde{G}_s(\widehat{N}_s)$
  \Comment{count moment at the calibrated $\widehat{N}_s$}
\State \Return $\widetilde{M}(\mu)$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Four features of Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} are worth emphasizing. First, the arrows run one way, $(\alpha,\kappa)\to(T,\Omega_L,\Omega_s,A)\to\{p_{r's}\}\to\{N_s\}\to\boldsymbol\beta$: the calibration of comparative advantage does not use the variety count, and the variety count does not revise it, so Steps~1--3 form a recursion rather than a simultaneous system. Second, the calibration of Step~1 runs on the \emph{expected} sector price indices $P_{sr}=\Gamma_s\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}$, while the moments of Step~4 use the \emph{realised} indices of the $m$ simulated varieties. At the calibrated values, the simulated blocks 1--3 therefore differ from their targets only by the Monte Carlo error of the realised indices, which the search must not chase: they leave the criterion. The same holds for $\tilde\gamma_{as}$, matched in expectation by the calibration, and for $\widetilde G_s(0)$, matched up to the integer step by the bisection; the moments that carry information on $\mu$ are $\boldsymbol\beta$. Third, $m$ is a Monte Carlo device unrelated to $\widehat{N}_s$: the winner of a variety depends only on relative delivered costs, so $p_{r's}$ is a continuum object estimated at any $m$ large enough (the code sets $m=\max(100,\max_s N^{hi}_s)$, which also bounds every candidate $N_s$). Fourth, the draws $u$ and the permutations $\sigma^s_j$ are held fixed across every evaluation (common random numbers), which makes the criterion a deterministic function of $\mu$ that a swarm can descend; $u$ is one low-dimensional scrambled Sobol net per sector, with regions as dimensions and varieties as points. Fake worlds (Algorithms~\ref{alg:W} and~\ref{alg:boot}) are drawn with independent pseudo-random numbers instead.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 2 -- joint calibration of T and the downstream technology
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{CalibrateT}: comparative advantage and downstream technology by matrix scaling with general-equilibrium expenditure weights}
\label{alg:invertT}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $(\alpha,\kappa)$; targets $\tilde\gamma_{as}$ and $(\tilde\Omega_L,\{\tilde\Omega_s\},\{\hat\pi_r\})$;
  damping $\varsigma=0.3$; tolerance $\epsilon_T=10^{-9}$; at most $600$ passes; starting values $T^{(0)}_{as}$
\Ensure $\{T_{as}\},\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\}$ reproducing $\tilde\gamma_{as}$, $\tilde\Omega_L$, $\tilde\Omega_s$ and $\hat\pi_r$ in expectation
\State $T\gets T^{(0)}$; normalise $T_{as}\gets T_{as}/T_{a_s^{\text{ref}}s}$ for each $s$
\For{$t=1,2,\dots$ \textbf{until} convergence}
    \State $B_{ar}\gets\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}_a}n_{r's}^{\kappa}(w_{r's}\tau_{r'r})^{-\theta}$,\quad
      $\Phi_{sr}\gets\sum_{a'}T_{a's}B_{a'r}$,\quad
      $P_{sr}\gets\Gamma_s\,\Phi_{sr}^{-1/\theta}$
      \Comment{expected price indices}
    \State \textbf{Downstream technology at these prices.} Solve for $(\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\})$, $\sum_s\Omega_s=1$, by Newton on $(\operatorname{logit}\Omega_L,\log\Omega_s/\Omega_1)$:
    \State \quad $s_{rs}=\dfrac{\Omega_s P_{sr}^{1-\nu}}{\sum_k\Omega_kP_{kr}^{1-\nu}}$,\quad
      $c_r=\bigl[\Omega_L w_r^{1-\lambda}+(1-\Omega_L)P_r^{1-\lambda}\bigr]^{\frac1{1-\lambda}}$,\quad
      $\ell_r=\Omega_L\Bigl(\dfrac{w_r}{c_r}\Bigr)^{1-\lambda}$
    \State \quad $\sum_r\hat\pi_r\ell_r=\tilde\Omega_L$,\qquad
      $\dfrac{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}}{\sum_r\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)}=\tilde\Omega_s,\ s=2,\dots,S$
      \Comment{blocks 1--2}
    \State $\omega_{rs}\gets\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)s_{rs}$
      \Comment{sales pinned at $\hat\pi_r$: $A_r$ does not enter}
    \State $\gamma_{as}(T)\gets\dfrac{\sum_r\omega_{rs}\,T_{as}B_{ar}/\Phi_{sr}}{\sum_r\omega_{rs}}$
    \State $T^{+}_{as}\gets T_{as}\bigl(\tilde\gamma_{as}/\gamma_{as}(T)\bigr)^{\varsigma}$ if $\tilde\gamma_{as}>0$, else $T^{+}_{as}\gets T_{as}$
    \State $T^{+}_{as}\gets T^{+}_{as}/T^{+}_{a^{\text{ref}}_s s}$ for each $s$
    \State $\text{resid}\gets\max_{a,s:\ \tilde\gamma_{as}>0}\bigl|\log T^{+}_{as}-\log T_{as}\bigr|$;\quad $T\gets T^{+}$
    \State \textbf{if} $\text{resid}<\epsilon_T$ \textbf{then break}
\EndFor
\State re-solve \eqref{eq:app-tech} at the final $T$;\quad $A_r\gets c_r\,(\hat\pi_r/\delta_r)^{-1/\varepsilon}$, normalised to $A_1=1$
  \Comment{block 3, closed form}
\State \Return $(T,\Omega_L,\{\Omega_s\},\{A_r\})$, converged iff both the $T$ loop and the solution of \eqref{eq:app-tech} converged
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

With the downstream technology held fixed, the multiplicative update is a Sinkhorn scaling of the strictly positive matrix $B$ --- the density term $n_{r's}^{\kappa}$ only changes the kernel --- hence a contraction with a fixed point unique up to the sector-specific scale removed by the reference-area normalisation. Given $T$, the downstream technology parameters are just-identified by blocks 1--3 through the expected price indices, which do not depend on them; $(\Omega_L,\Omega_s)$ are solved jointly because the input-share weights $\hat\pi_r(1-\ell_r)$ move with $\Omega_s$ through $P_r$, and $A_r$ follows in closed form since sales are $\pi_r\propto\delta_r(c_r/A_r)^{\varepsilon}$. Pinning sales at their target removes the demand loop through which, with fixed downstream technology, raising $T$ lowers input prices, raises sales and sends demand back to the same area. A zero-target column (an area active in the data that wins nothing, which only happens in a fake world) is held fixed relative to the reference and left out of the residual. Each worker keeps the last converged $(T,\Omega_L,\Omega_s)$ as the next starting value; since the fixed point is unique, a converged result does not depend on it, and a failed warm start is retried from $T^{(0)}$. A particle for which the calibration does not converge is rejected rather than evaluated. The starting value $T^{(0)}$ is the closed-form inversion of $\tilde\gamma$ at the priors $(\alpha_0,\kappa_0)$ with expenditure proxied by $\hat\pi_r$. \swann{Je n'ai pas réussi à montrer que quand on ajoute le GE feedback on obtient une application contractante (je ne suis pas sûr que ça soit possible d'ailleurs).}

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 3 -- variety count
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{CalibrateN}: variety count by monotone integer search}
\label{alg:countN}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require win counts $k_{r's}$ from $m$ simulated varieties; targets
  $\widehat{G}_s(0)$ (the data, or a fake world's own empty-cell shares); bounds $[N^{lo}_s,N^{hi}_s]$
\Ensure $\{\widehat{N}_s\}$
\For{$s=1,\dots,S$}
    \State define
      $\widetilde{G}_s(n)\gets\dfrac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^+_s|}\displaystyle\sum_{r'\in\mathcal{R}^+_s}
      \binom{m-k_{r's}}{n}\Big/\binom{m}{n}$
      \Comment{unbiased for $\frac{1}{|\mathcal{R}^+_s|}\sum_{r'}(1-p_{r's})^{n}$}
    \If{$\widetilde{G}_s(N^{lo}_s)\le\widehat{G}_s(0)$}
        \State $\widehat{N}_s\gets N^{lo}_s$
    \ElsIf{$\widetilde{G}_s(N^{hi}_s)\ge\widehat{G}_s(0)$}
        \State $\widehat{N}_s\gets N^{hi}_s$
    \Else
        \State $(\underline{n},\overline{n})\gets(N^{lo}_s,N^{hi}_s)$
        \While{$\overline{n}-\underline{n}>1$}
            \State $n\gets\lfloor(\underline{n}+\overline{n})/2\rfloor$
            \State \textbf{if} $\widetilde{G}_s(n)>\widehat{G}_s(0)$ \textbf{then}
              $\underline{n}\gets n$ \textbf{else} $\overline{n}\gets n$
        \EndWhile
        \State $\widehat{N}_s\gets\arg\min_{n\in\{\underline{n},\overline{n}\}}
          \bigl|\widetilde{G}_s(n)-\widehat{G}_s(0)\bigr|$
    \EndIf
\EndFor
\State \Return $\{\widehat{N}_s\}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Because $\widetilde{G}_s$ is decreasing in $n$ and is evaluated in closed form from the win counts of Step~2, the search costs $O(\log(N^{hi}_s-N^{lo}_s))$ evaluations and never re-simulates the Ricardian competition. The count is an integer at every evaluation: it is never relaxed to the real line and never rounded. A bound that binds is recorded and reported rather than silently absorbed.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 4 -- block 4
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{CellBeta}: the extensive-margin coefficients, averaged over $J$ networks}
\label{alg:cellbeta}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require win indicators $\iota_{r'\rho s}$ of Step~2; variety counts $\{\widehat N_s\}$; permutations $\sigma^s_j$ of $\{1,\dots,m\}$, $j=1,\dots,J$
\Ensure $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}$, an estimate of $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$, and the number of failed fits
\For{$j=1,\dots,J$}
    \State network $j$: the varieties $\sigma^s_j(1),\dots,\sigma^s_j(\widehat N_s)$ of each sector
      \Comment{nested in $N$}
    \State $y^{(j)}_{r's}\gets\max_{t\le\widehat N_s}\iota_{r'\sigma^s_j(t)s}$ for $(r',s)\in\mathcal{A}$
      \Comment{$\mathbf{1}\{K_{r's}>0\}$ in network $j$}
    \State $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(j)}\gets$ cloglog of $y^{(j)}$ on $\log d_{r'a(r')}$ (or the bins) and $\log n_{r's}$, sector$\times$area fixed effects, groups without variation dropped
      \Comment{the data regression}
\EndFor
\State $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}\gets$ mean of the $\hat{\boldsymbol\beta}^{(j)}$ over the converged fits; non-converged or quasi-separated fits are excluded and counted
\State \Return $\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Each network has exactly $\widehat N_s$ varieties per sector, as the data do, and $\boldsymbol\beta$ is computed on it exactly as on the data; averaging the statistic over networks, rather than computing the statistic of the average network, is what targets $\mathbb{E}_\Omega[\boldsymbol\beta(\mu,\Omega)]$ when the observed $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}$ is a single realisation. Drawing the $J$ networks among the $m$ winners already found costs no further Ricardian solve.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 5 -- PSO
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{Pso}: particle swarm minimisation of $\mathcal{Q}$ over a box}
\label{alg:pso}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require objective $\mathcal{Q}$; box $[\ell,u]\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}$; swarm size
  $P$; iterations $I$; warm start $x_0$; inertia range
  $[\underline{w},\overline{w}]=[0.4,0.9]$; cognitive $c_1=1.5$; social $c_2=2.5$
\Ensure $x^\star=\arg\min \mathcal{Q}$ over the visited points
\State draw $x_1,\dots,x_{P-1}$ from a scrambled Sobol net on $[\ell,u]$;\;
  $x_P\gets\Pi_{[\ell,u]}(x_0)$
\State $v_i\gets 0.1\,(u-\ell)\odot\xi_i$, $\xi_i\sim\mathcal N(0,I)$; \; $b_i\gets x_i$, $f_i\gets\mathcal{Q}(x_i)$ evaluated in
  parallel; \; $g\gets\arg\min_i f_i$
\For{$t=1,\dots,I$}
    \State $w\gets\overline{w}-(\overline{w}-\underline{w})\,t/I$
    \For{$i=1,\dots,P$ \textbf{in parallel}}
        \State draw $r_1,r_2\sim U[0,1]^d$
        \State $v_i\gets w\,v_i+c_1 r_1\odot(b_i-x_i)+c_2 r_2\odot(g-x_i)$
        \Comment{$\odot$: element-wise multiplication}
        \State $v_i\gets\Pi_{[-\bar v,\bar v]}(v_i)$, \; $\bar v=0.1\,(u-\ell)$
        \State $x_i\gets x_i+v_i$; reflect $x_i$ back into $[\ell,u]$, halving the
          reflected velocity
        \State $f\gets\mathcal{Q}(x_i)$
          \Comment{one call to Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate}; $+\infty$ on \textsc{reject}}
        \State \textbf{if} $f<\mathcal{Q}(b_i)$ \textbf{then} $b_i\gets x_i$;\;
          \textbf{if} $f<\mathcal{Q}(g)$ \textbf{then} $g\gets x_i$
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{if} $t>30$ and $t\equiv 0 \pmod{25}$: redraw uniformly on $[\ell,u]$ every particle with $\mathcal{Q}(x_i)>1.5\,\mathcal{Q}(g)$
\EndFor
\State \Return $g$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

When trade costs are binned, $\mathcal{Q}$ reads the block $(\alpha_1,\dots,\alpha_K)$ \emph{sorted}, and the box bounds of that block are sorted too, so every evaluated point satisfies $\alpha_1\le\dots\le\alpha_K$ and stays in the box. The warm start is always one of the particles, so a stage never ends worse than its incumbent.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 6 -- weighting matrix from fake worlds
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{WorldW}: the weighting matrix from fake worlds}
\label{alg:W}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require $\hat\mu_1=(\hat\alpha_1,\hat\kappa_1)$ and its calibrated parameters $(\widehat T,\widehat\Omega_L,\widehat\Omega_s,\widehat A,\widehat N)$; number of worlds $B_1=1\,000$
\Ensure $W$, on $\boldsymbol\beta$ only
\For{$b=1,\dots,B_1$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State draw a network $\Omega_b$ at $(\hat\mu_1,\widehat T,\widehat\Omega_L,\widehat\Omega_s,\widehat A)$ with $\widehat N_s$ varieties per sector, i.i.d.\ draws (seed $b$)
    \State on $\Omega_b$, as on the data: $\boldsymbol\beta_b$ (\emph{one} cloglog fit), $G_b$, $\gamma_b$, $\tilde\Omega_{L,b}$, $\tilde\Omega_{s,b}$, $\pi_b$
    \State \textbf{skip} the world if the fit of $\boldsymbol\beta_b$ fails or a reference area wins nothing \Comment{counted}
    \State $(T_b,\Omega_{L,b},\Omega_{s,b},A_b)\gets\Call{CalibrateT}{\hat\alpha_1,\hat\kappa_1;\ \gamma_b,\tilde\Omega_{L,b},\tilde\Omega_{s,b},\pi_b}$
      \Comment{an area with $\gamma_{b,as}=0$ gets $T_{as}=0$}
    \State simulator $U_b$: Sobol draws at $m$ randomised by a seed proper to the world, and its own permutations $\sigma^s_j$
    \State run Steps~2--4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} on $U_b$ at $(\hat\mu_1,T_b,\Omega_{L,b},\Omega_{s,b},A_b)$, with $N_b\gets\Call{CalibrateN}{\cdot\,;G_b}$: $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$
    \State $e_b\gets\boldsymbol\beta_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,b}$
\EndFor
\State $\hat\Sigma_u\gets\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(e_1,\dots,e_{B_1})$,\quad $W\gets\hat\Sigma_u^{-1}$
\State report $\bar e$ and its $t$-statistic
  \Comment{the bias of the continuum simulator against a finite world}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

No derivative is computed: to first order $e_b$ is the composite noise $u_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim,MC,b}$, so $\widehat{\mathrm{Cov}}(e_b)$ estimates $\Sigma_u$, including the part of the granular noise in $\boldsymbol\beta$ absorbed by the recalibration of $T$, the downstream technology parameters and $N$ on the same network. The world's simulator must have its own seed: if it reused the draws of $\Omega_b$, it would reproduce the fake data exactly at $\hat\mu_1$ and the dispersion would vanish; if it were common to all worlds, the simulation noise would be omitted. When $\boldsymbol\beta$ has as many entries as $\mu$ (one distance coefficient), the system is exactly identified and $W$ moves the inference, not the point estimate.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 7 -- parametric bootstrap
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{\textsc{WorldBoot}: inference by parametric bootstrap}
\label{alg:boot}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require an estimate $\hat\mu=(\hat\alpha,\hat\kappa)$ and its calibrated parameters $(\widehat T,\widehat\Omega_L,\widehat\Omega_s,\widehat A,\widehat N)$; a weighting matrix $W$, fixed; number of worlds $B_2=500$
\Ensure standard deviations, 95\% intervals and biases of $\alpha,\kappa,N,T,\Omega_L,\Omega_s,A$ and of the counterfactuals
\For{$b=1,\dots,B_2$ \textbf{in parallel}}
    \State draw $\Omega_b$ and compute $(\boldsymbol\beta_b,G_b,\gamma_b,\tilde\Omega_{L,b},\tilde\Omega_{s,b},\pi_b)$ as in Algorithm~\ref{alg:W}; simulator $U_b$ of its own
    \State define, for $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$ in the box ($\alpha$ read sorted):
    \State \quad $(T,\Omega_L,\Omega_s,A)\gets\Call{CalibrateT}{\alpha,\kappa;\ \gamma_b,\tilde\Omega_{L,b},\tilde\Omega_{s,b},\pi_b}$
      \Comment{warm-started within the world}
    \State \quad $\boldsymbol\beta_{sim}(\mu)\gets$ Steps~2--4 of Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate} on $U_b$, with $N\gets\Call{CalibrateN}{\cdot\,;G_b}$
    \State \quad $\mathcal{Q}_b(\mu)\gets\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim}(\mu)\bigr)'W\bigl(\boldsymbol\beta_b-\boldsymbol\beta_{sim}(\mu)\bigr)$, $+\infty$ outside the box or on \textsc{reject}
    \State $\mu_b\gets\arg\min_\mu\mathcal{Q}_b(\mu)$ by Nelder--Mead started at $\hat\mu$
      \Comment{common random numbers within the world}
    \State $(T_b,\Omega_{L,b},\Omega_{s,b},A_b,N_b)\gets$ the calibrated parameters and the count at $\mu_b$
    \State recompute the counterfactuals at $(\mu_b,T_b,\Omega_{L,b},\Omega_{s,b},A_b)$
      \Comment{closed form: estimate, equal $T$, $\tau\equiv1$}
\EndFor
\State for every parameter and result: sd across worlds, $[q_{2.5},q_{97.5}]$, bias $=$ mean $-$ estimate
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

This is a parametric bootstrap: the model is resampled, not the firms, so a fake world can host a supplier in a region that has none in the data. The whole chain is re-run in each world, including the calibration of $T$ and of the downstream technology on the world's own targets, so the sampling noise of every calibrated object enters the draws. The worlds are drawn i.i.d.\ rather than from the Sobol design of the estimation, whose stratification would understate the variance. An area active in the data that wins nothing in a world gets $T=0$ and is reported as an empty share rather than as a log of zero; a world whose reference area is empty cannot be normalised and is excluded. If the bias is of the order of the standard deviation, the bias-corrected interval $[2\hat\mu-q_{97.5},\,2\hat\mu-q_{2.5}]$ should be used instead of the quantiles.

% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
%  Algorithm 8 -- estimation
% ---------------------------------------------------------------------
\begin{algorithm}[H]
\caption{SMM estimation of $\mu=(\alpha,\kappa)$}
\label{alg:smm}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require targets $\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}$, $\tilde\gamma_{as}$, $\widehat{G}_s(0)$, $(\tilde\Omega_L,\{\tilde\Omega_s\},\{\hat\pi_r\})$; number of refinement loops $L=20$; swarm size $P=98$; iterations $I_0=200$ (initial fit) and $I=50$ (refinement)
\Ensure $\widehat{\mu}_1$, $\widehat{\mu}_2$, their calibrated parameters, and their bootstrap distributions
\vspace{2pt}
\State \textbf{Starting values.} $\alpha_0=-\hat\beta_{distance}/\theta$ from the continuous-distance specification (with bins: the slopes of $\log\tau_b=-\hat\beta_b/\theta$ between bin centres, as a running maximum); $\kappa_0=\hat\beta_\kappa$; $T^{(0)}$ the closed-form inversion of $\tilde\gamma$ at $(\alpha_0,\kappa_0)$. Box: $\alpha\in[0.1\,\alpha_0,\ \min(10\,\alpha_0,1.5)]$, $\kappa\in[-1,3]$.
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 1: first fit under equal weights.} Let
  $\mathcal{Q}_I(\mu)=\bigl\|\widetilde{M}_{4,5,6}(\mu)-\widehat{M}_{4,5,6}\bigr\|^2$, each evaluation running Algorithm~\ref{alg:simulate}
  \Comment{blocks 1--3 out of the loss}
\State \quad $\mu\gets\Call{Pso}{\mathcal{Q}_I,\text{box},P,I_0,\mu_0}$
\For{$\text{loop}=1,\dots,L$}
    \State $\mu\gets\Call{Pso}{\mathcal{Q}_I,\mathcal{B}_{\text{loop}}(\mu),P,I,\mu}$ \Comment{$\mathcal{B}_{\text{loop}}(\mu)$: box of radius $\varrho_{\text{loop}}$ around $\mu$, within the box above}
    \State \textbf{break if} $\text{loop}>2$ and $\max_k|\Delta\mu_k|/|\mu_k|<10^{-6}$
\EndFor
\State $\widehat{\mu}_1\gets\mu$ and its calibrated parameters $(\widehat T,\widehat\Omega_L,\widehat\Omega_s,\widehat A,\widehat N)$
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 2: the weighting matrix.} $W\gets\Call{WorldW}{\widehat\mu_1}$ \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:W}}
\State \quad inference on $\widehat\mu_1$: $\Call{WorldBoot}{\widehat\mu_1,\ I}$ \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:boot}, Step~1's weighting}
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 3: the efficient fit.} Let
  $\mathcal{Q}_W(\mu)=\bigl(\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)-\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}\bigr)'W
  \bigl(\bar{\boldsymbol\beta}(\mu)-\widehat{\boldsymbol\beta}\bigr)$; run the swarm from $\widehat\mu_1$ and the refinement loops of Step~1, yielding $\widehat{\mu}_2$ and its calibrated parameters
\vspace{3pt}
\State \textbf{Step 4: inference.} $\Call{WorldBoot}{\widehat\mu_2,\ W}$ \Comment{Algorithm~\ref{alg:boot}}
\State \Return $\widehat{\mu}_1$, $\widehat{\mu}_2$, their calibrated parameters and bootstrap distributions
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Two implementation notes. First, the calibration within the model divides the work: $\{T_{as}\}$, $\Omega_L$, $\{\Omega_s\}$ and $\{A_r\}$ are pinned exactly by $\tilde\gamma$ and blocks 1--3, and $\{N_s\}$ by $\widehat G_s(0)$, so the swarm searches a space of dimension $K+1$ (two with a power-law trade cost) and $\mu$ is identified by $\boldsymbol\beta$ alone: $\beta_{distance}$ by the decline of supplier presence with distance within an area, $\beta_\kappa$ by its rise with density within an area. This is why the criterion of Step~3 runs on $\boldsymbol\beta$ only, and why the weighting matrix is $(N_{REG}+1)\times(N_{REG}+1)$. Second, the refinement loops of Step~1 re-run the swarm on $\mu$ warm-started at the incumbent, in a box whose size is annealed across loops; since the incumbent is always a particle, the criterion is monotone across loops by construction.

\swann{Maintenant qu'on est passé à une économie granulaire, je ne suis pas sûr que l'on puisse exploiter facilement la décomposition de l'élasticité qui est obtenue plus bas. Je pense qu'il faut refaire les calculs pour voir comment la finitude du nombre de variétés change la formule. }
In the model, sales' growth of a supplier $i$ in $(r',s)$ of productivity $z$ is:
$$
d\ln x_{it} = \sum_r a_{ri}^D(z,d_{r'r}) d\ln x_{drt} + \delta_i^D
$$
hence the correlation $d\ln x_{it}$ and $d\ln x_{drt}$ is $a_{ri}^D(z, d_{r'r}).$ If we estimate:
\begin{equation}
        d\ln x_{i,t}^r = \alpha_{i} + \gamma Sup_{i} \times d\ln x_{rd,t}+ \delta Sup_{i} \log(d_{ir})\times d\ln x_{rd,t} + FE_{rs(i),t} +\varepsilon_{i,t}
\end{equation}
we recover the average correlation over firms and regions pairs: $\gamma = \mathbb{E}_{r,z,r',s}(\gamma_{rs}(z,d_{r'r}))$ and $\delta = \mathbb{E}_{r, z, r', s}(\delta_{rs}(z,d_{r'r}))$.

The average correlation between a downstream region and an upstream sector is:

\begin{align}
    \gamma_{rs} &= \mathbb{E}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(\gamma_{rs}(z, d_{r',r})) \\
    &= \mathbb{E}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(a_{ri}^D(z,d_{r'r}) | Sup_i = 1, d_{r'r} = 1) \\
    &=\mathbb{E}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(a_{ri}^D(z,d_{r'r}) | \text{i serves r} , d_{r'r} = 1) \mathbb{P}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(\text{i serves r} | Sup_i = 1)\\
    &=\mathbb{E}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(a_{ri}^D(z,d_{r'r}) \frac{\rho_{r'rs}(z)}{\Tilde{\rho}_{r's}(z)}| d_{r'r} = 1) \\
\end{align}

Similarly, the expression of $\delta$ is obtained from:
\begin{align}
    \delta_{rs} &= \frac{d}{d\ln d}\mathbb{E}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(a_{ri}^D(z,d_{r'r}) | Sup_i = 1, d_{r'r} = d) \\
    &=\frac{d}{d\ln d}\mathbb{E}_{z,r',d_{r'r}}(a_{ri}^D(z,d) \frac{\rho_{r'rs}(z)}{\Tilde{\rho}_{r's}(z)}| d_{r'r} = d) \\
\end{align}
To do the derivation, we assume that the distance is a continuous variable and drop the $r'$ subscript such as $\rho_{r'rs}(z)$ becomes $\rho_{rs}(z,d)$. Similarly, we drop $i$ as firms can be indexed by there productivity. 
\begin{align}
    \delta_{rs} &= \frac{d}{d\ln d}\mathbb{E}_{z,r',d}(a_{r}^D(z,d) \frac{\rho_{rs}(z,d)}{\Tilde{\rho}_{r's}(z)}) \\
    &= \mathbb{E}_{z,r',d}\left(a_{r}^D(z,d) \frac{\rho_{rs}(z,d)}{\Tilde{\rho}_{r's}(z)}(\frac{d\ln \rho_{rs}(z,d)}{d\ln d} + \frac{d\ln a_r^D(z,d)}{d\ln d})\right)\\
\end{align}
We introduce $\Lambda_{r'rs}(z,d)$ such as $\ln \rho_{rs}(z,d) = -\Lambda_{r'rs}(z,d)$ and $\frac{d\ln \rho_{rs}(z,d)}{d\ln d} = - \theta\Lambda_{r'rs}(z,d)\frac{d\ln \tau_{r'rs}}{d\ln d}$. Since:
$$
a_r^D(z,d) = \frac{\gamma_{r'rs}x_{rs}}{\gamma_{r'rs}x_{rs} + \sum_{r''\neq r} \gamma_{r'r''s}x_{r''s}\rho_{r'rs}(z)}
$$
we have 
\begin{align}
  \frac{d\ln a_r^D(z,d)}{d\ln d} &= \frac{d\ln \gamma_{r'rs}}{d\ln d}(1-a_r^D(z,d))\\
&=   -\theta\frac{d\ln \tau_{r'rs}}{d\ln d}(1-a_r^D(z,d))
\end{align}
Consequently: 
\begin{align*}
    \delta_{rs} &=  -\theta\frac{d\ln \tau_{r'rs}}{d\ln d}\mathbb{E}_{z, r', d}\left(a_{r}^D(z,d) \frac{\rho_{rs}(z,d)}{\Tilde{\rho}_{r's}(z)}(\Lambda_{r'rs} + (1-a_r^D(z,d)))\right)\\
\end{align*}
To obtain $\delta$ and $\gamma$ we must take the expectation over all sectors and downstream regions which yield the following ratio:
$$
\frac{\delta}{\gamma} \simeq -\theta \mathbb{E}(\frac{d\ln \tau}{d\ln d}) \times \frac{\mathbb{E}(\frac{\rho}{\Tilde{\rho}}a_r^D(\Lambda + 1-a_r^D))}{\mathbb{E}(\frac{\rho}{\Tilde{\rho}}a_r^D | d= 1)}
$$
The term $1-a_r^D$ capture the fact that when we increase the distance between a supplier and a downstream region, sales toward this region will be reduced thus increasing the importance of other downstream regions in the portfolio of the supplier. When the distance increases, the probability to serve downstream region $r$ decreases and therefore further reducing the comovement with this region. This is what $\Lambda$ captures.

\subsection{Sensitivity analysis}\label{app:sensitivity_analysis}




\subsection{EV eco-score reform: shock construction}\label{app:ev_ecoscore}
 
This appendix describes the construction of the region-specific downstream demand shocks used in Section~4.3, based on the estimates of \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}.
 
\paragraph{Vehicle categories.} We partition the vehicle market into three types: eligible electric vehicles (EEV), which retain the purchase subsidy post-reform; non-eligible electric vehicles (NEV), which lose the subsidy; and internal combustion engine vehicles (ICE). The reform amounts to a price shock
\begin{equation*}
    \Delta \tau_v = \begin{cases}
        0 & \text{if } v = \text{EEV}, \\
        +\sigma & \text{if } v = \text{NEV}, \\
        0 & \text{if } v = \text{ICE},
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}
where $\sigma = \text{\euro{4,260}}$ is the lower-bound subsidy loss computed in \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}, Equation~(1).
 
\paragraph{Demand parameters.} The counterfactual share ratios are computed using the three-level nested logit estimated in \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}. The relevant parameters are the price sensitivity $\alpha = 0.087$ (per \euro{1,000}), the upper-nest correlation $\mu_3 = 0.648$ (powertrain level), and the lower-nest scale $\mu_{4,E} = 0.569$ (within-electric type level). Pre-reform market shares are $s_E^{\circ} = 0.169$ (electric share in total registrations) and $\omega^{\text{NEV}} = 0.30$ (NEV share within the electric segment).
 
\paragraph{Counterfactual share ratios.} Following the exact hat-algebra approach of \cite{MalgouyresMayerNolden2025}, we compute the type-level dampening factor for non-eligible EVs:
\begin{equation*}
    \Lambda_{\text{NEV}} = \exp\!\left( -\frac{\alpha}{\mu_{4,E}} \, \sigma \right) = \exp\!\left( -\frac{0.087}{0.569} \times 4.26 \right) \approx 0.520,
\end{equation*}
and the electric nest dampening factor:
\begin{equation*}
    \Phi_E = (1 - \omega^{\text{NEV}}) \cdot 1 + \omega^{\text{NEV}} \cdot \Lambda_{\text{NEV}} = 0.70 + 0.30 \times 0.52 \approx 0.856.
\end{equation*}
The counterfactual share ratios $\hat{s}_v \equiv s_v^{\text{post}} / s_v^{\text{pre}}$ are then:
\begin{align}
    \hat{s}_{\text{EEV}} &= \frac{\Phi_E^{\,\mu_{4,E}/\mu_3 - 1}}{s_E^{\circ} \cdot \Phi_E^{\,\mu_{4,E}/\mu_3} + (1 - s_E^{\circ})} \approx 1.002, \label{eq:shat_eev} \\[4pt]
    \hat{s}_{\text{NEV}} &= \Lambda_{\text{NEV}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{EEV}} \approx 0.521, \label{eq:shat_nev} \\[4pt]
    \hat{s}_{\text{ICE}} &= \frac{1}{s_E^{\circ} \cdot \Phi_E^{\,\mu_{4,E}/\mu_3} + (1 - s_E^{\circ})} \approx 1.021. \label{eq:shat_ice}
\end{align}
Non-eligible EVs lose approximately 48\% of their sales, while ICE vehicles gain roughly 2\% from the contraction of the electric segment. Eligible EVs are approximately unchanged: within-nest gains from the exit of NEV competitors are almost exactly offset by the reduction in the overall attractiveness of the electric nest.
 
\paragraph{Regional demand shifters.} Let $\omega_r^v$ denote the share of vehicle type $v \in \{\text{EEV}, \text{NEV}, \text{ICE}\}$ in region $r$'s pre-reform motor vehicle production. The region-specific demand shock is constructed as the production-weighted average of the counterfactual share ratios:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:delta_r}
    \frac{\delta_r^{\text{post}}}{\delta_r^{\text{pre}}} = \omega_r^{\text{EEV}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{EEV}} + \omega_r^{\text{NEV}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{NEV}} + \omega_r^{\text{ICE}} \cdot \hat{s}_{\text{ICE}}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:delta_r} generates cross-regional heterogeneity solely through the production composition $(\omega_r^{\text{EEV}}, \omega_r^{\text{NEV}}, \omega_r^{\text{ICE}})$: regions assembling a larger share of disqualified models experience a more negative demand shift, which then propagates upstream through the production network estimated in our structural model.
