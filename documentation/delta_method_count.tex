\section{Definition}

Throughout, $\Gbar_s(K)$ is the \textbf{increment}, not the CDF:
\begin{equation}
\Gbar_s(K)\;=\;\frac{1}{|L_s|}\sum_{l\in L_s}\Pr\!\big[K_{ls}=K\big]
\;=\;\frac{1}{|L_s|}\sum_{l\in L_s}\binom{N_s}{K} q_l^{K}(1-q_l)^{N_s-K},
\end{equation}
the share of cells in sector $s$ hosting \emph{exactly} $K$ suppliers, with
$K_{ls}\sim\mathrm{Bin}(N_s,q_{ls})$. At $K=0$ this coincides with the CDF, so the
targeted moment $\Gbar_s(0)=\overline{(1-q)^{N_s}}$ and the calibration of $\Nhat_s$ are
unchanged; only the $K\ge1$ panels are redefined.

\paragraph{Why increments and not the CDF.}
$\Nhat_s$ is calibrated as the integer solving $\Gbar_s(0;n)=\Ghat_s(0)$, so the $K=0$ level
is fit essentially by construction. Under the CDF convention every panel
$\Pr[K_{ls}\le K]$ \emph{contains} that fitted level, and the untargeted panels re-test what
is already targeted. The increments isolate the free content --- the \emph{shape} of the
count distribution, which is what gate V8 asks about --- and, as shown below, the $\Ghat(0)$
noise largely cancels out of them.

\paragraph{Data side.}
\texttt{G\_K.csv} stores the cumulative $G=\Pr[K_{ls}\le K]$. The empirical increment is the
difference of consecutive columns, $\hat p_s(K)=\Ghat_s(K)-\Ghat_s(K-1)$.

\section{The derivative in $n$: one line, exact}

$N_s$ is an integer, so the relevant derivative is the unit first difference. From
$\mathrm{Bin}(n+1,q)=\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)+\mathrm{Bern}(q)$,
\begin{equation}
\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n+1,q)=K]=(1-q)\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)=K]+q\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n,q)=K-1],
\end{equation}
hence
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
D_s^{K}\;\equiv\;\Gbar_s(K;n+1)-\Gbar_s(K;n)
=\frac{1}{|L_s|}\sum_{l\in L_s} q_l\Big(\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n,q_l)=K-1]-\Pr[\mathrm{Bin}(n,q_l)=K]\Big)\;}
\label{eq:D}
\end{equation}
with the first term absent at $K=0$, where \eqref{eq:D} collapses to
$D_s^{0}=-\overline{q(1-q)^{n}}$ --- verbatim the quantity already computed beside the
analytic derivative in \texttt{tools.jl}. So $D_s^{K}$ needs only $\qhat$ and $\Nhat_s$: no
new simulation, no digamma, no continuum relaxation.

\section{Variance of the fitted increment}

Let $a_s^{K}=\partial\Gbar_s(K)/\partial\alpha$ (total, along the profiled manifold) and
$b_s^{K}=\big(\partial\Gbar_s(K)/\partial T\big)f_\gamma$. The calibration at $K=0$ gives, by
the implicit function theorem,
\begin{equation}
dN_s=\frac{d\Ghat_s(0)-a_s^{0}d\ahat-b_s^{0}d\ghat}{D_s^{0}} .
\end{equation}
Substituting into $d\Gbar_s(K)=a_s^{K}d\ahat+b_s^{K}d\ghat+D_s^{K}dN_s$ and writing
$r_s^{K}=D_s^{K}/D_s^{0}$,
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
d\Gbar_s(K)=r_s^{K}\,d\Ghat_s(0)
+\big(a_s^{K}-r_s^{K}a_s^{0}\big)d\ahat
+\big(b_s^{K}-r_s^{K}b_s^{0}\big)d\ghat \;}
\label{eq:main}
\end{equation}
Each structural channel enters \emph{residualized against its $K=0$ direction}: only the part
of the $\alpha$ (resp.\ $\gamma$) response that $\Nhat_s$ cannot absorb survives. Then
$\mathrm{Var}=L\,\mathcal{J}\,L'$ with $L$ read off \eqref{eq:main} and $\mathcal{J}$ the joint
covariance already assembled for the $\Nhat_s$ delta method,
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{J}=
\begin{pmatrix}
\Sigma_{\Ghat\Ghat} & \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\Ghat)' & \Sigma_{\gamma\Ghat}'\\
\mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\Ghat) & \mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{sw}}(\ahat) & \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\ghat)\\
\Sigma_{\gamma\Ghat} & \mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\ghat)' & \Sigma_{\gamma\gamma}
\end{pmatrix},
\qquad
\mathrm{Cov}(\ahat,\Ghat)=P\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{data}}[:,G],
\end{equation}
PSD by construction, as for $V_T$. \textbf{The only empirical input is $\Ghat_s(0)$} --- the
one number the calibration consumed --- so the $K\ge1$ bands need no new bootstrap on the data
side.

\paragraph{Two limits worth checking.}
\begin{itemize}
\item $K=0$: $r_s^{0}=1$ and both residuals vanish, leaving
$\mathrm{Var}[\Gbar_s(0)]=\mathrm{Var}(\Ghat_s(0))=[\Sigma_{\mathrm{data}}]_{G_sG_s}$. The
$\alpha$ and target channels do not add, they \emph{cancel}: the fitted moment \emph{is} the
target, so the current bar --- the $\alpha$ channel alone,
$\sqrt{a_s^{0\prime}\mathrm{Var}_{\mathrm{sw}}(\ahat)a_s^{0}}$ --- double counts.
\item \textbf{Clamped sector}: $\Nhat_s$ sits at a bound, $dN_s=0$, and the identity flips to
$d\Gbar_s(K)=a_s^{K}d\ahat+b_s^{K}d\ghat$ for every $K$, target channel absent. So the band
is a per-sector switch keyed on \texttt{clamped} --- the same distinction the figure already
stars.
\end{itemize}

\section{The empirical band}

For a bar on the \emph{data} increments, $\Sigma_{\mathrm{data}}$ must be extended: its count
block is $S$ rows at $K=0$ and carries nothing for $K\ge1$. A bar there today would be
invented, which is why SEs are currently drawn on the $K=0$ panel only. That is a
data-pipeline change (bootstrap $\hat p_s(K)$ alongside $\Ghat_s(0)$), outside this repo, and
\texttt{reconcile\_sigma\_data} must learn the wider block.

A marginal variance per $(s,K)$ suffices for two bands read side by side. The cross-covariance
$\mathrm{Cov}(\hat p_s(K),\Ghat_s(0))$ is needed only for a band on the model$-$data
\emph{difference}; under the increment convention it is second-order, which is a further
reason to prefer increments over the CDF, where it is $\approx\mathrm{Var}(\Ghat_s(0))$ and
cannot be ignored.

\section{Implementation}

\begin{enumerate}
\item \texttt{model\_CP.jl}: \texttt{gbar\_sector\_K}, the exact-$K$ analogue of
\texttt{gbar\_sector}. Keep the unbiased convention --- with the $\Nhat_s$ varieties taken as
a sample without replacement from the $m$ simulated draws, the hypergeometric expectation
$\mathbb{E}\big[\binom{k}{j}\binom{m-k}{n-j}/\binom{m}{n}\big]$ is the Binomial pmf
\emph{exactly} for every $j$, so the estimator is unbiased at every $K$, not only at $K=0$.
\item $\Gbar_s(K)$ as a diagnostic block in \texttt{compute\_moments} (\emph{not} a moment),
so the Jacobian has rows for $a_s^{K}$ and $b_s^{K}$.
\item $D_s^{K}$ from \eqref{eq:D}, beside the existing \texttt{D\_firstdiff}.
\item \texttt{var\_G\_curve\_delta.npy} from the existing \texttt{Joint}; the
\texttt{clamped}-keyed switch in \texttt{plot\_count\_curve}.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{One consistency choice to make deliberately.}
The existing $\Nhat_s$ delta method uses the \emph{analytic} $\partial\Gbar_s(0)/\partial n
=\overline{(1-\qhat)^{n}\ln(1-\qhat)}$, while \eqref{eq:D} is the unit first difference. They
agree to $O(\qhat)$ (the gap is already reported), but $D_s^{0}$ appears in both $r_s^{K}$ and
$\mathrm{Var}(\Nhat_s)$. Using the first difference throughout is the coherent choice --- one
variety \emph{is} the step --- but it will shift the published $\mathrm{se}_N$ slightly, so
make the switch explicitly rather than by accident.
